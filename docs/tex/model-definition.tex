\section{Motivation}

The standard empirical treatment of \snia\ light curves, exemplified by \salt\
\citep{Guy2007}, models the spectral energy distribution as a linear expansion about a
mean sequence,
\begin{equation}
  F(p,\lambda) = x_{0}\left[M_{0}(p,\lambda) + x_{1}M_{1}(p,\lambda) + \dots\right]
                 \exp\!\left[\colr\,\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)\right],
\end{equation}
with per-supernova parameters $(x_{0}, x_{1}, \colr, t_{0})$: a description of a
\emph{surface} over phase and wavelength, from which the light curve in any band
follows by integration against a filter response. The parameter $x_{1}$ is read as a
``stretch'', but it does not act as a reparameterization of time --- it sets the
amplitude of a fixed additive component --- so shape and timing are entangled in one
number.

The geometric alternative starts elsewhere. A supernova observed in $n$ bands
supplies a point in $\Rn$ at each epoch, and over the event those points trace a
curve. The curve is independent of when it was sampled, and by the fundamental
theorem of curves it is determined, up to a rigid motion, by $n-1$ scalar invariants
of arclength. Shape then lives entirely in those invariants and timing entirely in
the arclength map, since a reparameterization of time changes how fast a supernova
moves along its curve but does not change the curve.

That separation is exact as a statement about curves, and is not thereby a guarantee about
fits: over a finite, noisy window a change in the traversal and a change in curvature can
mimic one another. Unlike the exact redundancies of Section~\ref{sec:pars}, which
are removed by convention, how far the two can be told apart is an empirical question.


We work in magnitude space, with the Euclidean metric
$\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$. Both are choices, argued at length in
\texttt{docs/model-note-backlog.md}: the space because distance and dust (to first order)
act there as translations, to which curvature is blind; the metric because none is
physically preferred, so one must be declared. The likelihood, by contrast, is evaluated
in native flux (Section~\ref{sec:samples}).

The primary configuration is \ztf\ $g$ and $r$ \citep{Bellm2019}, where $n=2$ and
$\curve = (\Magg, \Magr)$, so focus on $n=2$.

\section{The model}

\subsection{Light curves as traversal over curves in magnitude space}
\label{sec:curve}
The rest-frame multi-band light-curve template, as a function of phase $\phase$ measured
from $g$ maximum, is denoted as
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\phase)
    = \bigl(\Magg(\phase), \Magr(\phase)\bigr) \in \Rtwo.
  \label{eq:curvedef}
\end{equation}
$\curve$ is a parameterized curve in the $2$-dimensional magnitude space.
The relation between phase and arclength along the curve is $\arc(\phase)$, defined by
$\dd \arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd \phase}\,\dd \phase$ with the constant of integration
fixed by $\arc = 0$ at $g$ maximum. Wherever $\norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase} \neq 0$ this is
strictly increasing and invertible, and the curve reparameterized as $\curve(\arc)$ is
unit-speed, $\norm{\curve'(\arc)} = 1$.
The template is carried on a fixed arclength interval $\arc \in [\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$
with $\arc_{\min} < 0 < \arclen$, declared once for the model rather than fitted and wide
enough to cover the modelled phase range for every supernova. Every global function below
is a function on this interval, and it is $\arclen$ that appears in the gauge conditions
of Section~\ref{sec:pars}. What varies between supernovae is which phases these arclengths
map to, not the interval itself.
A unit-speed curve in $\Rtwo$ admits a Frenet frame,
 \begin{equation}
  \curve' = \tang, \qquad
  \tang' = \curv\,\norml.
  \label{eq:frenet2d}
\end{equation}
with $\tang$ the unit \emph{tangent} and $\norml$ the \emph{normal}, the counterclockwise
$90^{\circ}$ rotation of $\tang$; the \emph{curvature} $\curv$ is correspondingly signed.


We now map out the inverse: how a curve, specified by curvature and three initial conditions,
 and the traversal over that curve, specified by a function,
define light curves.


Given $\curv$, $\curve(\arc)$ is determined through the above differential equations and
initial conditions for the direction of $\tang$ and the position of $\curve$. Writing the
unit tangent by its angle $\psi$, the equations are solved with
\begin{equation}
  \tang(\arc) = \bigl(\cos\psi(\arc),\ \sin\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \norml(\arc) = \bigl(-\sin\psi(\arc),\ \cos\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \label{eq:tangangle}
\end{equation}
and $\psi' = \curv$. Curvature is thus the rate of turning of the tangent, and two
quadratures give $\psi$ and the curve,
\begin{align}
  \psi(\arc)   &= \psi_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}\curv(\zeta)\,\dd\zeta ,
                  \label{eq:turning}\\
  \curve(\arc) &= \curve_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}
                  \bigl(\cos\psi(\zeta),\ \sin\psi(\zeta)\bigr)\,\dd\zeta .
                  \label{eq:quadrature}
\end{align}
We choose $\curve_{0} = (0,0)$, so that at $\arc = 0$ the template $g$ magnitude and color
are zero ($\Magg(0) = 0$, $\Magr(0) = 0$). Both $\psi_{0} = \pm\pi/2$ make $\arc = 0$ a
critical point of $\Magg$, differing only in whether $r$ is brightening or fading there.
We take $\psi_{0} = -\pi/2$, for which $\Magr'(0) < 0$, since $r$ peaks after $g$.
 The extremum is peak brightness, a local minimum of $\Magg$, if
\begin{equation}
  \curv(0) > 0 ,
  \label{eq:peakcond}
\end{equation}
since $\Magg'' = -\sin\psi\,\curv$. Furthermore it is the only critical point on the
interval, hence the global minimum, if and only if
\begin{equation}
  0 < \Psi(\arc) < \pi \quad (\arc > 0), \qquad
  -\pi < \Psi(\arc) < 0 \quad (\arc < 0) ,
  \label{eq:turncond}
\end{equation}
where $\Psi(\arc) = \int_{0}^{\arc}\curv$ is the turning since maximum. The two are not
independent: \eqref{eq:turncond} forces $\curv(0) \geq 0$.

The connection to supernova phase is specified by the inverse of $s(p)$,  the function $p(s)$.

\subsection{Per-supernova light-curve model}
\label{sec:pars}

A supernova is described by six parameters,
$\allpars = (\peakmag,\, \colr,\, \xstretch,\, x_p,\, \shapekappa,\, \tmax)$, entering as
\begin{align}
  \begin{pmatrix} \magg \\ \magr \end{pmatrix}\!(\arc \mid \allpars)
    &= \curve\bigl(\arc;\, \shapekappa\bigr) \;+\; \peakmag\,\mathbf{1}
       \;+\; \colr\,\dustunit,
    \label{eq:modelmag}\\
  t(\arc \mid \allpars)
    &= \tmax + (1+\zred)\,\phase(\arc;\, \xstretch, x_p),
    \label{eq:modeltime}
\end{align}
where $\curve(\arc;\shapekappa)$ is generated from the curvature by the quadratures
\eqref{eq:turning}--\eqref{eq:quadrature}. This is the light curve in parametric form:
$\arc$ sweeps the template, \eqref{eq:modeltime} places each of its points in observer
time and \eqref{eq:modelmag} gives the magnitudes there. Evaluation at the observed
epochs is a monotone interpolation of \eqref{eq:modeltime}. Only $\shapekappa$ affects the curve shape. The others translate it, retime it, or set the
clock.
The redshift $\zred$ is known rather than
fitted, and $\phase$ is rest-frame throughout. Table~\ref{tab:pars} collects the
parameters.

The curvature is expanded as
\begin{equation}
  \curv(\arc; \shapekappa) = \curv_{0}(\arc) + \shapekappa\,\curv_{1}(\arc) ,
  \label{eq:curvlin}
\end{equation}
the leading coefficient pinned at unity. This costs no direction of shape variation, since
$\curv_{1} \propto \curv_{0}$ is admissible and gives an overall amplitude of turning; it
costs a second shape mode, which in two bands is what a second free coefficient would be.
The traversal is expanded through its rate,
\begin{equation}
  \deriv{\phase}{\arc}
    = \exp\bigl[\xstretch + q_{0}(\arc) + x_p\,q_{1}(\arc)\bigr] ,
  \qquad
  \phase(\arc) = \int_{0}^{\arc} \deriv{\phase}{\zeta}\,\dd\zeta ,
  \label{eq:travlog}
\end{equation}
so that $\phase(0) = 0$ and $\dd\phase/\dd\arc > 0$ hold identically, for every
$(\xstretch, x_p) \in \Rtwo$ and every basis. Here $\xstretch$ is a
\emph{log} stretch: at $x_p = 0$ the traversal is the classical dilation by
$e^{\xstretch}$.

Nothing constrains the coefficients. The turning condition \eqref{eq:turncond} is an
expectation the fit should meet, not a condition imposed on it: since
$\Psi(\arc; \shapekappa) = K_{0}(\arc) + \shapekappa\,K_{1}(\arc)$ with
$K_{i} = \int_{0}^{\arc}\curv_{i}$ is linear in $\shapekappa$, it is one linear inequality
per arclength and would be an interval in $\shapekappa$ were it imposed. It is not, because
a supernova clipped to that interval is reported as an extreme but valid fit, whereas one
left free reports $\max_{\arc}\abs{\Psi}$ and can be recognized. A supernova whose fit
violates \eqref{eq:turncond} has two $g$ extrema, so its $\peakmag$ and $\colr$ are not the
peak magnitude and color; $\max_{\arc}\abs{\Psi}/\pi$ is therefore a validity criterion for
the fit, to be reported per object and acted on like a bad $\chi^{2}$. The same holds for
\eqref{eq:peakcond}, whose failure is worse: $\curv(0) < 0$ makes $\arc = 0$ a minimum of
brightness, so the model has anchored to the wrong feature entirely. A prior on
$\shapekappa$ is a prior on turning:
the curve reaches the turning through \eqref{eq:quadrature}, so the displacement it induces
in magnitude space is a double integral of $\curv_{1}$ and grows with arclength.

\paragraph{Gauge.} Each expansion fixes only the span of its basis: a change of basis
undone by a compensating change of coefficients alters no prediction. The traversal has
four such freedoms, $q_{1} \mapsto \lambda q_{1} + \mu$ and
$q_{0} \mapsto q_{0} + \alpha + \nu q_{1}$, absorbed by $(\xstretch, x_p)$; the curvature has
two, $\curv_{1} \mapsto \lambda\curv_{1}$ and
$\curv_{0} \mapsto \curv_{0} + \nu\curv_{1}$, absorbed by $\shapekappa$. We remove them on
the functions rather than on the population. Writing
$\langle f, g\rangle = \int_{\arc_{\min}}^{\arclen} fg\,\dd\arc$ and $\bar f$ for the mean
of $f$ over that interval,
\begin{equation}
  \bar q_{1} = 0, \quad \langle q_{1},q_{1}\rangle = 1, \quad
  \bar q_{0} = 0, \quad \langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle = 0;
  \qquad
  \langle \curv_{1},\curv_{1}\rangle = 1, \quad
  \langle \curv_{0},\curv_{1}\rangle = 0 ,
  \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
four conditions for four freedoms and two for two. Each pair leaves a sign, fixed by
$\int_{0}^{\arclen} q_{1} > 0$ and $\int_{0}^{\arclen} \curv_{1} > 0$, so a positive
coefficient lengthens the late traversal and turns the track further.


\subsection{Modelling functions}
\label{sec:traversal}

Four global functions on $[\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$ carry the template. Together $\curv_{0}$
and $q_{0}$ are the reference \snia: the track through magnitude space, and the rate at
which a supernova walks along it. $\curv_{1}$ and $q_{1}$ are the first mode of departure
from it, split so the two halves cannot be confused --- $\curv_{1}$ moves where the track
goes, $q_{1}$ how fast the supernova covers it. All
four are expansions on a fixed basis, their coefficients fit jointly with the per-supernova
parameters.

Let $\{\phi_{k}\}$ be orthonormal on $[\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$ with $\phi_{0}$ constant, and
write
\begin{equation}
  q_{1} = \sum_{k \geq 1} a_{k}\phi_{k}, \quad
  q_{0} = \sum_{k \geq 1} b_{k}\phi_{k}, \quad
  \curv_{1} = \sum_{k \geq 0} c_{k}\phi_{k}, \quad
  \curv_{0} = \sum_{k \geq 0} d_{k}\phi_{k} ,
  \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
the traversal pair omitting $\phi_{0}$, which is what makes $\bar q_{0} = \bar q_{1} = 0$
in \eqref{eq:gauge} hold identically; the curvature pair carries no mean condition and
keeps it. The remaining conditions are then algebra:
$\langle q_{1}, q_{1}\rangle = \norm{a}^{2}$ and $\langle q_{0}, q_{1}\rangle = b \cdot a$
exactly, and likewise for $(c,d)$. Fitting unconstrained $\tilde a, \tilde b$ and setting
$a = \tilde a/\norm{\tilde a}$ and $b = \tilde b - (\tilde b \cdot a)\,a$ imposes
\eqref{eq:gauge} to machine precision; $q_{0}$ is orthogonalized but not normalized, its
norm being physical. The two sign conditions are linear inequalities on $a$ and $c$, fixed
by flipping the raw vector. \salt\ has the same freedom and fixes it the other way, by
normalizing $x_{1}$ over its training set; the difference is that a condition on functions
can be built into how the functions are written, while a condition on a population cannot.

A basis of $K$ functions leaves $4K - 2$ global parameters, and truncates the function
space: $K$ is a modelling choice and not only a parameterization, and raising it tests
whether the data want more freedom than it allows. The construction degenerates only when $a$ and $b$ become
parallel, which is not a numerical nuisance to suppress but the statement that the two
functions carry the same information.

The fit is therefore unconstrained: monotonicity is structural, the gauge is built into
\eqref{eq:basis}, and \eqref{eq:turncond} is checked rather than imposed. What it checks is
a statement about supernovae --- that there is one $g$ maximum and not two: maximum light
is where the tangent runs along the $r$ axis, $\Psi$ measures how far it has turned since,
and a turn of $\pi$ places a second $g$ peak inside the window. Violation for more than a
few objects is a verdict on the basis rather than a numerical nuisance, and
\eqref{eq:peakcond} is worth monitoring during the fit rather than only at the end, since
$\curv(0) < 0$ is a reachable basin from a poor start; initializing $\curv_{0}$ from a
template rather than at random keeps the fit out of it.

That the clock runs forward is not a condition here but a property of the form. Writing the
traversal through $\log\,\dd\phase/\dd\arc$ admits no coefficients for which phase runs
backwards, and an object whose phase ran backwards partway along its track would not be a
slow supernova but not a supernova.

What is \emph{not} fixed by convention is the point of the exercise. Nothing at all is
imposed on the per-supernova coefficients, so every moment of them is a measurement:
$\mathrm{Cov}(\xstretch, x_p)$ says whether the second timing mode carries anything stretch
does not, and the shape--timing correlations $\mathrm{Cov}(\shapekappa, \xstretch)$ and
$\mathrm{Cov}(\shapekappa, x_p)$ are what the model is built to measure. Fixing any of them
by convention would assume the answer.

The model as it stands carries no intrinsic scatter: every departure of a supernova from
the six-parameter family must be absorbed by photometric noise. This is a limitation
rather than a claim, and is the first thing to revisit once the six parameters are shown
to earn their cost.


\section{Sample}
\label{sec:samples}

Coverage was measured directly from the \ztf\ SN~Ia DR2 archive
\citep{Rigault2025}. Starting from the 3628 cataloged objects, we require
$\zred < 0.05$ and both the \texttt{lccoverage} and \texttt{fitquality} flags set,
leaving 652 supernovae. Fourteen of these have no light-curve file in the release; they
are exactly the fourteen whose \salt\ $t_{0}$ is NaN, so the absence is of photometry
rather than of shape, and 638 objects remain with light curves. Epochs are counted per
band inside the rest-frame window $[-15,+40]$~d with signal-to-noise above five and with
none of the quality bits excluded by \texttt{flag \& 31 == 0}, the archive default. The
signal-to-noise requirement defines the sample and nothing more: every epoch in the window
enters the likelihood, non-detections and negative fluxes included. The likelihood is
Gaussian in native flux, so a low-signal epoch weights itself down correctly, whereas
discarding one would select on the noise realization of the very quantity being fitted.

The analysis uses the 598 supernovae with at least five good epochs in \emph{each} of $g$
and $r$; the count is 635 at three or more and 464 at ten or more. The $i$ band is not
used: only 178 objects reach five good $i$ epochs, too few to support a third dimension.
Selection and counts are produced by \texttt{dgsn.data.sample}.

\paragraph{A caveat on the window used for counting.} Counting epochs inside
$[-15,+40]$~d requires a date of maximum, but $\tmax$ is precisely what the model fits
(Section~\ref{sec:pars}); the numbers above were obtained using the archive's
\salt\ $t_{0}$ to place the window. This is \salt\ entering upstream of
\emph{selection}, far weaker than \salt\ entering the fit --- no photometry is
transformed, initialized, or K-corrected by it, and the fitted $\tmax$ remains free and
independent. It is nonetheless an exception to the rule that \salt\ stays strictly
downstream, and is recorded here rather than left implicit. The check that settles it is
to recount using an SED-free anchor, for instance a window placed on the brightest good
$g$ epoch, and confirm that the two samples agree in \emph{membership}: equal counts can
hide different objects, so the symmetric difference is the quantity to report. The
fourteen selected objects with NaN $t_{0}$ need no such estimator, having no photometry
to place a window on.

\paragraph{Milky Way extinction.} Galactic reddening is tabulated per object as
\texttt{mwebv}, so its \emph{amplitude} is known and is removed deterministically
before fitting rather than being fitted. What remains free is $\colr$, which absorbs
host reddening and intrinsic color together and does not separate them. Note that what is
known is $E(B-V)$, not $A_{g}(\phase)$ and $A_{r}(\phase)$; the conversion is
phase-dependent and needs an SED, so removing a constant offset is an approximation
here as well. It is not uniformly mild: the sample has median \texttt{mwebv} of $0.043$,
but $24\%$ of it exceeds $0.1$, $12\%$ exceeds $0.2$ and the largest is $1.06$, with a
fifth of the objects at Galactic latitude $|b| < 20^{\circ}$. No latitude or reddening cut
is imposed, so the approximation is worst for precisely the objects where it matters most.
