\section{Motivation}

The standard empirical treatment of \snia\ light curves, exemplified by \salt\
\citep{Guy2007}, models the spectral energy distribution as a linear expansion about a
mean sequence,
\begin{equation}
  F(p,\lambda) = x_{0}\left[M_{0}(p,\lambda) + x_{1}M_{1}(p,\lambda) + \dots\right]
                 \exp\!\left[\colr\,\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)\right],
\end{equation}
with per-supernova parameters $(x_{0}, x_{1}, \colr, t_{0})$: a description of a
\emph{surface} over phase and wavelength, from which the light curve in any band
follows by integration against a filter response. The parameter $x_{1}$ is read as a
``stretch'', but it does not act as a reparameterization of time --- it sets the
amplitude of a fixed additive component --- so shape and timing are entangled in one
number.

The geometric alternative starts elsewhere. A supernova observed in $n$ bands
supplies a point in $\Rn$ at each epoch, and over the event those points trace a
curve. The curve is independent of when it was sampled, and by the fundamental
theorem of curves it is determined, up to a rigid motion, by $n-1$ scalar invariants
of arclength. Shape then lives entirely in those invariants and timing entirely in
the arclength map, since a reparameterization of time changes how fast a supernova
moves along its curve but does not change the curve.

That separation is exact as a statement about curves, and is not thereby a guarantee about
fits: over a finite, noisy window a change in the traversal and a change in curvature can
mimic one another. Unlike the exact redundancies of Section~\ref{sec:pars}, which
are removed by convention, how far the two can be told apart is an empirical question.


We work in magnitude space, with the Euclidean metric
$\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$. Both are choices, argued at length in
\texttt{docs/model-note-backlog.md}: the space because distance and dust (to first order)
act there as translations, to which curvature is blind; the metric because none is
physically preferred, so one must be declared. The likelihood, by contrast, is evaluated
in native flux (Section~\ref{sec:samples}).

The primary configuration is \ztf\ $g$ and $r$ \citep{Bellm2019}, where $n=2$ and
$\curve = (\Magg, \Magr)$, so focus on $n=2$.

\section{The model}

\subsection{Light curves as traversal over curves in magnitude space}
\label{sec:curve}
The rest-frame $g$ and $r$-band light curves, as a function of phase $\phase$ measured
from $g$ maximum, are denoted as
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\phase)
    = \bigl(\Magg(\phase), \Magr(\phase)\bigr) \in \Rtwo.
  \label{eq:curvedef}
\end{equation}
$\curve$ is a parameterized curve in the $2$-dimensional magnitude space.
The arclength along the curve, $\arc(\phase)$ (not to be confused with ``stretch''), is defined by
$\dd \arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd \phase}\,\dd \phase$ with the constant of integration
fixed by $\arc = 0$ at $g$ maximum; wherever $\norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase} \neq 0$ this is
strictly increasing and invertible.  The curve $\gamma(s)$ is
unit-speed, $\norm{\curve'(\arc)} = 1$.
The curve is defined on a fixed arclength interval $\arc \in [\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$
with $\arc_{\min} < 0 < \arcmax$, declared once for the model rather than fitted and wide
enough to cover the modelled phase range for every supernova. A unit-speed curve in $\Rtwo$ admits a Frenet frame,
 \begin{equation}
  \curve' = \tang, \qquad
  \tang' = \curv\,\norml.
  \label{eq:frenet2d}
\end{equation}
with $\tang$ the unit \emph{tangent} and $\norml$ the \emph{normal}, the counterclockwise
$90^{\circ}$ rotation of $\tang$; the \emph{curvature} $\curv$ is correspondingly signed.

We now map out the inverse: how a curve, specified by the angle of the unit tangent
$\psi$ and the position of one of its points, and the traversal over that curve,
define light curves.
Given $\psi(s)$, $\curve(\arc)$ is determined through the differential equations in
Eq.~\ref{eq:frenet2d} and an initial condition for the position of $\curve$. The equations are solved with
\begin{equation}
  \tang(\arc) = \bigl(\cos\psi(\arc),\ \sin\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \norml(\arc) = \bigl(-\sin\psi(\arc),\ \cos\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \label{eq:tangangle}
\end{equation}
The curvature is the rate of turning of the tangent, $\curv = \psi'$, so a curve is as
well described by its tangent angle as by its curvature. Given $\psi$, the curve follows by
one quadrature,
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\arc) = \curve_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}
                  \bigl(\cos\psi(\zeta),\ \sin\psi(\zeta)\bigr)\,\dd\zeta .
                  \label{eq:quadrature}
\end{equation}

For light-curve templates, we choose $\curve_{0} = (0,0)$, such that at
$\arc = 0$ both magnitudes, and hence also the color, are zero
($\Magg(0) = \Magr(0) = 0$). Both $\psi_{0} = \pm\pi/2$ make $\arc = 0$ a
critical point of $\Magg$, differing only in whether $r$ is brightening or fading there;
we take $\psi_{0} = -\pi/2$, for which $\Magr'(0) < 0$, since $r$ peaks after $g$.
The extremum is peak brightness, a local minimum of $\Magg$, if
\begin{equation}
  \curv(0) > 0 ,
  \label{eq:peakcond}
\end{equation}
since $\Magg'' = -\sin\psi\,\curv$. We impose no constraint based on it and rather
anticipate the condition will be satisfied by the fit output.

The connection between arclength and phase remains specified by $s(p)$.

\subsection{Per-supernova light-curve model}
\label{sec:pars}
Having presented our mathematical representation of light curves and our convention to pin
the template position, here we describe the
model for the range of expected light-curves from the family of Type~Ia supernova.
A supernova is described by six parameters,
$\allpars = (\peakmag,\, \colr,\, \xstretch,\, x_p,\, \shapeturn,\, \tmax)$, entering as
\begin{align}
  \phase(t \mid \tmax) &= \frac{t - \tmax}{1 + \zred} ,
    \label{eq:modeltime}\\
  \begin{pmatrix} \magg \\ \magr \end{pmatrix}\!(\phase \mid \allpars)
    &= \curve\bigl(\arc(\phase;\, \xstretch, x_p);\, \shapeturn\bigr)
       \;+\; \peakmag\,\mathbf{1} \;+\; \colr\,\dustunit .
    \label{eq:modelmag}
\end{align}
An epoch observed at $t$ is carried to
rest-frame phase by \eqref{eq:modeltime} where the redshift $\zred$ is known, which involves no modelling functions, then to
arclength by $\arc(\phase)$, and \eqref{eq:modelmag} gives the magnitudes there.
The
basis $\mathbf{1} = (1,1)$, $\dustunit = (0,-1)$ is used to specify the light curve placement in magnitude space.
The parameter pair
$(\peakmag, \colr)$
gives the $g$ magnitude and the $g-r$ color  at $\arc = p = 0$.

The parameterized curves are given by $\curve(\arc;\shapeturn)$, which in turn are
specified by the turning.  It is the tangent angle that is expanded,
\begin{equation}
  \psi(\arc; \shapeturn) = -\frac{\pi}{2} + \turnzero(\arc)
                            + \shapeturn\,\turnone(\arc) ,
  \qquad \turnzero(0) = \turnone(0) = 0.
  \label{eq:curvlin}
\end{equation}
The traversal $\arc(\phase;\, \xstretch, x_p)$ is expanded through its rate, as a function
of phase,
\begin{equation}
  \deriv{\arc}{\phase}
    = \exp\bigl[\xstretch + q_{0}(\phase) + x_p\,q_{1}(\phase)\bigr] ,
  \qquad
  \arc(\phase) = \int_{0}^{\phase} \deriv{\arc}{\zeta}\,\dd\zeta ,
  \label{eq:travlog}
\end{equation}
so that $\arc(0) = 0$ and $\dd\arc/\dd\phase > 0$ hold identically, for every
$(\xstretch, x_p) \in \Rtwo$ and every basis. Here $\xstretch$ is a \emph{log} speed: at
$x_p = 0$ the supernova covers $e^{\xstretch}$ times the reference arclength per rest-frame
day, which is the classical dilation of the time axis by $e^{-\xstretch}$.

Table~\ref{tab:pars} summarizes the parameters.
Only $\shapeturn$ affects the curve shape. The others translate it, retime it, or set the
clock.

The models for $\psi$ and $\arc$ have gauge freedom. Each fixes the family of functions the
supernovae range over but not the coordinates placed on that family, and a change of those
coordinates undone by a compensating change of parameters alters no prediction.
The traversal has
four such freedoms, $q_{1} \mapsto \lambda q_{1} + \mu$ and
$q_{0} \mapsto q_{0} + \alpha + \nu q_{1}$, absorbed by $(\xstretch, x_p)$; the turning has
two, $\turnone \mapsto \lambda\turnone$ and
$\turnzero \mapsto \turnzero + \nu\turnone$, absorbed by $\shapeturn$. The turning admits
no additive constant among these, $\turnzero(0) = \turnone(0) = 0$ having already pinned
it. We specify the gauge as follows:
Writing $\langle f,g\rangle$ for $\int fg\,\dd\phase$ over
the phase window when the arguments are $q_{0}, q_{1}$ and for $\int fg\,\dd u$ in the
coordinate $u$ of \eqref{eq:umap} when they are $\turnzero, \turnone$, and $\bar f$ for the
corresponding mean,
\begin{equation}
  \bar q_{1} = 0, \quad \langle q_{1},q_{1}\rangle = 1, \quad
  \bar q_{0} = 0, \quad \langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle = 0;
  \qquad
  \langle \turnone,\turnone\rangle = 1, \quad
  \langle \turnzero,\turnone\rangle = 0 ,
  \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
four conditions for four freedoms and two for two. Each pair leaves a sign, fixed by
$\int_{0}^{\phase_{\max}} q_{1} > 0$ and $\turnone(\arcmax) > 0$, so a positive
coefficient speeds the late traversal and turns the track further --- the second literally,
$\turnone(\arcmax)$ being the turning the departure mode accumulates over the interval.


\subsection{Modeling functions}
\label{sec:traversal}

In this section, we go into detail into how the functions that describe the turning and transversal
are modeled.
Four global functions carry the template, on two domains. Together $\turnzero$ and $q_{0}$
describe an ``average''  \snia: the track through magnitude space, and the rate at which a
supernova walks along it. $\turnone$ and $q_{1}$ are the first mode of departure from it ---
$\turnone$ perturbs where the track goes,
$q_{1}$ how fast the supernova covers it.

These functions are modeled as truncated Legendre series,
$f(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} c_{k}P_{k}(x)$, retaining terms through degree $K-1$, with the
$P_{k}$ shifted to the interval of their argument and normalized there. Normalizing makes
them orthonormal under the flat measure of that variable, which is the measure
\eqref{eq:gauge} uses, so the gauge conditions become algebra on the coefficients. The
constant mode is absent from both series, though for different reasons, and the two pairs
differ in their argument and in their interval. We take them in turn.

To understand the expected behavior of the modeling functions
and estimate reasonable values for $K$ and the arclength interval, we compute them from \salt\
across its $x_{1}$ range, and ask what it takes to reproduce a track to $\sim 0.02$~mag.

\paragraph{The traversal.} $q_{0}$ and $q_{1}$ are functions of rest-frame phase on
$[\phase_{\min}, \phase_{\max}] = [-15, +40]$~d, the window the sample is selected on in
Section~\ref{sec:samples} and the same for every supernova by construction. The constant mode is omitted because $\xstretch$
already carries it, so that $\bar q_{0} = \bar q_{1} = 0$ holds identically. Writing
\begin{equation}
  q_{1} = \sum_{k \geq 1} a_{k}P_{k}(\phase), \qquad
  q_{0} = \sum_{k \geq 1} b_{k}P_{k}(\phase),
  \label{eq:basisq}
\end{equation}
the conditions of \eqref{eq:gauge} on this pair are $\norm{a}^{2} = 1$ and $b \cdot a = 0$,
since $\langle q_{1}, q_{1}\rangle = \norm{a}^{2}$ and
$\langle q_{0}, q_{1}\rangle = b \cdot a$; fitting unconstrained $\tilde a, \tilde b$ and
setting $a = \tilde a/\norm{\tilde a}$ and $b = \tilde b - (\tilde b \cdot a)\,a$ imposes
them to machine precision. Only $a$ is normalized: $q_{0}$ is orthogonalized against
$q_{1}$ but keeps its own norm, which is physical. Against \salt, $K_{q}$ between $16$ and $32$ suffices over the
three quarters of the sample with $x_{1} \gtrsim -1$, and considerably more in the
fast-decliner tail, where the speed approaches zero and its logarithm diverges.

\paragraph{The turning.} $\turnzero$ and $\turnone$ are functions of arclength, on an
interval $[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$ that we want to be large enough to span the data, and
which is measured from the sample in Section~\ref{sec:samples}. Here a change of coordinate is
wanted. The two maxima are close in time, about a day apart in $g$ and $r$, so the track
doubles back on itself and the turn is confined to a very short stretch of arclength: at
the median $x_{1}$, $|\phase| < 3$~d carries under two percent of the arclength and over
half of the $5.3$ radians through which $\psi$ turns. A polynomial in $\arc$ spreads its resolution evenly and
cannot meet that compression, so the turning is expanded in
\begin{equation}
  u = \asinh(\arc/\delta), \qquad \delta = 3\times10^{-4},
  \label{eq:umap}
\end{equation}
smooth and strictly increasing, linear for $|\arc| \ll \delta$ and logarithmic beyond, so
that resolution spread evenly in $u$ is resolution in $\arc$ concentrated where the track
turns. Too small a $\delta$ costs little and too large a $\delta$ fails, so it is set from
the sharpest track rather than the average one. The constant mode is absent here because
the basis is built to vanish at $\arc = 0$, which is what makes
$\turnzero(0) = \turnone(0) = 0$ of \eqref{eq:curvlin} hold identically rather than by
constraint: the $\tilde P_{k}$ are the Legendre polynomials in $u$, each shifted by its
value at $\arc = 0$ and the set re-orthonormalized afterwards, the shift having cost
orthonormality by a rank-one term. Writing
\begin{equation}
  \turnone = \sum_{k \geq 1} c_{k}\tilde P_{k}(u), \qquad
  \turnzero = \sum_{k \geq 1} d_{k}\tilde P_{k}(u),
  \label{eq:basispsi}
\end{equation}
the conditions of \eqref{eq:gauge} on this pair are $\norm{c}^{2} = 1$ and $d \cdot c = 0$,
imposed as above. Against \salt, $K_{\psi} = 24$ reproduces the track across the whole
range $-2 \leq x_{1} \leq 1.7$, from $0.007$~mag at the sample median to $0.022$ at the
ends.

Each pair is left with a sign:
the likelihood is invariant under $(q_{1}, x_p) \mapsto (-q_{1}, -x_p)$ and under the same
change in $(\turnone, \shapeturn)$, so the sign condition is a relabeling of the draws
after the fit.

Truncating the turning at $K_{\psi}$ and the traversal at $K_{q}$ gives
$2K_{\psi} + 2K_{q} - 4$ coefficients, neither pair carrying a constant, of which the four
conditions above leave $2K_{\psi} + 2K_{q} - 8$ free. Truncation bounds the function space,
so the two orders are modeling choices and not only a parameterization, and raising one
tests whether the data want more freedom than it allows. The construction degenerates only
when $a$ and $b$ become parallel, or $c$ and $d$, which is not a numerical nuisance to
suppress but the statement that the pair's two functions carry the same information.

\section{Data Sample}
\label{sec:samples}

Supernova light curves are taken from  the \ztf\ SN~Ia DR2 archive
\citep{Rigault2025}. Starting from the \Ncatalogued\ cataloged objects, we require
$\zred < 0.05$, both the \texttt{lccoverage} and \texttt{fitquality} flags equal to one,
and the archive's own cosmology classification \texttt{sn\_type == snia-cosmo}, leaving
\Nselected\ supernovae. That last cut is what restricts the sample to normal \sneia: it admits
\texttt{norm}, \texttt{91t} and \texttt{99aa} and no peculiar subtype, while
\texttt{snia-pec} holds the 91bg, Iax, 03fg, 02es, 00cx and 18byg objects whose light-curve
shapes a single $\shapeturn$ could not span alongside normals. Excluding
\texttt{snia-pec} instead would not do: one 91bg-like object is typed \texttt{snia}, its
peculiarity recorded only in \texttt{sub\_type}. Of the \Nselected, \Nnolc\ have no light-curve
file in the release; they are exactly those whose \salt\ $t_{0}$ is NaN, so the
absence is of photometry rather than of shape, and \Nphot\ objects remain with light curves. Epochs are counted per
band inside the rest-frame window $[-15,+40]$~d with signal-to-noise above five and with
none of the quality bits excluded by \texttt{flag \& 31 == 0}, the archive default. That
mask's first bit marks $\sigma_{f} = 0$, which six epochs of this sample carry; each also
fails on \texttt{chi2dof} and limiting magnitude, so the mask removes them on their own
merits and the likelihood never meets a zero error. 
The analysis uses the \Nanalysis\ supernovae with at least five good epochs in \emph{each} of $g$
and $r$; the count is \Nthree\ at three or more and \Nten\ at ten or more.
Selection and counts are produced by \texttt{dgsn.data.sample}.

Observed-frame $g$ and $r$ are identified with their rest-frame counterparts. A two-band
model carries no spectral energy distribution and so cannot K-correct itself: only the time
axis is de-redshifted, in \eqref{eq:modeltime}. This is the reason for the $\zred < 0.05$
cut, which bounds the band shift rather than removing it. What remains is a systematic,
smooth in $\zred$ and hence partly absorbed by $\peakmag$ and $\colr$; it is quantified
after the fit against an external SED, never corrected for within it.

Counting epochs inside $[-15,+40]$~d requires a date of
maximum, and the numbers above use the archive's \salt\ $t_{0}$ to place the window.
For the fit,
$\tmax$ is compared with $t_{0}$ and the analysis repeated with one substituted for the
other. For the selection, the sample is
recounted against an SED-free anchor --- a window on the brightest good $g$ epoch --- and
the two samples compared by \emph{membership}: equal counts can hide different objects, so
the symmetric difference is what to report. The \Nnolc\ selected objects with NaN $t_{0}$
need neither, having no photometry to place a window on.

The arclength interval of Section~\ref{sec:curve} is declared from the sample rather than
assumed. Smoothing each well-observed light curve in flux and integrating
$\dd\arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase}\,\dd\phase$ outward from the $g$ maximum gives, over
the $[-15,+40]$~d window, a median span of $[-4.9, +3.9]$ mag with fifth and ninety-fifth
percentiles at $-8.9$ and $+7.4$. The measurement is biased high, since noise lengthens a
path, and moves by $0.7$ mag across a factor of ten in smoothing; the \citet{Hsiao2007}
template, having no noise, gives $[-3.2, +3.3]$. We declare
$[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax] = [-8, +8]$, wide enough that every supernova's window maps inside
it, and verify after fitting that it does --- the fitted traversal gives
$\arc(\phase_{\min})$ and $\arc(\phase_{\max})$ per object, and both must fall inside
$[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$. Width is not free, the basis spending resolution where no
supernova reaches, so that check is also how the interval is tightened.

Supernova light curves are not corrected for Galactic dust.
To first order, such corrections would appear in the form
\begin{equation}
  m_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{corr}} = m_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{obs}} - A_{\lambda},
  \qquad A_{\lambda} = R_{\lambda}\,E(B-V) ,
  \label{eq:mwext}
\end{equation}
with $R_{\lambda}$ from \citet{Schlafly2011} assuming a \citet{Fitzpatrick1999} law at
$R_{V} = 3.1$, and $E(B-V)$ the \citet{Schlegel1998} value at the supernova's coordinates,
tabulated per object as \texttt{mwebv}. A constant per band is a translation in magnitude
space, and $\mathbf{1}$ and $\dustunit$ span $\Rtwo$, so it is absorbed exactly by the
placement: $\peakmag \mapsto \peakmag + A_{g}$ and
$\colr \mapsto \colr + (A_{g} - A_{r})$, leaving the curve and hence $\curv$ untouched.
Not correcting therefore costs nothing in the fit, and means instead that $\peakmag$ and
$\colr$ carry the Galactic term, to be removed downstream where \texttt{mwebv} is known
per object. The absorption is exact only for constant $A_{\lambda}$: the band-averaged
extinction depends on a source spectrum that evolves, so $R_{\lambda}$ drifts across the
window by a few percent, and that residual does reach $\curv$ --- not negligibly, since
$E(B-V)$ exceeds $0.1$ for $23\%$ of the sample and reaches $1.06$.

\section{Likelihood}
\label{sec:likelihood}

The model is built in magnitude space and confronted with the data in flux. Magnitude space
is where distance and reddening act as translations, to which $\psi$ is insensitive; flux is
where the \ztf\ errors are Gaussian and where a non-detection is an ordinary measurement
near zero rather than a missing one.

For an epoch $i$ observed at $t_{i}$ in band $b_{i}$, \eqref{eq:modeltime} gives the
rest-frame phase $\phase_{i}$ and \eqref{eq:travlog} the arclength
$\arc_{i} = \arc(\phase_{i})$.
The predicted flux is
\begin{equation}
  \hat f_{i}(\allpars)
    = 10^{-0.4\,[\,m_{b_{i}}(\phase_{i} \mid \allpars)\, -\, \zp\,]} ,
  \label{eq:predflux}
\end{equation}
with $\zp = 30$, the archive's zero point. It is pinned, not fitted: $\zp$ and $\peakmag$
are exactly degenerate in \eqref{eq:modelmag} and \eqref{eq:predflux}, and this choice is
what makes $\peakmag$ the $g$ magnitude on the archive's scale.
The set of data is fixed, even if they fall out of the [-15,+40]~d window for the
sampled $\tmax$. It is the set of Section~\ref{sec:samples} without the signal-to-noise
cut used there for counting: every epoch contributes, non-detections and negative fluxes
included,
\begin{equation}
  -2\ln\mathcal{L}
    = \sum_{i}\left[\frac{\bigl(f_{i} - \hat f_{i}\bigr)^{2}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}
      + \ln\sigma_{i}^{2}\right], \qquad
  \sigma_{i}^{2} = \sigma_{f,i}^{2}
    + \bigl(0.4\ln 10\ \sigma_{b_{i}}\,\hat f_{i}\bigr)^{2} ,
  \label{eq:like}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{f,i}$ the archive's flux error, which already carries \texttt{err\_scale};
\texttt{flux} already has \texttt{flux\_offset} subtracted. 

The second variance term is the intrinsic scatter, carried by two fitted scalars
$\sigma_{g}$ and $\sigma_{r}$: one per band, constant in phase, and in magnitudes, which
is why they enter \eqref{eq:like} through $0.4\ln 10$ as a fractional flux error scaled by
$\hat f_{i}$. Here $\sigma_{b_{i}}$ is whichever belongs to the band of epoch $i$. The
inclusion of these model parameters necessitates the log-determinant term in the
likelihood.

Two intervals bound where the modeling functions of Section~\ref{sec:traversal} are
defined, and the posterior respects neither: $\tmax$ can carry an epoch's phase outside
$[\phase_{\min}, \phase_{\max}]$, and $\xstretch$ can carry $\arc(\phase_{\max})$ past
$\arcmax$. Both are chosen to cover most of the posterior mass, but excursions occur, and
dropping an epoch that makes one would leave the objective discontinuous. Each bound is
therefore softened rather than enforced. Taking the phase window first, whose bounds are
fixed and the same for every supernova: with
$w_{i} = [\phase_{i} - \phase_{\min}]/[\phase_{\max} - \phase_{\min}]$,
$\mathrm{sp}(x) = \epsilon\log(1 + e^{x/\epsilon})$ and $\epsilon = 0.01$, one percent of
that range, the phase is soft-clamped and the flux tapered,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\phase_{i} = \phase_{\min} + (\phase_{\max} - \phase_{\min})
    \bigl[\mathrm{sp}(w_{i}) - \mathrm{sp}(w_{i}-1)\bigr] ,
  \qquad
  \hat f_{i} \;\longmapsto\; \hat f_{i}\,
    \exp\!\bigl[-\bigl(\mathrm{sp}_{t}(-w_{i}) + \mathrm{sp}_{t}(w_{i}-1)\bigr)^{2}
      /\epsilon_{t}^{2}\bigr] ,
  \label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{sp}_{t}$ the same softplus at width $\epsilon_{t}/10$ and
$\epsilon_{t} = 0.02$, one fiftieth of the range. Both are $C^{\infty}$ in $\allpars$ and
send the predicted flux to zero outside. The taper wants a turn-off scale distinct from its
softplus width, which is why $\epsilon_{t}$ appears: built on one scale, its argument at
the window edge is $O(\epsilon)$ against a denominator $\epsilon^{2}$, and the flux there
is suppressed by $38$ percent --- at $\phase = -15$ and $+40$~d, where real epochs sit. As
written the suppression at the edge is half a percent, indistinguishable from unity a day
inside, and the flux is down by $e^{-1}$ at $\epsilon_{t}$ outside the window and gone at
$2\epsilon_{t}$. The clamp needs no second scale and agrees with the model to
$O(\epsilon)$ for $w_{i} \in [0,1]$. An epoch carried out of
range then contributes $f_{i}^{2}/\sigma_{i}^{2}$: free if it was consistent with zero,
expensive if it was a detection, which is the pushback wanted. The arclength needs the same
treatment for the same reason --- $\arcmax$ bounds $\psi$, and $\xstretch$ can carry
$\arc(\phase_{\max})$ past it --- and takes the same softplus, in $\arc$ on
$[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$.
The model should be well-conditioned outside of this range to enable convergence of the fitter.


\section{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

The posterior is sampled with the no-U-turn Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampler
\citep{Hoffman2011} in \texttt{cmdstan} \citep{Carpenter2017}. Global coefficients, per-supernova parameters and $\sigma_{g}, \sigma_{r}$
are sampled jointly: $2K_{\psi} + 2K_{q} - 4$ coefficients, six parameters for each
training supernova, and two scalars. Nothing is profiled or held at a point estimate. The per-supernova parameters
are incidental and grow in number with the sample, so a fit that maximized over them would
return $\sigma_{g}, \sigma_{r}$ biased low \citep{Neyman1948}; marginalizing over them does
not.



The gauge is imposed by construction rather than sampled. The sampler moves on the
unconstrained raw coefficients $(\tilde a, \tilde b, \tilde c, \tilde d)$ of
Section~\ref{sec:traversal}, whose normalization and orthogonalization are deterministic
transforms of them, so \eqref{eq:gauge} holds at every iteration and no constraint enters
the likelihood. The two sign conventions are not among these transforms, being
discontinuous; they are applied per draw in \texttt{generated quantities}, where they
cannot reach the gradient.
The raw coefficients are given normal priors whose width decays with the mode index,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde a_{k},\, \tilde b_{k},\, \tilde c_{k},\, \tilde d_{k}
    \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\; \tau^{2} k^{-2\alpha}\right) ,
  \qquad \alpha > \tfrac{1}{2} ,
  \label{eq:coefprior}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha$ and $\tau$ hyperparameters, sampled with the rest under a half-normal on
$\tau$ and a prior on $\alpha$ supported above $\tfrac{1}{2}$; the smoothness of the global
functions is thus inferred and not asserted. The coefficients are written non-centered,
$\tilde a_{k} = \tau k^{-\alpha}z_{k}$ with $z_{k}$ standard normal, since a scale sampled
jointly with the coefficients it multiplies is the geometry Hamiltonian Monte Carlo handles
worst.

A common width would make the prior a function of the
truncation: the gauge leaves $a$ uniform on the sphere, which is equal expected power in
every mode and so a function as rough as the basis allows and rougher with each mode added,
while $q_{0}$ and $\turnzero$, normalized by nothing, would carry
$\langle q_{0}, q_{0}\rangle \sim K_{q}$. Raising an order would then coarsen and inflate
the functions as well as freeing them, and the comparison across orders below would be
reading that rather than flexibility. A decaying width states instead that the functions are
smooth, with $\alpha$ setting how smooth and $\tau$ the overall scale, and leaves the
truncation to do only its own work. The remaining priors are standard normal on
$(\xstretch, x_p, \shapeturn)$ --- the gauge fixes the scale of the latent parameters,
which is what makes a unit normal a statement rather than an arbitrary width ---
half-normal on $\sigma_{g}$ and $\sigma_{r}$, and normal on $\tmax$ centered on the
archive's $t_{0}$ with a width of one day. $\peakmag$ and $\colr$ are given flat priors,
being placement rather than shape.

Chains are started from the archive's \salt\ fit: $\tmax$ at $t_{0}$, $\peakmag$ and
$\colr$ at the magnitude and color it implies, shape parameters at zero, and the global
coefficients at a least-squares fit to the mean template. Initialization is not use of
\salt\ as a prior --- it sets where the chains begin and not what they converge to, and the
run is only accepted if chains from dispersed starts agree. The number of chains, and the
warmup and sampling iterations, are Stan's defaults. Convergence is judged from the
usual statistics --- $\hat R$, effective sample size, divergent transitions --- read on the
gauge-fixed functions and on the predicted fluxes rather than on the raw coefficients, four
of whose directions the gauge leaves unidentified and on which those statistics carry no
information about the fit.

\subsection{Evaluating the model}
\label{sec:numerics}

Two quadratures are wanted, not three: $\psi$ is the expansion itself, a matrix product
against \eqref{eq:basispsi}, and is never integrated for. Neither of the remaining two is
elementary --- $\arc(\phase)$ is $\exp$ of an expansion and $\curve$ is $\cos$ of one --- so \eqref{eq:frenet2d} is still
solved by quadratures of known integrands rather than as an initial-value problem, evaluated
on shared nodes rather than by stepping, and the gauge \eqref{eq:gauge} does not touch
them. Where \eqref{eq:gauge} itself is
evaluated on nodes, those nodes must carry its measure: orthonormalizing sampled columns
imposes $L^{2}$ of the sampling density, so the nodes are uniform in $u$ for the turning
pair and in $\phase$ for the traversal pair, or else weighted. Nodes uniform in $\arc$ give
$L^{2}(\dd\arc)$, which is not the gauge.

The same node sets serve the quadratures, shared across supernovae and fixed for the run,
and $u$ and $\phase$ are also the coordinates the integrands are smooth in. It is $\curve$
that sets the requirement, its integrand $\cos\psi$ oscillating where the track turns.
Because the integrands are
analytic in the working coordinate, a spectral rule is worth its extra structure over a
composite one: integrating an exact expansion and comparing the recovered magnitudes, so
that quadrature alone is measured, gives $\sim10^{-4}$~mag at a hundred nodes, where the
trapezoid rule converges as the square of the spacing and is still at $10^{-1}$ with four
times as many.

Cost follows from where the per-supernova parameters enter. The turning is linear in
$\shapeturn$, so $\psi$ for every supernova is one matrix product and a scalar
combination; but $\curve$ is $\cos$ of $\psi$, and that integral is taken for each. In
\eqref{eq:travlog} $\xstretch$ factors out as $e^{\xstretch}$ and only $x_p$ survives inside,
leaving one more integral per supernova. Two sweeps of the training curves over the shared
nodes, then, at every evaluation of the log-density.

The sample is split into $442$ supernovae for training and $110$ for validation, drawn
$22$ at a time from each fifth of the $x_{1}$ distribution. Stratifying on $x_{1}$ rather
than drawing at random is deliberate: it is along $x_{1}$ that the difficulty of this model
is known to vary, the traversal wanting $K_{q}$ near $70$ at $x_{1} = -2$ against $16$ to
$32$ elsewhere, and a random draw would leave the number of fast decliners in validation to
chance --- some nine of the $45$ in the sample, but a poor draw could give three. There is
no third split: nothing here is tuned finely enough on validation to need one.

What validation withholds is the global functions, which is what the truncation orders
control. The six per-supernova parameters are not shared and cannot be withheld --- a
supernova of which nothing has been seen cannot be predicted --- so a validation object is
fit for its own six with $\turnzero, \turnone, q_{0}, q_{1}, \sigma_{g}, \sigma_{r}$ held
at their training values. Those six are fit on the same epochs whose residuals are then
scored, which makes the exercise mildly optimistic: six parameters against a median of $44$
good epochs. Fitting them on alternate epochs and scoring on the rest removes the optimism
at no computational cost, and is worth doing if the validation residuals come out better
than the training ones.

The truncation orders are set by running the fit over
$K_{\psi} \in \{12, 24, 48\}$ and $K_{q} \in \{8, 16, 32\}$ and comparing the normalized
residual $(f_{i} - \hat f_{i})/\sigma_{i}$ over validation epochs, whose spread is unity
for a model whose scatter and truncation are both right. Residual within the training
sample falls with either order by construction and so ranks nothing. \eqref{eq:coefprior}
is what keeps the comparison about truncation rather than prior: a larger basis is free to
leave its high modes near zero instead of obliged to fill them, and with $\alpha$ inferred
the fit sets how fast they decay, so an order past what the data support costs shrunken
modes rather than a worse fit. Orders the validation residual cannot separate are orders
the data cannot separate, and the smallest of those is taken. The residual is reported by
$x_{1}$ stratum as well as pooled, since it is the fast decliners that are expected to fail
first.

The brackets straddle what Section~\ref{sec:traversal} found a \salt\ template to ask
for. They reach above it because $K_{\psi} = 48$ buys another factor of two in template
accuracy, and below it because a template carries no noise --- \ztf\ epochs at
signal-to-noise of order ten cannot resolve structure at $0.02$~mag --- so the orders the
data support should fall under the ones a template asks for; $K_{\psi} = 12$ is there to
find that floor, reaching only $0.07$ to $0.19$~mag against the template.

That the turning is uniform across $x_{1}$ where the traversal is not is the asymmetry
worth expecting in the fits. The fast decliners are the traversal's difficulty alone: near
$x_{1} = -2$, some $8$ percent of the sample, $K_{q}$ near $70$ would be wanted to reach
$0.02$~mag against a template. Those objects are expected to fit less well than the rest,
and the residuals should be reported by $x_{1}$ rather than pooled.
