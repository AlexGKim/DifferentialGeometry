\section{Motivation}

The standard empirical treatment of \snia\ light curves, exemplified by \salt\
\citep{Guy2007}, models the spectral energy distribution as a linear expansion about a
mean sequence,
\begin{equation}
  F(p,\lambda) = x_{0}\left[M_{0}(p,\lambda) + x_{1}M_{1}(p,\lambda) + \dots\right]
                 \exp\!\left[\colr\,\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)\right],
\end{equation}
with per-supernova parameters $(x_{0}, x_{1}, \colr, t_{0})$: a description of a
\emph{surface} over phase and wavelength, from which the light curve in any band
follows by integration against a filter response. The parameter $x_{1}$ is read as a
``stretch'', but it does not act as a reparameterisation of time --- it sets the
amplitude of a fixed additive component --- so shape and timing are entangled in one
number.

The geometric alternative starts elsewhere. A supernova observed in $n$ bands
supplies a point in $\Rn$ at each epoch, and over the event those points trace a
curve. The curve is independent of when it was sampled, and by the fundamental
theorem of curves it is determined, up to a rigid motion, by $n-1$ scalar invariants
of arclength. Shape then lives entirely in those invariants and timing entirely in
the arclength map, since a reparameterisation of time changes how fast a supernova
moves along its curve but does not change the curve.

That separation is exact as a statement about curves, and is not thereby a guarantee about
fits: over a finite, noisy window a change in the traversal and a change in curvature can
mimic one another. Unlike the exact redundancies of Section~\ref{sec:degeneracies}, which
are removed by convention, how far the two can be told apart is an empirical question.


We work in magnitude space, with the Euclidean metric
$\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$. Both are choices, argued in Section~\ref{sec:magspace}: the
space because distance and dust (to first order) act there as translations, to which curvature
is blind; the metric because none is physically preferred, so one must be declared.
The likelihood, by contrast, is evaluated in native flux
(Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}).

The primary configuration is \ztf\ $g$ and $r$ \citep{Bellm2019}, where $n=2$ and
$\curve = (\Magg, \Magr)$, so focus on $n=2$.

\section{The model}

\subsection{Light curves as traversal over curves in magnitude space}
\label{sec:curve}
The rest-frame multi-band light-curve template, as a function of phase $\phase$ measured
from $g$ maximum, is denoted as
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\phase)
    = \bigl(\Magg(\phase), \Magr(\phase)\bigr) \in \Rtwo.
  \label{eq:curvedef}
\end{equation}
$\curve$ is a parameterized curve in the $2$-dimensional magnitude space.
The relation between phase and arclength along the curve is $\arc(\phase)$, defined by
$\dd \arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd \phase}\,\dd \phase$ with the constant of integration
fixed by $\arc = 0$ at $g$ maximum. Wherever $\norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase} \neq 0$ this is
strictly increasing and invertible, and the curve reparameterised as $\curve(\arc)$ is
unit-speed, $\norm{\curve'(\arc)} = 1$.\footnote{The condition fails only where every band is
simultaneously stationary, which is a non-issue as the epoch of light curve maximum is
band-dependent.}
A unit-speed curve in $\Rtwo$ admits a Frenet frame,
 \begin{equation}
  \curve' = \tang, \qquad
  \tang' = \curv\,\norml.
  \label{eq:frenet2d}
\end{equation}
with $\tang$ the unit \emph{tangent} and $\norml$ the principal \emph{normal}, defined as
the $90^{\circ}$ rotation of $\tang$ rather than by $\curve''$. The \emph{curvature}
$\curv$ is therefore \emph{signed}, and the frame stays defined wherever $\curv = 0$.


We now map out the inverse: how a curve, specified by curvature and two boundary conditions,
 and the traversal over that curve, specified by a function,
define light curves.


Given $\curv$, $\curve(\arc)$ is determined through the above differential equations and
initial conditions for the direction of $\tang$ and the position of $\curve$. Writing the
unit tangent by its angle $\psi$, the equations are solved with
\begin{equation}
  \tang(\arc) = \bigl(\cos\psi(\arc),\ \sin\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \norml(\arc) = \bigl(-\sin\psi(\arc),\ \cos\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \label{eq:tangangle}
\end{equation}
and $\psi' = \curv$. Curvature is thus the rate of turning of the tangent, and two
quadratures give $\psi$ and the curve,
\begin{align}
  \psi(\arc)   &= \psi_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}\curv(\zeta)\,\dd\zeta ,
                  \label{eq:turning}\\
  \curve(\arc) &= \curve_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}
                  \bigl(\cos\psi(\zeta),\ \sin\psi(\zeta)\bigr)\,\dd\zeta .
                  \label{eq:quadrature}
\end{align}
We choose $\curve_{0} = (0,0)$ and $\psi_{0} = -\pi/2$, which at $\arc = 0$ gives
$\Magg' = 0$ and a decreasing $\Magr$. The choice of $\psi_{0}$ is born of our knowledge
that $g$-band light curves have only one critical point (which corresponds to peak
brightness) and that the $r$-band light curve is brightening at the $g$-band peak.
Setting the template $g$ magnitude and colour to zero at maximum makes $\dm$ the peak
$g$ magnitude and $\colr$ the colour at peak, following \salt's practice of
normalising at maximum --- there in $B$, here in $g$.

The connection to supernova phase is specified by the inverse of $s(p)$,  the function $p(s)$.

\subsection{Parameterization of Light Curves}
\label{sec:pars}

We construct a parameterized light curve model that accommodates supernova heterogeneity.
Parameters considered in traditional models are
\begin{itemize}
\item $\dm$: A wavelength-independent flux normalization, for example due to
  distance.  This is a term $\dm\,\mathbf{1}$ added to $\curve$.
\item $\colr$: A phase-independent shift in color.  This is a term
  $\colr\,\dustunit$ added to $\curve$, with $\dustunit = (0,-1)$, so that $\colr$ is the
  shift in $\Magg - \Magr$.
\item $\xstretch$: A wavelength-independent dilation in the light curve, which
  corresponds to `stretch'.  This is a term $\phase \rightarrow \xstretch\,\phase$.
\end{itemize}

The above follows the  SALT2 procedure of decoupling light curve shape from dust,
whose focus is on supernova standardization.
An alternative and equally valid parameterization retains $\colr$ but replaces the fixed
$\dustunit$ with a vector $\mathbf{R}$ drawn from an extinction law, shifting light curves
by $(\colr\,R_{g}, \colr\,R_{r})$, notation that asserts correspondence with dust. This is
the approach of \citet{Mandel2022}, whose focus is on cosmology: the shape of $\mathbf{R}$
is not adopted but fitted globally, through a host dust law with
$R_{V} = 2.9 \pm 0.2$, alongside per-supernova dust amplitudes and intrinsic components.

The benefit of our light-curve shape model is apparent: $\dm\,\mathbf{1}$ and
$\colr\,\dustunit$ are translations and $\xstretch$ is a reparameterisation, so the
curvature is independent of all three.

To go beyond traditional light-curve models, we explore two new per-supernova parameters,
\begin{itemize}
\item $x_p$: Variation in time evolution beyond that described by $s$, with
$p(\arc; \xstretch, x_p) = \xstretch\bigl(p_0(\arc) + x_p\,p_1(\arc)\bigr)$, where $p_0$
and $p_1$ are functions obeying $p_{0}(0) = p_{1}(0) = 0$, so that $\phase = 0$ at $g$
maximum for every supernova. The fiducial traversal is $x_p = 0$, $\xstretch = 1$.
\item $\shapekappa$: First-order deviations in curvature, with
$\curv(s; \shapekappa) = \curv_{0}(s) + \shapekappa\,\curv_{1}(s)$, where  $\curv_0(s)$ and $\curv_1(s)$ are functions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Degeneracies}
\label{sec:degeneracies}

\emph{Placeholder.} Directions along which the likelihood is \emph{exactly} constant. Along
each, a change in one quantity is undone by a compensating change in others, so no data can
determine it and none may be fitted: each is removed by declaring a convention. Those still
open are flat directions in the model as specified above.

\paragraph{Open.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Basis of the curvature expansion.} For any $a$ and $\lambda \neq 0$,
  \begin{equation*}
    \bigl(\curv_{0},\ \curv_{1},\ \shapekappa\bigr) \longmapsto
    \bigl(\curv_{0} + a\,\curv_{1},\ \lambda\,\curv_{1},\ (\shapekappa - a)/\lambda\bigr)
  \end{equation*}
  leaves $\curv$ unchanged for every supernova. Fixed by normalising the population,
  $\langle \shapekappa \rangle = 0$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\shapekappa) = 1$, with a sign
  convention to remove $\lambda < 0$.
\item \textbf{Basis of the traversal.} Here the redundancy is larger, because $\xstretch$
  makes \emph{both} coefficients free: $\xstretch(p_{0} + x_p p_{1}) = a\,p_{0} + b\,p_{1}$
  with $a = \xstretch$ and $b = \xstretch\,x_p$. The model therefore depends only on
  $\mathrm{span}\{p_{0}, p_{1}\}$, not on the basis, and a change of basis in
  $\mathrm{GL}(2,\Real)$ is absorbed. Declaring the fiducial traversal to be $x_p = 0$,
  $\xstretch = 1$ removes $p_{0} \mapsto p_{0} + a\,p_{1}$ and $p_{0} \mapsto \nu p_{0}$.
  Two directions survive it: $p_{1} \mapsto \lambda p_{1}$, absorbed by
  $x_p \mapsto x_p/\lambda$ and fixed by $\mathrm{Var}(x_p) = 1$; and
  $p_{1} \mapsto p_{1} + \rho\,p_{0}$, absorbed exactly by
  $\xstretch \mapsto \xstretch(1 + \rho x_p)$ and $x_p \mapsto x_p/(1 + \rho x_p)$, which
  is removed only by requiring $p_{1}$ to carry no component along $p_{0}$. In the singular
  limit $p_{1} \propto p_{0}$ the two parameters coincide outright.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Closed.} Recorded because the redundancy is what makes each convention free,
and because a convention mistaken for a measurement is the error these entries exist to
prevent.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reddening direction against placement.} Since $\mathbf{1}$ and $\dustunit$
  span $\Rtwo$, replacing $\dustunit$ by $\alpha\mathbf{1} + \beta\dustunit$ is undone
  exactly by $(\dm, \colr) \mapsto (\dm + \alpha\colr,\ \beta\colr)$. Any direction not
  parallel to $\mathbf{1}$ therefore gives the same model, and $\dustunit = (0,-1)$ is
  declared rather than fitted.
\item \textbf{Arclength origin against frame orientation.} Integrating $\curv(\cdot + a)$
  from $\psi_{0} + \int_{0}^{a}\curv$ reproduces the same curve, so origin and orientation
  carry one joint redundancy. Fixing $\psi_{0} = -\pi/2$ removes it: a shift now requires
  $\int_{0}^{a}\curv = 0$, so generically $a = 0$. The anchor is self-enforcing, since
  $\psi(0) = -\pi/2$ forces $\Magg'(0) = 0$.
\item \textbf{Phase origin against the traversal.} A per-supernova shift in $\phase$ would
  be absorbed by the traversal were $p(0)$ free, leaving any fitted epoch of maximum
  undetermined. The condition $p_{0}(0) = p_{1}(0) = 0$ removes it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Global functions}
\label{sec:traversal}

The global functions of the model --- the curvature basis and the traversal --- are
represented by neural networks,
\begin{align}
  \bigl(\curv_{0}(\arc),\, \curv_{1}(\arc)\bigr) & = \NN_{\curv}\bigl(\arc \bigr),
    \label{eq:curvnet}\\
  \phase(\arc) & = \NN_{\phase}\bigl(\arc \bigr). \label{eq:arcnet}
\end{align}
The curvature of an individual supernova is built from $\curv_{0}$ and $\curv_{1}$ with a
per-supernova coefficient, introduced in Section~\ref{sec:pars}. The traversal is written
$\phase(\arc)$, which is the direction the forward model evaluates: the template is
generated on a grid in arclength, and $\phase(\arc)$ places each of its points in time.
No inversion is required. The traversal must be monotone, $\dd\phase/\dd\arc > 0$, for
the reparameterisation of Section~\ref{sec:curve} to exist.

\section{Samples}
\label{sec:samples}

Coverage was measured directly from the \ztf\ SN~Ia DR2 archive
\citep{Rigault2025}. Starting from the 3628 catalogued objects, we require
$\zred < 0.05$ and both the \texttt{lccoverage} and \texttt{fitquality} flags, leaving
669 supernovae, of which 654 have a light-curve file present in the release. Epochs are
counted inside the rest-frame window $[-15,+40]$~d with signal-to-noise above five and
with none of the quality bits $\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ set.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Minimum epochs per band & $g$ & $r$ & $i$ & $g\,\&\,r$ & $g\,\&\,r\,\&\,i$ \\
\midrule
$\geq 3$  & 636 & 639 & 216 & 636 & 215 \\
$\geq 5$  & 606 & 631 & 179 & 599 & 177 \\
$\geq 10$ & 490 & 537 &  82 & 465 &  80 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two samples follow. The \textbf{primary sample} is the 599 supernovae with at least
five good $g$ and $r$ epochs; here $n=2$ and there is a single invariant. The
\textbf{torsion subsample} is the 177 that additionally have at least five good
$i$-band epochs; here $n=3$ and $\tors(\arc)$ exists as a genuine second invariant.
The latter is small, but it is the only configuration in which the original motivation
for a second shape parameter --- that torsion describes the evolution of inter-band
magnitude differences --- can be tested, so it is a distinct analysis rather than an
afterthought. Torsion is undefined wherever the curvature vanishes
(Section~\ref{sec:curve}), so it is identified only where the track turns.

\paragraph{A caveat on the window used for counting.} Counting epochs inside
$[-15,+40]$~d requires a date of maximum, but $\tmax$ is precisely what the model fits
(Section~\ref{sec:traversal}); the numbers above were obtained using the archive's
\salt\ $t_{0}$ to place the window. This is \salt\ entering upstream of
\emph{selection}, far weaker than \salt\ entering the fit --- no photometry is
transformed, initialised, or K-corrected by it, and the fitted $\tmax$ remains free and
independent. It is nonetheless an exception to the rule that \salt\ stays strictly
downstream, and is recorded here rather than left implicit. The check that settles it is
to recount using an SED-free anchor, for instance a window placed on the brightest good
$g$ epoch, and confirm the sample sizes move negligibly. The 36 objects with NaN
$t_{0}$ must in any case be selected by such an estimator or excluded explicitly.

\paragraph{Milky Way extinction.} Galactic reddening is tabulated per object as
\texttt{mwebv}, so its \emph{amplitude} is known and is removed deterministically
before fitting rather than being fitted. Only host-galaxy reddening remains a free
quantity, and only in rung L2c. Note that what is known is $E(B-V)$, not
$A_{g}(\phase)$ and $A_{r}(\phase)$; by Section~\ref{sec:phasedust} the conversion is
phase-dependent and needs an SED, so removing a constant offset is an approximation
here as well --- milder, since Galactic columns at the high latitudes of this sample
are low, but the same approximation and not exact.
