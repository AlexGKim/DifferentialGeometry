\section{Motivation}

The standard empirical treatment of \snia\ light curves, exemplified by \salt\
\citep{Guy2007}, models the spectral energy distribution as a linear expansion about a
mean sequence,
\begin{equation}
  F(p,\lambda) = x_{0}\left[M_{0}(p,\lambda) + x_{1}M_{1}(p,\lambda) + \dots\right]
  \quad\text{(\salt's own $M_{0}, M_{1}$, not the template magnitudes below)}
                 \exp\!\left[\colr\,\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)\right],
\end{equation}
with per-supernova parameters $(x_{0}, x_{1}, \colr, t_{0})$: a description of a
\emph{surface} over phase and wavelength, from which the light curve in any band
follows by integration against a filter response. The parameter $x_{1}$ is read as a
``stretch'', but it does not act as a reparameterization of time --- it sets the
amplitude of a fixed additive component --- so shape and timing are entangled in one
number.

The geometric alternative starts elsewhere. A supernova observed in $n$ bands
supplies a point in $\Rn$ at each epoch, and over the event those points trace a
curve. The curve is independent of when it was sampled, and by the fundamental
theorem of curves it is determined, up to a rigid motion, by $n-1$ scalar invariants
of arclength. Shape then lives entirely in those invariants and timing entirely in
the arclength map, since a reparameterization of time changes how fast a supernova
moves along its curve but does not change the curve.

That separation is exact as a statement about curves, and is not thereby a guarantee about
fits: over a finite, noisy window a change in the traversal and a change in curvature can
mimic one another. Unlike the exact redundancies of Section~\ref{sec:pars}, which
are removed by convention, how far the two can be told apart is an empirical question.


We work in magnitude space, with the Euclidean metric
$\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$. Both are choices, argued at length in
\texttt{docs/model-note-backlog.md}: the space because distance and dust (to first order)
act there as translations, to which curvature is blind; the metric because none is
physically preferred, so one must be declared. The likelihood, by contrast, is evaluated
in native flux (Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}).

The primary configuration is \ztf\ $g$ and $r$ \citep{Bellm2019}, where $n=2$ and
$\curve = (\Magg, \Magr)$, so focus on $n=2$.

\section{The model}

\subsection{Light curves as traversal over curves in magnitude space}
\label{sec:curve}
The rest-frame $g$ and $r$-band light curves, as a function of phase $\phase$ measured
from $g$ maximum, are denoted as
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\phase)
    = \bigl(\Magg(\phase), \Magr(\phase)\bigr) \in \Rtwo.
  \label{eq:curvedef}
\end{equation}
$\curve$ is a parameterized curve in the $2$-dimensional magnitude space; the same
symbol denotes the curve as a function of phase here and of arclength below, the
parameterization being read from the argument.
The arclength along the curve, $\arc(\phase)$ (not to be confused with ``stretch''), is defined by
$\dd \arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd \phase}\,\dd \phase$ with the constant of integration
fixed by $\arc = 0$ at $g$ maximum; wherever $\norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase} \neq 0$ this is
strictly increasing and invertible.  The curve $\gamma(s)$ is
unit-speed, $\norm{\curve'(\arc)} = 1$.
The curve is defined on a fixed arclength interval $\arc \in [\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$
with $\arc_{\min} < 0 < \arcmax$, declared once for the model rather than fitted and wide
enough to cover the modelled phase range for every supernova. A unit-speed curve in $\Rtwo$ admits a Frenet frame,
 \begin{equation}
  \curve' = \tang, \qquad
  \tang' = \curv\,\norml.
  \label{eq:frenet2d}
\end{equation}
with $\tang$ the unit \emph{tangent} and $\norml$ the \emph{normal}, the counterclockwise
$90^{\circ}$ rotation of $\tang$; the \emph{curvature} $\curv$ is correspondingly signed.

We now map out the inverse: how a curve, specified by the angle of the unit tangent
$\psi$ and the position of one of its points, and the traversal over that curve,
define light curves.
Given $\psi(s)$, $\curve(\arc)$ is determined through the differential equations in
Eq.~\ref{eq:frenet2d} and an initial condition for the position of $\curve$. The equations are solved with
\begin{equation}
  \tang(\arc) = \bigl(\cos\psi(\arc),\ \sin\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \norml(\arc) = \bigl(-\sin\psi(\arc),\ \cos\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \label{eq:tangangle}
\end{equation}
The curvature is the rate of turning of the tangent, $\curv = \psi'$, so a curve is as
well described by its tangent angle as by its curvature. Given $\psi$, the curve follows by
one quadrature,
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\arc) = \curve_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}
                  \bigl(\cos\psi(\zeta),\ \sin\psi(\zeta)\bigr)\,\dd\zeta .
                  \label{eq:quadrature}
\end{equation}

For light-curve templates, we choose $\curve_{0} = (0,0)$, such that at
$\arc = 0$ both magnitudes, and hence also the color, are zero
($\Magg(0) = \Magr(0) = 0$). Both $\psi_{0} = \pm\pi/2$ make $\arc = 0$ a
critical point of $\Magg$, differing only in whether $r$ is brightening or fading there;
we take $\psi_{0} = -\pi/2$, for which $\Magr'(0) < 0$, since $r$ peaks after $g$.
The extremum is peak brightness, a local minimum of $\Magg$, if
\begin{equation}
  \curv(0) > 0 ,
  \label{eq:peakcond}
\end{equation}
since $\Magg'' = -\sin\psi\,\curv$. We impose no constraint based on it and rather
anticipate the condition will be satisfied by the fit output.

The connection between arclength and phase remains specified by $s(p)$.

\subsection{Per-supernova light-curve model}
\label{sec:pars}
Having presented our mathematical representation of light curves and our convention to pin
the template position, here we describe the
model for the range of expected light-curves from the family of Type~Ia supernova.
A supernova is described by six parameters,
$\allpars = (\peakmag,\, \colr,\, \xstretch,\, x_p,\, \shapeturn,\, \tmax)$, entering as
\begin{align}
  \phase(t \mid \tmax) &= \frac{t - \tmax}{1 + \zred} ,
    \label{eq:modeltime}\\
  \begin{pmatrix} \magg \\ \magr \end{pmatrix}\!(\phase \mid \allpars)
    &= \curve\bigl(\arc(\phase;\, \xstretch, x_p);\, \shapeturn\bigr)
       \;+\; \peakmag\,\mathbf{1} \;+\; \colr\,\dustunit .
    \label{eq:modelmag}
\end{align}
An epoch observed at $t$ is carried to
rest-frame phase by \eqref{eq:modeltime} where the redshift $\zred$ is known, which involves no modelling functions, then to
arclength by $\arc(\phase)$, and \eqref{eq:modelmag} gives the magnitudes there.
The
basis $\mathbf{1} = (1,1)$, $\dustunit = (0,-1)$ is used to specify the light curve placement in magnitude space.
The parameter pair
$(\peakmag, \colr)$
gives the $g$ magnitude and the $g-r$ color  at $\arc = p = 0$.

The parameterized curves are given by $\curve(\arc;\shapeturn)$, which in turn are
specified by the turning.  It is the tangent angle that is expanded,
\begin{equation}
  \psi(\arc; \shapeturn) = -\frac{\pi}{2} + \turnzero(\arc)
                            + \shapeturn\,\turnone(\arc) .
  \label{eq:curvlin}
\end{equation}
The traversal $\arc(\phase;\, \xstretch, x_p)$ is expanded through its rate, as a function
of phase,
\begin{equation}
  \deriv{\arc}{\phase}
    = \exp\bigl[\xstretch + q_{0}(\phase) + x_p\,q_{1}(\phase)\bigr] ,
  \qquad
  \arc(\phase) = \int_{0}^{\phase} \deriv{\arc}{\zeta}\,\dd\zeta ,
  \label{eq:travlog}
\end{equation}
so that $\arc(0) = 0$ and $\dd\arc/\dd\phase > 0$ hold identically, for every
$(\xstretch, x_p) \in \Rtwo$ and every basis. Here $\xstretch$ is a \emph{log} speed, the
speed measured in magnitudes per rest-frame day: at
$x_p = 0$ the supernova covers $e^{\xstretch}$ times the reference arclength per rest-frame
day, which is the classical dilation of the time axis by $e^{-\xstretch}$.

Table~\ref{tab:pars} summarizes the parameters.
Only $\shapeturn$ affects the curve shape. The others translate it, retime it, or set the
clock.

The models for $\psi$ and $\arc$ have gauge freedom. Each fixes the family of functions the
supernovae range over but not the coordinates placed on that family, and a change of those
coordinates undone by a compensating change of parameters alters no prediction.
The traversal has
four such freedoms, $q_{1} \mapsto \lambda q_{1} + \mu$ and
$q_{0} \mapsto q_{0} + \beta + \nu q_{1}$, absorbed by $(\xstretch, x_p)$; the turning has
two, $\turnone \mapsto \lambda\turnone$ and
$\turnzero \mapsto \turnzero + \nu\turnone$, absorbed by $\shapeturn$.

The turning has two and not four because it carries one per-supernova amplitude where the
traversal carries two. An additive constant in $\turnone$ puts $\shapeturn\mu$ into $\psi$,
a rotation of that supernova's track in the magnitude plane, and a constant in $\turnzero$
rotates every track; $m_{\mathrm{peak}}$ and $\colr$ span the translations of that plane
and nothing else, so neither is absorbable and neither is a freedom. Until
2026-11-23 this note carried $\turnzero(0) = \turnone(0) = 0$ inside
\eqref{eq:curvlin} and attributed the missing constants to them. That was wrong twice
over: the conditions fix nothing, since the transformations they would fix are not
symmetries, and they are therefore restrictions on the family rather than bookkeeping.
$\turnone(0) = 0$ in particular asserts that the $g$ maximum sits at the same arclength
origin for every supernova whatever its departure amplitude. They are lifted here, and
both constants are fitted; $\turnzero(0) = \turnone(0) = 0$ is the nested model and is
testable. A per-object anchor rotation is the best-identified direction in the per-object
sector, three times better determined than $m_{\mathrm{peak}}$ and $\colr$ and an order of
magnitude better than $\Delta$, so the capacity the restriction declined is capacity the
data can use.

We specify the gauge as follows:
Writing $\langle f,g\rangle$ for $\int fg\,\dd v$ in the
coordinate $v$ of \eqref{eq:vmap} when the arguments are $q_{0}, q_{1}$ and for
$\int fg\,\dd\arc$ over the gauge span $[\arc_{\mathrm{g}-}, \arc_{\mathrm{g}+}] = [-6, +5]$
when they are $\turnzero, \turnone$ ---
arclength rather than the expansion coordinate $u$ of \eqref{eq:umap}, since $u$ spends a
third of its range inside $|\arc| < 0.01$~mag, where a mode of unit norm in $u$ can carry
most of its power and the curve, an integral $\dd\arc$, cannot see it; and the span rather
than the interval $[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$ of Section~\ref{sec:samples} for the same reason,
the interval reaching past where supernovae go. The span is where they go: the first
hierarchical fits place $\arc(-15\,\mathrm{d})$ in $[-6.3, -2.3]$ and $\arc(+40\,\mathrm{d})$
in $[3.0, 6.2]$ across thirty objects, nine in ten of the latter below $4.2$. Normed over the
whole interval, $\turnone$ put half its norm outside the arclengths reached, and $\mu_{\psi}$
and every $\shapeturn$ grew to compensate --- a direction the gauge was meant to have
removed --- the gauge conditions are
\begin{equation}
  q_{1}(0) = 0, \quad \langle q_{1},q_{1}\rangle = 1, \quad
  q_{0}(0) = 0, \quad \langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle = 0;
  \qquad
  \langle \turnone,\turnone\rangle = 1, \quad
  \langle \turnzero,\turnone\rangle = 0 ,
  \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
four conditions for four freedoms and two for two. Both pairs are anchored at the origin,
so the traversal reads as the turning already did, and the per-supernova constant in
$\ln \dd\arc/\dd p$ is that rate at $p = 0$ exactly rather than its mean over $v$. The
traversal inner products are taken on the anchored modes $P_{k} - P_{k}(0)$, which carry the
Gram matrix $G = I + (v_{\max} - v_{\min})\,\mathbf{P}(0)^{\top}\mathbf{P}(0)$ rather than
the identity, the cross terms vanishing because every retained $P_{k}$ is orthogonal to the
constant. Anchoring changes no prediction --- per supernova the reachable family is
$q_{0} + \mathrm{span}\{1, q_{1}\}$ under either choice --- but \eqref{eq:coefprior} is read
in the anchored basis, and a coefficient prior is basis dependent, so the posterior is not
the mean-zero gauge's. Each pair leaves a sign, fixed by
$\int_{0}^{v_{\max}} q_{1}\,\dd v > 0$ and $\turnone(\arc_{\mathrm{g}+}) > 0$, so a positive
coefficient speeds the late traversal and turns the track further --- the second literally,
$\turnone(\arc_{\mathrm{g}+})$ being the turning the departure mode accumulates over the span.


\subsection{Modeling functions}
\label{sec:traversal}

In this section, we go into detail into how the functions that describe the turning and transversal
are modeled.
Four global functions carry the template, on two domains. Together $\turnzero$ and $q_{0}$
describe an ``average''  \snia: the track through magnitude space, and the rate at which a
supernova walks along it. $\turnone$ and $q_{1}$ are the first mode of departure from it ---
$\turnone$ perturbs where the track goes,
$q_{1}$ how fast the supernova covers it.

These functions are modeled as truncated Legendre series,
$f(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K-1} c_{k}P_{k}(x)$, retaining terms through degree $K-1$, with the
$P_{k}$ shifted to the interval of their argument and normalized to unit integral of their
square over it. Normalizing makes
them orthonormal under the flat measure of that variable, which is the measure
\eqref{eq:gauge} uses, so the gauge conditions become algebra on the coefficients, and it
gives a unit amplitude of a normalized mode a root-mean-square of $L^{-1/2}$ in the
expansion variable, $L$ the length of its interval. The
constant mode is absent from both series, though for different reasons, and the two pairs
differ in their argument and in their interval. Both arguments are compressions of the
physical variable about the anchor, for the same reason: the two maxima are close in time,
about a day apart in $g$ and $r$, so at the anchor the track doubles back on itself and
the supernova slows to walk it, and neither the turn nor the slowing is resolved by a basis
spending its resolution evenly. We take them in turn.

To understand the expected behavior of the modeling functions
and estimate reasonable values for $K$ and the arclength interval, we compute them from \salt\
across its $x_{1}$ range, and ask what it takes to reproduce a track to $\sim 0.02$~mag.
Those computations were made by a script that is not in the repository; every number quoted
from them below is to be regenerated by code under \texttt{src/dgsn} before it is relied on.

\paragraph{The traversal.} $q_{0}$ and $q_{1}$ are functions of rest-frame phase on the
modelling window $[\phase_{\min}, \phase_{\max}] = [-20, +45]$~d, the same for every
supernova by construction. It contains the window $[-15, +40]$~d the sample is selected on
in Section~\ref{sec:samples} with a margin of five days at each end, and the margin is what
keeps the softening of Section~\ref{sec:likelihood} away from any epoch the data window
admits. At $\phase = 0$ the $g$ magnitude is stationary by construction, so the speed there
is $|\dd\Magr/\dd\phase|$ at $g$ maximum, the $r$ curvature times the separation of the two
maxima, against a speed an order of magnitude larger a week away: the log rate carries a dip
at the anchor whose depth grows and whose width shrinks as the maxima approach, without
bound in the limit that they coincide, which the model therefore does not reach. It is the
traversal's face of the hairpin the turning meets in arclength, and it is met the same way,
\begin{equation}
  v = \asinh(\phase/\delta_{p}), \qquad \delta_{p} = 1~\mathrm{d},
  \label{eq:vmap}
\end{equation}
with $\delta_{p}$ set, like $\delta$ below, from the sharpest dip rather than the typical
one. The constant mode is omitted because $\xstretch$
already carries it, and each retained mode is taken relative to its value at the anchor, so
that $q_{0}(0) = q_{1}(0) = 0$ holds identically. Writing
\begin{equation}
  q_{1} = \sum_{k \geq 1} a_{k}\bigl[P_{k}(v) - P_{k}(0)\bigr], \qquad
  q_{0} = \sum_{k \geq 1} b_{k}\bigl[P_{k}(v) - P_{k}(0)\bigr],
  \label{eq:basisq}
\end{equation}
the conditions of \eqref{eq:gauge} on this pair are $\norm{\mathbf{a}}^{2} = 1$ and
$\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a} = 0$, since $\langle q_{1}, q_{1}\rangle = \norm{\mathbf{a}}^{2}$
and $\langle q_{0}, q_{1}\rangle = \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a}$; fitting unconstrained
$\tilde{\mathbf{a}}, \tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ and setting
$\mathbf{a} = \tilde{\mathbf{a}}/\norm{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}$ and
$\mathbf{b} = \tilde{\mathbf{b}} - (\tilde{\mathbf{b}} \cdot \mathbf{a})\,\mathbf{a}$ imposes
them to machine precision. Only $a$ is normalized: $q_{0}$ is orthogonalized against
$q_{1}$ but keeps its own norm, which is physical. Against \salt\ in $v$
(\texttt{scripts/salt\_orders.py}, the track's light curve fit in magnitude space at
each pair of orders), $K_{q} = 16$ suffices at every $x_{1} \geq -1$ and $K_{q} = 32$
gains no more than $0.001$~mag: the light curve is reproduced to $0.007$~mag at
$x_{1} = 0$ and $-1$ and to $0.014$ and $0.019$ at $+1$ and $+1.7$, the remainder set by
$K_{\psi}$, not $K_{q}$. Expanded in $\phase$ the same fit had wanted $K_{q}$ near $70$
at $x_{1} = -2$; in $v$ the dependence on $x_{1}$ is gone, and what fails at $x_{1} = -2$
is the turning (below).

\paragraph{The turning.} $\turnzero$ and $\turnone$ are functions of arclength, on an
interval $[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$ that we want to be large enough to span the data, and
which is measured from the sample in Section~\ref{sec:samples}. The turn at the anchor is
confined to a very short stretch of arclength: at
the median $x_{1}$, $|\phase| < 3$~d carries under two percent of the arclength and over
half of the $5.3$ radians through which $\psi$ turns. A polynomial in $\arc$ spreads its resolution evenly and
cannot meet that compression, so the turning is expanded in
\begin{equation}
  u = \asinh(\arc/\delta), \qquad \delta = 3\times10^{-4}~\mathrm{mag},
  \label{eq:umap}
\end{equation}
smooth and strictly increasing, linear for $|\arc| \ll \delta$ and logarithmic beyond, so
that resolution spread evenly in $u$ is resolution in $\arc$ concentrated where the track
turns. Too small a $\delta$ costs little and too large a $\delta$ fails, so it is set from
the sharpest track rather than the average one. The $\tilde P_{k}$ are built to vanish at
$\arc = 0$, and the constant mode is carried beside them rather than within them: the
$\tilde P_{k}$ are the Legendre polynomials in $u$, each shifted by its
value at $\arc = 0$ and the set re-orthonormalized afterwards. The shift costs
orthonormality by a rank-one term, $\langle P_{j} - P_{j}(0), P_{k} - P_{k}(0)\rangle
= \mathbb{I}_{jk} + (u_{\max} - u_{\min})P_{j}(0)P_{k}(0)$ with $\mathbb{I}$ the identity,
and the re-orthonormalization is
Gram--Schmidt in ascending degree. The order is not a detail. Gram--Schmidt in that order
is triangular, so $\tilde P_{k}$ is a combination of degrees no higher than $k$ and mode
$k$ still means degree at most $k$; a symmetric orthonormalization would mix every degree
into every mode, and the prior of \eqref{eq:coefprior}, whose width decays with the mode
index, would then shrink combinations with no particular smoothness. The Gram matrix is a
rank-one update of the identity, so its Cholesky factor is available in closed form and the
transformation is computed once for the run; at $K_{\psi} = 24$ its condition number is
$15$. Writing
\begin{equation}
  \turnone = c_{0} + \sum_{k \geq 1} c_{k}\tilde P_{k}(u), \qquad
  \turnzero = d_{0} + \sum_{k \geq 1} d_{k}\tilde P_{k}(u),
  \label{eq:basispsi}
\end{equation}
so that $\turnone(0) = c_{0}$ and $\turnzero(0) = d_{0}$ are fitted and the old model is
$c_{0} = d_{0} = 0$. Through \eqref{eq:curvlin} they enter only as the per-supernova
rotation $d_{0} + \shapeturn c_{0}$ of the tangent angle, and since
$\curve(\arc) = \int_{0}^{\arc}(\cos\psi, \sin\psi)\,\dd\arc'$ with $\curve(0) = 0$, that
is exactly a rotation $R(d_{0} + \shapeturn c_{0})$ of the track about the anchor, with no
translation remainder. The anchor is then a reference point on the track and not
necessarily the $g$ maximum, which it is only at $d_{0} + \shapeturn c_{0} = 0$.

The conditions of \eqref{eq:gauge} on this pair are
$\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf T}G_{\arc}\mathbf{c} = 1$ and
$\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf T}G_{\arc}\mathbf{c} = 0$ over the extended coefficient vectors
$\mathbf{c} = (c_{0}, c_{1}, \ldots)$ and $\mathbf{d} = (d_{0}, d_{1}, \ldots)$, with
$(G_{\arc})_{jk} = \int \tilde P_{j}\tilde P_{k}\,\dd\arc = \int \tilde P_{j}\tilde P_{k}\,\delta\cosh u\,\dd u$
the Gram matrix under arclength over the gauge span, $\tilde P_{0} \equiv 1$, computed once
for the run; imposed as above with $G_{\arc}$ in place of the identity. Both conditions are
needed on the extended pair and neither may be traded for a point condition at the anchor:
$\turnzero(0) = 0$ fixes $\nu$ only while $c_{0} \neq 0$ and so degenerates at the nested
model, and $\turnone'(0) = 1$ is unusable because $\turnone'(0)$ sits $0.2\sigma$ from zero
and changes sign in a third of consecutive draws. The sign of $\mathbf{c}$ is therefore
still unfixed by \eqref{eq:gauge} and is relabelled downstream. The basis stays orthonormal in $u$, which
is the measure the prior of \eqref{eq:coefprior} is written in; only the gauge inner
product is taken in $\arc$. Against \salt, $K_{\psi} = 24$ reproduces the curve at the track's own arclengths to
$0.011$~mag at $x_{1} = 0$, $0.026$ at $-1$, and $0.019$ and $0.032$ at $+1$ and $+1.7$;
$K_{\psi} = 48$ halves the first two and gains a quarter to a third on the others, and at
$K_{\psi} = 12$ the same errors are $0.100$, $0.132$, $0.086$ and $0.096$, several times
the intrinsic scatter the fits of Section~\ref{sec:inference} find. Two things the
comparison does not say. The curve error is taken only at arclengths inside the declared
interval, where the basis is defined, so it is silent about a track that leaves it; and the
$x_{1} = -2$ track does leave it, reaching $\arc = -10.06$ by $-15$~d against a lower edge
of $-8$. Inside the interval its curve error is $0.056$~mag, while its light-curve error
over the data window exceeds a magnitude at every pair of orders tried, the two quadrature
resolutions disagreeing on how much: the early rise, where the template nears zero flux, is
clamped rather than fitted. That track is not fit at any pair of orders, and what prevents
it is the interval and not the truncation --- the interval is set by where the fits reach
and not by the template, so this is a systematic of the two-band model against \salt\ and
not a defect of the basis.

Each pair is left with a sign:
the likelihood is invariant under $(q_{1}, x_p) \mapsto (-q_{1}, -x_p)$ and under the same
change in $(\turnone, \shapeturn)$, so the sign condition is a relabeling of the draws
after the fit.

Truncating the turning at $K_{\psi}$ and the traversal at $K_{q}$ gives
$2K_{\psi} + 2K_{q} - 4$ coefficients, neither pair carrying a constant, of which the four
conditions above leave $2K_{\psi} + 2K_{q} - 8$ free. Truncation bounds the function space,
so the two orders are modeling choices and not only a parameterization, and raising one
tests whether the data want more freedom than it allows. The construction degenerates only
when $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ become parallel, or $\mathbf{c}$ and $\mathbf{d}$, which is not a numerical nuisance to
suppress but the statement that the pair's two functions carry the same information.

\section{Data Sample}
\label{sec:samples}

Supernova light curves are taken from  the \ztf\ SN~Ia DR2 archive
\citep{Rigault2025}. Starting from the \Ncatalogued\ cataloged objects, we require
$\zred < 0.05$, both the \texttt{lccoverage} and \texttt{fitquality} flags equal to one,
and the archive's own cosmology classification \texttt{sn\_type == snia-cosmo}, leaving
\Nselected\ supernovae. That last cut is what restricts the sample to normal \sneia: it admits
\texttt{norm}, \texttt{91t} and \texttt{99aa} and no peculiar subtype, while
\texttt{snia-pec} holds the 91bg, Iax, 03fg, 02es, 00cx and 18byg objects whose light-curve
shapes a single $\shapeturn$ could not span alongside normals. Excluding
\texttt{snia-pec} instead would not do: one 91bg-like object is typed \texttt{snia}, its
peculiarity recorded only in \texttt{sub\_type}. Of the \Nselected, \Nnolc\ have no light-curve
file in the release; they are exactly those whose \salt\ $t_{0}$ is NaN, so the
absence is of photometry rather than of shape, and \Nphot\ objects remain with light curves. Epochs are counted per
band inside the rest-frame window $[-15,+40]$~d with $f_{i}/\sigma_{f,i} > 5$, signed, so
that a negative flux fails it as a detection should, and with
none of the quality bits excluded by \texttt{flag \& 31 == 0}, the archive default. That
mask's first bit marks $\sigma_{f} = 0$, which six epochs of this sample carry; each also
fails on \texttt{chi2dof} and limiting magnitude, so the mask removes them on their own
merits and the likelihood never meets a zero error. 
The analysis uses the \Nanalysis\ supernovae with at least five good epochs in \emph{each} of $g$
and $r$; the count is \Nthree\ at three or more and \Nten\ at ten or more.
Selection and counts are produced by \texttt{dgsn.data.sample}.

Observed-frame $g$ and $r$ are identified with their rest-frame counterparts. A two-band
model carries no spectral energy distribution and so cannot K-correct itself: only the time
axis is de-redshifted, in \eqref{eq:modeltime}. This is the reason for the $\zred < 0.05$
cut, which bounds the band shift rather than removing it. What remains is a systematic,
smooth in $\zred$ and hence partly absorbed by $\peakmag$ and $\colr$; its phase-dependent
part is not, and at $\zred = 0.05$ it is of the order of the intrinsic scatter and
correlated with $\zred$. It is quantified before the fit rather than after, as the floor
it is: a K-correction from the \citet{Hsiao2007} template at $\zred = 0.05$ is pushed
through the truncated model by the forward map of
Section~\ref{sec:staging}, and what it does to $\shapeturn$ and to $\sigma_{g}, \sigma_{r}$
is reported; the same is done for the phase drift of the Galactic extinction below at
$E(B-V) = 0.3$. Neither is ever corrected for within the fit. Measured
(\texttt{scripts/floors.py}: noise-free Hsiao light curves at signal-to-noise $50$ at
peak, the six parameters fit with the \salt-placed global functions of
Section~\ref{sec:inference} at $K_{\psi} = 24$, $K_{q} = 16$, each floor the difference
between two such fits so that the template's own mismatch cancels): the K-correction at
$\zred = 0.05$ moves $x_p$ by $+1.0$ and $\shapeturn$ by $-2.1$ and raises the per-band
residual from $0.059$ and $0.036$~mag to $0.120$ and $0.078$; the extinction drift at
$E(B-V) = 0.3$ moves $x_p$ by $-0.08$ and $\shapeturn$ by $+0.5$ and leaves the residual
at $0.061$ and $0.035$. The first is at the level of the scatter the fit measures and is
correlated with $\zred$; the second the placement absorbs, as claimed below.

Counting epochs inside $[-15,+40]$~d requires a date of
maximum, and the numbers above use the archive's \salt\ $t_{0}$ to place the window.
For the fit,
$\tmax$ is compared with $t_{0}$ and the analysis repeated with one substituted for the
other. For the selection, the sample is
recounted against an SED-free anchor --- a window on the brightest good $g$ epoch --- and
the two samples compared by \emph{membership}: equal counts can hide different objects, so
the symmetric difference is what to report. The \Nnolc\ selected objects with NaN $t_{0}$
need neither, having no photometry to place a window on.

The arclength interval of Section~\ref{sec:curve} is declared from the sample rather than
assumed. Smoothing each well-observed light curve in flux and integrating
$\dd\arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase}\,\dd\phase$ outward from the $g$ maximum gives, over
the $[-15,+40]$~d window, a median span of $[-4.9, +3.9]$ mag with fifth and ninety-fifth
percentiles at $-8.9$ and $+7.4$. The measurement is biased high, since noise lengthens a
path, and moves by $0.7$ mag across a factor of ten in smoothing; the \citet{Hsiao2007}
template, having no noise, gives $[-3.2, +3.3]$, and the \salt\ tracks across
$-2 \leq x_{1} \leq 1.7$ give $\arc(-15\,\mathrm{d})$ from $-3.7$ to $-10.1$ and
$\arc(+40\,\mathrm{d})$ from $+2.8$ to $+3.8$ (\texttt{scripts/salt\_orders.py}). We declare
$[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax] = [-12, +6]$, asymmetric: the negative edge past the fastest
decliner's $-15$~d point with margin for noise, the positive edge past every track's
$+40$~d point --- and past the $5.3$ a fast-declining object reached in the first
hierarchical fit, which is what set it. The cost of width is nearly void in the coordinate the basis is
built in, $u$ of \eqref{eq:umap} being logarithmic in $\arc$ at these scales ---
$u(-12) - u(-8)$ is four percent of the range --- while an interval extending where no
supernova goes is not free: it leaves the basis unconstrained there, and a fit
places arbitrary structure in it (Section~\ref{sec:inference}). Which edge is nearer the
truth the fit decides: the fitted traversal gives
$\arc(\phase_{\min})$ and $\arc(\phase_{\max})$ per object, and both must fall inside
$[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$; that check is how the interval is tightened or widened. The window also
opens before explosion for the fastest risers, whose early magnitude is then infinite and
whom a bounded interval cannot represent; the number of non-detection epochs inside the
window is to be counted per object, so that the size of this is known rather than absorbed
by the clamp of Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}. The same smoothed light curves supply the
sign of the $r$ slope at $g$ maximum, which the convention $\psi_{0} = -\pi/2$ of
Section~\ref{sec:curve} takes as negative for every object; the count with the opposite
sign is to be reported, such an object being one the model can hold only as an outlier.

Supernova light curves are not corrected for Galactic dust.
To first order, such corrections would appear in the form
\begin{equation}
  m_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{corr}} = m_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{obs}} - A_{\lambda},
  \qquad A_{\lambda} = R_{\lambda}\,E(B-V) ,
  \label{eq:mwext}
\end{equation}
with $R_{\lambda}$ computed for the \ztf\ passbands as in \citet{Schlafly2011}, assuming a
\citet{Fitzpatrick1999} law at
$R_{V} = 3.1$, and $E(B-V)$ the \citet{Schlegel1998} value at the supernova's coordinates,
tabulated per object as \texttt{mwebv}. A constant per band is a translation in magnitude
space, and $\mathbf{1}$ and $\dustunit$ span $\Rtwo$, so it is absorbed exactly by the
placement: $\peakmag \mapsto \peakmag + A_{g}$ and
$\colr \mapsto \colr + (A_{g} - A_{r})$, leaving the curve and hence $\curv$ untouched.
Not correcting therefore costs nothing in the fit, and means instead that $\peakmag$ and
$\colr$ carry the Galactic term, to be removed downstream where \texttt{mwebv} is known
per object. The absorption is exact only for constant $A_{\lambda}$: the band-averaged
extinction depends on a source spectrum that evolves, so $R_{\lambda}$ drifts across the
window by a few percent, and that residual does reach $\curv$ --- not negligibly, since
$E(B-V)$ exceeds $0.1$ for $23\%$ of the sample and reaches $1.06$.

\section{Likelihood}
\label{sec:likelihood}

The model is built in magnitude space and confronted with the data in flux. Magnitude space
is where distance and reddening act as translations, to which $\psi$ is insensitive; flux is
where the \ztf\ errors are Gaussian and where a non-detection is an ordinary measurement
near zero rather than a missing one.

For an epoch $i$ observed at $t_{i}$ in band $b_{i}$, \eqref{eq:modeltime} gives the
rest-frame phase $\phase_{i}$ and \eqref{eq:travlog} the arclength
$\arc_{i} = \arc(\phase_{i})$.
The predicted flux is
\begin{equation}
  \hat f_{i}(\allpars)
    = 10^{-0.4\,[\,m_{b_{i}}(\phase_{i} \mid \allpars)\, -\, \zp\,]} ,
  \label{eq:predflux}
\end{equation}
with $\zp = 30$, the archive's zero point. It is pinned, not fitted: $\zp$ and $\peakmag$
are exactly degenerate in \eqref{eq:modelmag} and \eqref{eq:predflux}, and this choice is
what makes $\peakmag$ the $g$ magnitude on the archive's scale.
The set of data is fixed, even if they fall out of the [-15,+40]~d window for the
sampled $\tmax$. It is the set of Section~\ref{sec:samples} without the signal-to-noise
cut used there for counting: the $g$ and $r$ epochs with \texttt{flag \& 31 == 0} inside
the rest-frame selection window placed on $t_{0}$, $i$ excluded, and excluding the epochs
the baseline was estimated from (\texttt{in\_baseline}). Every epoch contributes,
non-detections and negative fluxes included,
\begin{equation}
  -2\ln\mathcal{L}
    = \sum_{i}\left[\frac{\bigl(f_{i} - \hat f_{i}\bigr)^{2}}{V_{i}} + \ln V_{i}\right] ,
  \qquad
  V_{i} = \sigma_{f,i}^{2} + \bigl(0.4\ln 10\;\sigma_{b(i)}\,f_{i}\bigr)^{2} ,
  \label{eq:like}
\end{equation}
with $f_{i}$ the archive's baseline-subtracted flux, $\sigma_{f,i}$ its error taken as
published, and $\sigma_{b(i)}$ one fitted scalar per band, in magnitudes, for the band of
epoch $i$.

The variance is the archive's and nothing else. \texttt{flux\_err} already carries both the
sky and the source contribution: regressed against flux over the epochs of
Section~\ref{sec:inference}'s sample it is
$\sigma_{f}^{2} = 3.85\times10^{6} + 5.39\,f$ in $g$ and
$2.6\times10^{6} + 38.1\,f$ in $r$, a floor plus a term rising with the source, steeply in
$r$, where the faint and bright deciles of $\sigma_{f}$ stand at $1168$ and $1806$. It is
also calibrated as published: over the baseline epochs the flux scatters at $0.977$ times
$\sigma_{f}$, so DR2's own \texttt{err\_scale} is already folded in and is not to be
applied again.

\eqref{eq:like}'s second variance term is therefore \emph{not} measurement error, and
adding it as such would double-count the source contribution the regression above
displays. It is model error: the gap between the model curve and this supernova's own
curve, whose natural size is fractional because a curve good to a few percent is good to a
few percent bright or faint. $\sigma_{b}$ is fitted rather than declared, which makes its
value a measurement of how far the chosen orders fall short --- the quantity worth
minimising when $K_{q}$ and $K_{\psi}$ are selected, and one in magnitudes rather than in nats.
Its prior is half-normal with scale $0.1$~mag.

Without it the fit is driven by whichever supernovae happen to be best observed. Over the
sixty of \texttt{runs/stage3-k88-n60}, Spearman's correlation between $\chi^{2}$ per epoch
and peak signal-to-noise is $+0.862$, and with the median $\sigma_{f}/f$ it is $-0.797$:
the best-measured objects were the worst fit, which is the signature of model error and not
of noise. One object at $z = 0.0181$ with $222$ epochs carried $29\%$ of the total
$\chi^{2}$.

The term rides on the observed $f_{i}$ and not on $\hat f_{i}$. Where it dominates, the
flux is known to one or two percent, so $(a f)^{2}$ and $(a\hat f)^{2}$ agree to a few
percent --- negligible in a term whose own magnitude is what is being estimated --- and at
every epoch where the two could differ materially, $\sigma_{f}/f > 0.1$, $426$ of $3681$,
the term does not dominate at all. What this buys is that $V_{i}$ is data but for one
scalar per band, so $\ln V_{i}$ couples to no coordinate of the geometry and the
conditioning of the coefficient block of Section~\ref{sec:inference} is untouched.

The log-determinant is not optional. $\sum (f-\hat f)^{2}/V$ alone rises monotonically in
$V$, so a fitted $\sigma_{b}$ would run to infinity without $\sum\ln V_{i}$; this holds
whichever flux the term rides on, since $\sigma_{b}$ is a parameter either way, and riding
on $f$ makes $\ln V$ cheap rather than unnecessary. Walked up at the \salt-placed start
with everything else held, the density rises to a maximum near $\sigma_{b} = 0.16$~mag and
falls monotonically beyond it. One consequence is that \eqref{eq:like} now carries no
dropped constant and a log-likelihood from it is \emph{not} comparable with one from a
program that omits the term; the implementation therefore reports $-\chi^{2}/2$ on the
archive's errors alone alongside it, unchanged in meaning, so that figures quoted for
earlier runs remain usable.

Two intervals bound where the modeling functions of Section~\ref{sec:traversal} are
defined, and the posterior respects neither: $\tmax$ can carry an epoch's phase outside
$[\phase_{\min}, \phase_{\max}]$, and $\xstretch$ can carry $\arc(\phase_{\max})$ past
$\arcmax$. Both are chosen to cover most of the posterior mass, but excursions occur, and
dropping an epoch that makes one would leave the objective discontinuous. Each bound is
therefore softened rather than enforced. Taking the phase window first, whose bounds are
fixed and the same for every supernova: with
$w_{i} = [\phase_{i} - \phase_{\min}]/[\phase_{\max} - \phase_{\min}]$,
$\mathrm{sp}(x) = \epsilon\log(1 + e^{x/\epsilon})$ and $\epsilon = 10^{-3}$, one part in a
thousand of that range, the phase is soft-clamped and the epoch's weight tapered,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\phase_{i} = \phase_{\min} + (\phase_{\max} - \phase_{\min})
    \bigl[\mathrm{sp}(w_{i}) - \mathrm{sp}(w_{i}-1)\bigr] ,
  \qquad
  \mathcal{T}_{i} =
    \exp\!\bigl[-\bigl(\mathrm{sp}_{t}(-w_{i}) + \mathrm{sp}_{t}(w_{i}-1)\bigr)^{2}
      /\epsilon_{t}^{2}\bigr] ,
  \label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{sp}_{t}$ the same softplus at width $\epsilon_{t}/10$ and
$\epsilon_{t} = 0.02$, one fiftieth of the range. Both are $C^{\infty}$ in $\allpars$.
$\mathcal{T}_{i}$ multiplies that epoch's term in \eqref{eq:like} and leaves
$\hat f_{i}$ alone: outside the window an epoch is deweighted, never assigned a different
expected value. The taper wants a turn-off scale distinct from its softplus width, which is
why $\epsilon_{t}$ appears: the suppression at the edge is half a percent,
indistinguishable from unity a day inside, and the weight is down by $e^{-1}$ at
$\epsilon_{t}$ outside the window and gone at $2\epsilon_{t}$ --- a turn-off of $1.3$~d on
the $65$~d window.

Weighting and tapering the flux are not interchangeable, and the distinction is the reason
for the form above. Driving $\hat f_{i}$ to zero does not reduce an epoch's influence; it
makes the residual the whole measured flux, so the epoch contributes
$f_{i}^{2}/\sigma_{f,i}^{2}$ --- a penalty, independent of every parameter and carrying no
gradient --- and in the partial-taper zone it asks the model to predict a fraction of the
true flux, which the placement parameters absorb. Deweighting lets the epoch stop
contributing instead, and what it gives up, the pushback holding $\tmax$ near where the
data lie, is supplied by $\Delta$'s own bound --- though not everywhere before the window:
for sixteen of the thirty objects of Section~\ref{sec:samples} the window is crossed at a
smaller $|\Delta|$ than the bound allows, by $0.75$~d at the earliest, so there the weight
meets the departing epoch first.

The clamp's job is different and is still needed: $\hat\phase_{i}$ stands in for
$\phase_{i}$ in \eqref{eq:predflux} so that the modeling functions are never evaluated
outside the interval they are defined on, which is the point of clamping rather than
dropping. But a clamp moves the point the prediction is evaluated at, and
$\mathrm{sp}(w) - \mathrm{sp}(w-1)$ is symmetric about the edge, so it is half engaged
there. At the $\epsilon = 10^{-2}$ this section carried until now it still displaced an
epoch two days inside the window by $0.028$~d, where $\mathcal{T}$ is exactly one --- and
with $\mathrm{d}\arc/\mathrm{d}\phase$ near $1$ mag/d a fortnight before peak, that is up
to $0.012$ mag on the epochs that constrain the rise, against a per-band residual scatter
of $0.036$ to $0.059$. At $10^{-3}$ it is $2.7\times10^{-15}$~d two days inside and
$1.4\times10^{-8}$~d one day inside, which is zero in double precision; outside the window
the displacement is the excursion itself. So the clamp does not touch the prediction for
any epoch the window admits, and beyond the edge the weight alone sets what that epoch
counts for. The narrower $\epsilon$ is affordable for the same reason $\epsilon_{\arc}$ is,
below, and more so: the modelling window of Section~\ref{sec:traversal} exceeds the data
window by five days at each end, where the arclength interval has no such padding.

The arclength interval needs its own bound, and clamping the phase does not supply one:
$\arc(\phase)$ is finite for $\phase$ in the window but its size is set by $\xstretch$, and
a supernova one unit of log speed above the reference covers $e$ times the median span of
Section~\ref{sec:samples} --- already outside $[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$, where the turning
basis grows as a polynomial in $u$ left of its interval. With
$v_{i} = [\arc_{i} - \arc_{\min}]/[\arcmax - \arc_{\min}]$ and the softplus of
\eqref{eq:soft} at width $\epsilon_{\arc} = 10^{-3}$, one part in a thousand of the
interval, or $0.017$~mag,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\arc_{i} = \arc_{\min} + (\arcmax - \arc_{\min})
    \bigl[\mathrm{sp}(v_{i}) - \mathrm{sp}(v_{i}-1)\bigr] ,
  \label{eq:softarc}
\end{equation}
and $\psi$ is evaluated at $\hat\arc_{i}$. There is no second taper, and the asymmetry is
deliberate. The phase weight is for epochs the model does not claim to describe, which have
left the window the modeling functions are defined over; an epoch inside the phase window
whose arclength has left the interval is one the model does describe, and it is the
parameters that are out of range. Clamping alone leaves it the magnitude at the interval
endpoint --- a finite prediction a real epoch will not match, so the residual is informative
about the mismatch, where deweighting it would discard the information that the epoch is
bright. That the residual carries a useful \emph{gradient} there is a separate claim and a
weaker one: it holds in flux, and fails in parameter space once the clamp saturates, where
$\partial\arc/\partial\xstretch$ and the coefficient derivatives vanish together. The narrower $\epsilon_{\arc}$ is affordable for the
same reason the interval is padded: Section~\ref{sec:samples} finds the median span well
inside $[\arc_{\min}, \arcmax]$, so the clamp bites only on draws that are already wrong,
and its smoothing need not be gentle.


\section{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

The posterior is sampled with the no-U-turn Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampler
\citep{Hoffman2011} in \texttt{cmdstan} \citep{Carpenter2017}, and the forward map of
Section~\ref{sec:staging} is the same \texttt{Stan} code: there is no second implementation,
and it is checked against closed forms and against finite differences of its own log
density. Global coefficients, per-supernova parameters and the hyperparameters below
are sampled jointly: $2K_{\psi} + 2K_{q} - 4$ coefficients, six parameters for each
training supernova, and the scalars. Nothing is profiled or held at a point estimate. The
per-supernova parameters are incidental in the sense of \citet{Neyman1948}, and maximizing
over them biases the global quantities that share the same epochs; with
\eqref{eq:like}'s second variance term restored, $\sigma_{g}$ and $\sigma_{r}$ are again
the clearest instance --- profiling the per-supernova parameters would push the residuals
down and the fitted scatters with them --- and the reason for sampling rather than
profiling is unchanged.

The gauge is imposed by construction rather than sampled. The sampler moves on the
unconstrained raw coefficients $(\tilde{\mathbf{a}}, \tilde{\mathbf{b}}, \tilde{\mathbf{c}}, \tilde{\mathbf{d}})$ of
Section~\ref{sec:traversal}, whose normalization and orthogonalization are deterministic
transforms of them, so \eqref{eq:gauge} holds at every iteration and no constraint enters
the likelihood. The two sign conventions are not among these transforms, being
discontinuous; they are applied per draw in \texttt{generated quantities}, where they
cannot reach the gradient. Each is a joint relabeling and has to be applied as one:
flipping $q_{1}$ carries every supernova's $x_p$ with it, and flipping $\turnone$ every
$\shapeturn$, since it is only the products that the likelihood leaves alone. Doing this
inside the fit rather than afterwards is what makes the saved draws comparable across
chains, which land in either branch independently.
The mean coefficients are given normal priors whose width decays with the mode index, one
pair of hyperparameters per domain since the traversal and the turning need not be equally
smooth,
\begin{equation}
  b_{k} \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\; \tau_{q}^{2} k^{-2\alpha_{q}}\right) ,
  \qquad
  d_{k} \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\; \tau_{\psi}^{2} k^{-2\alpha_{\psi}}\right) ,
  \qquad \alpha_{q}, \alpha_{\psi} > \tfrac{1}{2} ,
  \label{eq:coefprior}
\end{equation}

and the departure coefficients carry the same decay with their scale removed by
\eqref{eq:gauge}, $\tilde a_{k} = k^{-\alpha_{q}}z_{k}$ with $z_{k}$ standard normal and
likewise $\tilde c_{k}$, so that $\|q_{1}\| = \|\turnone\| = 1$ fixes what $\tau$ would
otherwise set. \eqref{eq:coefprior} is written on the coefficients that satisfy
\eqref{eq:gauge} and not on the raw $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}, \tilde{\mathbf{d}}$ the sampler
moves on. The distinction is not
cosmetic. \eqref{eq:coefprior} is a statement about the smoothness of $q_{0}$ and
$\turnzero$, and those functions are built from $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{d}$; the tilde is
an artifact of how the gauge is imposed. Writing the prior on the tilde instead gives a
prior to a direction that reaches nothing. The orthogonalization
$\mathbf{b} = \tilde{\mathbf{b}} - (\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{a})\mathbf{a}$
discards $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$'s component along $\mathbf{a}$, and
$\mathbf{d} = \tilde{\mathbf{d}} - (\tilde{\mathbf{d}}^{\mathsf T}G_{\arc}\mathbf{c})\mathbf{c}$
discards $\tilde{\mathbf{d}}$'s, so those two components enter neither the forward map nor
the likelihood; under the tilde form their only curvature is
\eqref{eq:coefprior}'s own, whose width grows with $\tau$, so the posterior's condition
number grows with $\tau$ along directions that carry no information at all. Measured on
the mode-bearing runs, removing the four such directions takes $\sqrt{\kappa}$ from $5.62$
to $1.00$ and from $9.84$ to $1.38$ at median draws.

Two of the four cost nothing already: the departure pairs are non-centered, so the scales
of $\tilde{\mathbf{a}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}$ are carried by standard normal
$\mathbf{z}$ and have unit precision. It is the mean pairs that need the change.

Written on $\mathbf{b}$, \eqref{eq:coefprior} is a density on the hyperplane
$\{\mathbf{x} : \mathbf{n}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{x} = 0\}$ with $\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{a}$, and
on that hyperplane it must be normalized, the orientation being a parameter. For a
diagonal covariance $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(w_{k}^{2})$ and $\mathbf{n}$ of unit length the
normalization is
\begin{equation}
  -\log Z = \mathrm{const} - \sum_{k}\log w_{k}
          + \tfrac{1}{2}\log\!\left(\sum_{k} n_{k}^{2} w_{k}^{2}\right) ,
  \label{eq:hypernorm}
\end{equation}
the last term being the only departure from the unconstrained case. The turning pair is
the same with $\mathbf{n} = G_{\arc}\mathbf{c}$ rescaled to unit length, the constraint
$\langle \turnzero, \turnone\rangle = 0$ being $\mathbf{n}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{d} = 0$.

The discarded components are given unit width, $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{a} \sim
\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{d}}^{\mathsf T}G_{\arc}\mathbf{c} \sim
\mathcal{N}(0,1)$. Their priors are unobservable --- nothing downstream of them depends on
either --- so the choice is free, and unit width is what makes the precision along them
independent of $\tau$. The change of variables from $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ to the pair
$(\mathbf{b}, \tilde{\mathbf{b}}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{a})$ has unit Jacobian, $\mathbf{a}$ being
a unit vector; for the turning pair the decomposition is oblique in the coefficient basis,
$\mathbf{c}$ being normalized under $G_{\arc}$ rather than Euclidean, and the Jacobian is
$\|G_{\arc}\mathbf{c}\|^{-1}$.

What \eqref{eq:hypernorm} does \emph{not} do is say anything about where $q_{1}$ and
$\turnone$ point, and it is worth being explicit because the opposite is easy to
believe: the normalization depends on $\mathbf{n}$, so it looks like a prior on the
departure direction. It is the reverse. The joint factors as
$p(\mathbf{z}_{c})\,p(\tilde{\mathbf{d}} \mid \mathbf{c})$, and
\eqref{eq:hypernorm} together with the Jacobian is exactly what makes the conditional
integrate to one at every $\mathbf{c}$ --- so the marginal prior on $\mathbf{c}$ is the
projected normal that $\mathbf{z}_{c}$ carries and nothing else, unchanged by any of this.
Dropping the two terms is what would tilt it, by a factor of $1.8$ across six draws from
the prior and $6$ to $8$ across its range.

What does change is the measure on $\mathbf{d}$ itself. Under the tilde form
$\mathbf{d}$ was the \emph{pushforward} of \eqref{eq:coefprior} through the projection,
covariance $P\Sigma P^{\mathsf T}$; written on $\mathbf{d}$ it is \eqref{eq:coefprior}
\emph{restricted} to the hyperplane, covariance
$\Sigma - \Sigma\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma / (\mathbf{n}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf{n})$.
These are different measures and the difference is not small: per-coefficient prior
standard deviations are up to $1.83$ times narrower under restriction at $K_{\psi} = 12$.
The second change is that $\tau$ now normalizes $K-2$ coefficients rather than $K-1$,
shifting its own effective prior by one power.

None of these terms reaches the likelihood, which is all the transfer gate of
Section~\ref{sec:staging} tests, so a prior error here passes that gate silently. They are
checked instead on the directions along which the likelihood is \emph{exactly} invariant,
which isolates them without a second implementation of it: adding $\lambda\mathbf{a}$ to
$\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ or $\lambda\mathbf{c}$ to $\tilde{\mathbf{d}}$ must change the target
by the unit prior on the discarded component alone; rescaling $\mathbf{z}_{a}$ or
$\mathbf{z}_{c}$ must change it by the standard normal alone; rescaling $\tau$ must change
it by \eqref{eq:coefprior}'s quadratic plus $-(K-2)\log\lambda$; and the declared
conditional must integrate to one whatever the hyperplane's orientation.
with the $\tau$ flat on $\tau > 0$ and the $\alpha$ flat on
$\tfrac{1}{2} < \alpha < 8\tfrac{1}{2}$, all
sampled with the rest; the smoothness of the global functions is
thus inferred and not asserted. The bounds on $\alpha$ carry no preference: the lower is
\eqref{eq:coefprior}'s own convergence condition, and the upper is there only to make the
flat prior proper, standing some ten posterior standard deviations above where the training
sample puts either exponent. An $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ on $\alpha - \tfrac12$ was carried
through \texttt{model-v1.1} and is not neutral: on the same data, orders and start it
returns $\alpha_{q}$ and $\alpha_{\psi}$ lower by $0.77$ and $0.41$ of a posterior standard
deviation, a pull toward rougher $q_{0}$ and $\turnzero$ than the sample asks for, while
moving no other quantity in the model by as much as $0.16\sigma$. Asserting less of the
smoothness is the point of \eqref{eq:coefprior} being a hierarchy at all. The mode index is referred to its geometric mean,
$\bar k = \exp\langle \log k\rangle$, the width reading
$\tau^{2}(k/\bar k)^{-2\alpha}$: written against $k$ itself, \eqref{eq:coefprior} is a
line in $\log k$ whose intercept is read at $k = 1$, a mode neither series carries, and
intercept and slope then correlate as in any uncentered regression --- measured at $+0.55$
between $\log\tau_{q}$ and $\log(\alpha_{q} - \tfrac12)$ on thirty objects and $+0.81$ on
the training sample, falling to $-0.07$ at the pivot. It is the centering the prior's own
information matrix picks out, and it makes $\tau$ the scale of a mid-range mode. What is
flat is then $\tau$ at the pivot. How the pairs are parameterized follows from what
identifies them. The departure coefficients are written non-centered,
$\tilde a_{k} = k^{-\alpha_{q}}z_{k}$ with $z_{k}$ standard normal --- the gauge normalizes
the functions they carry, so their scale is the prior's alone, and a scale sampled jointly
with coefficients it multiplies is the geometry Hamiltonian Monte Carlo handles worst. The
mean coefficients are written centered, $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ sampled directly and
$\mathbf{b}$ carrying \eqref{eq:coefprior}: the training sample pins them, and under strong data the non-centered
form is the pathology rather than the cure --- the first hierarchical fit, with all four
vectors non-centered, held $\|\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\|$ fixed while trading $\tau_{q}$ against
$\|\mathbf{z}_{b}\|$ at correlation $-0.6$, with $88$ percent of transitions at the maximum
treedepth of $12$ and $11$ percent divergent. The per-supernova amplitudes with sampled
locations are likewise written as deviations about them, $x_p = \mu_{p} + u$ with $u$
standard normal and $\tmax$'s offset as $\Delta = \mu_{\Delta} + s_{\Delta}u$: the same
fit's longest directions were the common modes of the per-supernova block riding with
their locations --- $\mathrm{corr}(\mu_{\psi}, \overline{\shapeturn}) = +1.0$ across
draws, and $+0.95$ between $\log s_{\Delta}$ and the spread of the $\Delta$ --- thirty
coordinates wide each, which a diagonal metric cannot see; written as deviations, each is
the one coordinate its location already is. None of this changes the posterior.

The width decays so that the prior on the functions does not move when a truncation order
does; a common width would make it a function of the order. The remaining priors are as
follows. The departure amplitudes are normal with unit width about a mean sampled per
amplitude, $x_p \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{p}, 1)$ and $\shapeturn \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\psi}, 1)$
with $\mu_{p}, \mu_{\psi}$ flat. The width is a statement because the gauge normalizes the
functions they multiply, $\langle q_{1}, q_{1}\rangle = \langle \turnone, \turnone\rangle = 1$,
so a unit amplitude is a departure of root-mean-square $(v_{\max} - v_{\min})^{-1/2}$ in
the log rate and $(\arc_{\mathrm{g}+} - \arc_{\mathrm{g}-})^{-1/2}$ radians along the reached track in the turning. The means are free because
$\langle q_{0}, q_{1}\rangle = 0$ makes the reference supernova the one orthogonal to the
departure mode and not the population mean, so a prior centred at zero would pull the
population toward a point with no physical meaning; $\mu_{p}$ is identified because that
same condition has fixed the component of $q_{0}$ along $q_{1}$, and $\mu_{\psi}$ likewise.
The time origin is written
$\tmax = t_{0} + \Delta$ with $\Delta \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{\Delta}, s_{\Delta}^{2})$,
$\mu_{\Delta}$ flat and $s_{\Delta}$ flat on $s_{\Delta} > 0$: $t_{0}$ is $B$ maximum and
$\tmax$ is $g$ maximum, and the offset between them is measured by the fit, centre and
sign included, rather than assumed. The traversal basis of \eqref{eq:basisq} drops the constant Legendre term and anchors each
mode at the origin, so $q_{0}(0) = 0$ identically and $q_{0}$ cannot carry the mean log rate:
it is $\xstretch$ that carries it outright, and under this gauge it is $\ln \dd\arc/\dd p$
at $p = 0$ exactly rather than a mean speed over the window. Under the mean-zero gauge the
same parameter was instead the $v$-weighted mean of the log rate, which that measure pulls
towards the anchor where the rate is smallest, and it differed from a mean speed by more than
a unit ($-1.8$ for the log of the median span over the window against $-3.0$ for the
parameter, the same to $0.1$ across $x_{1}$ and for the \citet{Hsiao2007} template); the
anchored value has not been measured. That mean is written as a parameter of its own,
\begin{equation}
  x_{s,n} = \bar s + \delta_{n} , \qquad \sum_{n} \delta_{n} = 0 ,
  \label{eq:xsmean}
\end{equation}
with $\bar s$ flat. The constraint is what fixes the split, and nothing is assumed about how
the $\xstretch$ are distributed: a fitted mean with a normal population prior would instead
assume them Gaussian, which a sum over the realized values does not. The two
parameterizations span the same thirty coordinates and, under flat priors, the same
posterior; writing the mean separately makes the common mode of the block one coordinate
rather than thirty, for the reason the deviations of $x_p$ and $\Delta$ are written the same
way.

Where the departure amplitudes are given flat boxes in place of the normal population prior
above --- the variant that drops the hierarchy entirely --- they take the same split and for
the same reason, with both parts flat as in \eqref{eq:xsmean}:
\begin{equation}
  x_{p,n} = \bar x_{p} + \delta_{p,n} , \quad \sum_{n} \delta_{p,n} = 0 ;
  \qquad
  x_{\psi,n} = \bar x_{\psi} + \delta_{\psi,n} , \quad \sum_{n} \delta_{\psi,n} = 0 ,
  \label{eq:ampmean}
\end{equation}
$\bar x_{p}$ and $\bar x_{\psi}$ flat on $[-20, 20]$ and the deviations flat on the same
box, which spans the realized amplitudes with room to spare --- the largest drawn are $4.4$
and $9.6$. The box is on each part rather than on the sum, so the reachable set is not
quite the one a single box gives; nothing has approached either wall. Written without the
split, the mean of a block of $N$ amplitudes is one direction inside it and a diagonal
metric must give it the scale it gives the deviations. Measured as
$\mathrm{var}(\bar x)\,N / \overline{\mathrm{var}(x_{n})}$, which is unity when that
scale is right, the unsplit amplitudes read $6.3$ to $8.2$ over sixty supernovae and $2.4$
to $46.2$ over $255$, against exactly zero for every block already written this way. The
cost is the whole of it: at $255$ the unsplit $\bar x_{p}$ was the slowest direction in the
problem, an integrated autocorrelation time of $28.8$ draws against $2.0$ for $\bar c$, and
a split $\hat{R}$ of $2.48$ where its own deviations sat at $1.07$. Like \eqref{eq:xsmean}
this is a change of coordinates and not of the model: the amplitudes span the same $N$
coordinates and, under flat priors, the same posterior. It buys conditioning, not
identification --- $\langle q_{0}, q_{1}\rangle = 0$ fixes $\bar x_{p}$, so its value is a
statement about the gauge and not about supernovae, and whether $q_{1}$ earns its place is
read from the spread of the $\delta_{p}$ and from prediction, never from the mean.

An earlier version of this section centred $\xstretch$ on $\bar x_{s}$, the mean of
$\log\,\dd\arc/\dd\phase$ in $v$ of the \salt\ track at the sample-median $x_{1}$, $-3.0$.
That was a template-derived constant inside the prior rather than the start, and it was
misplaced: the first hierarchical fits put the population mean at $-2.85$ and $-2.70$ at
$K_{\psi} = 12$ and $24$, $2.3$ and $3.1$ standard deviations away, while the realized
spread of $\xstretch$ across thirty objects was $0.12$ to $0.25$ against a prior width of
unity, and no object drew as much as a tenth of its precision from it.

$\peakmag$ and $\colr$ are written the same way. An earlier version of this section had
$\peakmag \sim \mathcal{N}(m_{g}^{\mathrm{bright}}, 1)$, centred on the magnitude of the
brightest good $g$ epoch, and $\colr \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. The first read the same
photometry the likelihood reads, a datum used twice, and the second asserted a mean anchor
colour of zero where the fits put it at $+0.060 \pm 0.013$ over thirty objects and
$+0.0597 \pm 0.0032$ over a hundred. In place of them, $\peakmag$ is flat on $[5, 30]$~mag
and $\colr = \bar c + \delta_{c}$ with $\sum_{n}\delta_{c,n} = 0$ and $\bar c$ flat on
$[-5, 5]$. Neither box is an assertion about supernovae: $\zp$ and $\peakmag$ are exactly
degenerate, so the magnitude origin is a convention already fixed at $\zp = 30$, and in it
$\peakmag = 30$ is a predicted flux of one archive unit against a sample spanning
$1.5\times10^{4}$ to $7.8\times10^{5}$.

None of these is informative against the data, and each is there to make the posterior
proper. With flat priors the likelihood tends to a positive constant in \emph{both} tails of
$\xstretch$, not one. As $\xstretch \to -\infty$ the rate $\dd\arc/\dd\phase$ vanishes, the
track freezes at the anchor and the model is a constant light curve; as
$\xstretch \to +\infty$ the rate diverges, every epoch is driven into the arclength clamp,
and the model is a constant light curve again, at a different constant. Walked out from a
\salt\ start at thirty objects, the log density settles to $-4.83\times10^{6}$ downward and
to $-2.93\times10^{6}$ upward, each flat to four decimals over a further factor of two in
$|\xstretch|$ --- and the upward plateau, the one an earlier version of this section did not
name, is the higher of the two. So \eqref{eq:xsmean} cannot be left flat however
uninformative a prior would be where the data are: $\bar s$ is given $\mathcal{N}(0, 10^{2})$
and the deviations $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, the first against a mean the data pin to $0.07$ and
the second four to eight times wider than the spread it bounds, so each is a propriety
condition and neither is a population model. The deviations need their own prior and do not
inherit propriety from the constraint: a ray inside the sum-to-zero hyperplane sends one
object to each plateau and settles at $-2.47\times10^{5}$, flat to four decimals.
The same happens as $\peakmag \to +\infty$ or $\colr \to -\infty$, where the predicted flux
vanishes, and the two boxes above are what close it. And as $\alpha \to \infty$ every mode
but the first has gone and the fit is poor but finite. A chain started in any of those tails
would stay there, and a flat prior over them integrates to infinity.

The deviations of $\Delta$ take a broad normal, $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\Delta}^{2})$ with
$\sigma_{\Delta} = 3$~d held fixed and $\mu_{\Delta}$ fitted, and those of $\colr$ take no
prior at all. The colour deviations need none because colour is the only placement direction
the forward map does not clamp: a runaway inside the sum-to-zero hyperplane drives a
predicted $r$ flux to infinity, where the Gaussian-in-flux likelihood closes it unaided.
$\Delta$'s do need one, since with the mean held a hyperplane ray saturates the phase clamp
and the density goes flat. Through 2026-11-16 that was a flat wall at $3$~d, imposed as a
sampling statement --- a \texttt{sum\_to\_zero\_vector} admits no declared bound, but a
\texttt{uniform} statement on one still returns $-\infty$ outside the interval while
contributing exactly nothing inside. Rejecting proved to be the wrong instrument: the
deviations were the only block pressed against a wall, and a crossing mid-trajectory is an
$-\infty$ the sampler records as a divergence. Three days is $3.2$ times the realized spread
of $0.929$~d, so the normal shapes nothing it replaces, while supplying the restoring
gradient the wall withheld --- $\chi^{2}$ of $1.0$ at $3$~d and $4.0$ at $6$~d, which is the
pushback holding $\tmax$ near where the data lie that the taper of
Section~\ref{sec:likelihood} gives up. It is fixed and never fitted: fitting it is the
$(s_{\Delta}, \mathbf{u})$ degeneracy of \eqref{eq:deltaridge}, whose supremum is at
$s_{\Delta} \to \infty$. On a sum-to-zero block the marginal width is
$\sigma_{\Delta}\sqrt{(N-1)/N}$. \texttt{docs/runs-ledger.md} carries the measurement.
Whether either is needed at all is unmeasured: the sum-to-zero constraint forces a
compensating object, and for colour the compensating direction drives the predicted flux to
$+\infty$, where the Gaussian likelihood dies, which may make a flat prior proper on its own,
while for $\Delta$ both directions reach the phase clamp and saturate, which would not.
$\alpha_{q}$ is informed by $\tilde{\mathbf{a}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ and
$\alpha_{\psi}$ by $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{d}}$, since normalizing
$\tilde{\mathbf{a}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{c}}$ removes $\tau$ but not the relative decay
across modes; $\tau_{q}$ only by $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ and $\tau_{\psi}$ only by
$\tilde{\mathbf{d}}$, whose norms are physical. The $\alpha$ are the hyperparameters to
watch: a fit that wants no high modes will push them up against the prior.

Initialization is exempt from the rule that \salt\ stays downstream, since it sets where
chains start and not what they converge to, and dispersed-start agreement is what checks
it. Per supernova, $\Delta$ starts at zero, $\peakmag$ and $\colr$ at the magnitude and
color the archive's \salt\ fit implies, $\xstretch$ at $\bar x_{s}$, and the departure
amplitudes $x_p$ and $\shapeturn$ at zero, so that a chain begins on the reference
supernova and the first modes contribute nothing. The global functions start from the
\salt\ fits of Section~\ref{sec:traversal}: $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{d}}$
from the fits at the sample median $x_{1}$, and $\tilde{\mathbf{a}}$ and
$\tilde{\mathbf{c}}$ from the difference between the fits at $x_{1} = +1$ and $-1$, which
the normalization then scales. Those fits carry a ridge of the form of
\eqref{eq:coefprior}, $\lambda k^{2\alpha}c_{k}^{2}$ with $\alpha = 2$ and $\lambda$
small enough to shrink the highest constrained mode by a few percent: a template reaches
$\arc \approx +3.3$ at $+40$~d while the interval runs to $\arcmax = 8$, so a quarter of
the turning interval holds no template, an unregularized fit leaves arbitrary coefficients
there, and the difference of two such fits is dominated by them --- a departure mode living
where no epoch goes, invisible to the likelihood however it is normalized. With the ridge
the departure mode is smooth, and the single-supernova fit of Section~\ref{sec:staging}
resolves $\shapeturn$; without it, its posterior width was its prior width. The hyperparameters, the scatters and the departure means
are left to Stan's own initialization. A random start for $\xstretch$ would be not a slow
start but a stuck one: half of Stan's default range $[-2, 2]$ gives speeds that carry every
epoch to the arclength clamp of Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}, where the gradient in
$\xstretch$ vanishes and warmup has nothing to follow. If warmup does not arrive, the
initialization is the first thing to revisit. Four chains are run. The number of chains, and the
warmup and sampling iterations, are Stan's defaults; the four chains are run as four
separate jobs rather than one job of four, so that they are independent processes and a
chain that stalls does not hold up the rest. Convergence is judged from the
usual statistics --- $\hat R$, effective sample size, divergent transitions --- read on the
gauge-fixed functions and on the predicted fluxes rather than on the raw coefficients, four
of whose directions the gauge leaves unidentified and on which those statistics carry no
information about the fit.

\subsection{Evaluating the model}
\label{sec:numerics}

Two quadratures are wanted, not three: $\psi$ is the expansion itself, a matrix product
against \eqref{eq:basispsi}, and is never integrated for. Neither of the remaining two is
elementary --- $\arc(\phase)$ is $\exp$ of an expansion and $\curve$ is $\cos$ of one --- so \eqref{eq:frenet2d} is still
solved by quadratures of known integrands rather than as an initial-value problem, evaluated
on shared nodes rather than by stepping. The gauge does not enter: \eqref{eq:gauge} is
algebra on the coefficients, not an integral.

Both are taken by composite Gauss--Legendre: each side of zero is divided into $M/2 = 10$
equal panels, $M = 20$ in all, and a $J = 8$ point rule applied in each, in $u$ for the
curve and in $v$ for the traversal, the coordinates the integrands are smooth in. Each change of variable carries
its Jacobian, $\dd\arc = \delta\cosh u\,\dd u$ and $\dd\phase = \delta_{p}\cosh v\,\dd v$, so the curve integrand is
$(\cos\psi, \sin\psi)\,\delta\cosh u$ and the traversal integrand
$\exp[\xstretch + q_{0} + x_p q_{1}]\,\delta_{p}\cosh v$; the first factor spans four orders of magnitude across
the interval, and placing the nodes in $u$ rather than in $\arc$ is what keeps the rest of
the integrand smooth against it. The panels are laid from zero outward on each side of it,
so that zero is a panel edge and $\arc(0) = 0$, $\curve(0) = 0$ hold by construction of the
prefix table below rather than up to a partial panel.

The nodes are fixed for the run, so abscissae and weights are constants and only the
integrand values change. What is wanted is the integral to each epoch rather than over the
whole interval, and epochs do not sit on nodes: the panel integrals are summed once into a
prefix table, and the remainder from the last completed edge to $v_{i}$, or to
$u_{i}$, is one further $J$-point panel. Against the same rule at $M = 80$, with the
\salt-fitted functions at $K_{\psi} = 24$, $K_{q} = 16$ and the traversal in $v$
(\texttt{scripts/salt\_orders.py}), this gives $4\times10^{-7}$~mag on the traversal and
$1\times10^{-7}$ on the curve over the data window, $4\times10^{-7}$ on the light curve;
$2\times10^{-4}$ at $M = 10$ and below $10^{-11}$ at $M = 40$. Two hazards of the arithmetic rather than of the rule: the softplus of
Section~\ref{sec:likelihood} is evaluated as $\max(x,0) + \log(1 + e^{-|x|})$, its naive
form overflowing at the widths used, and the arithmetic is double precision, the
accuracies above being unreachable in single.

Cost follows from where the per-supernova parameters enter. The turning is linear in
$\shapeturn$, so $\psi$ for every supernova is one matrix product and a scalar
combination; but $\curve$ is $\cos$ of $\psi$, and that integral is taken for each. In
\eqref{eq:travlog} $\xstretch$ factors out as $e^{\xstretch}$ and only $x_p$ survives inside,
leaving one more integral per supernova. Two sweeps of the training curves over the shared
nodes, then, at every evaluation of the log-density.

The sample is split into $442$ supernovae for training and $110$ for validation, drawn
$22$ at a time from each fifth of the $x_{1}$ distribution, since it is along $x_{1}$ that
the difficulty of this model varies. The training count coincides with the \Nten\ objects
having ten or more epochs and is not that subsample. There is no third split.

What validation withholds is the global functions, which is what the truncation orders
control. The six per-supernova parameters are not shared and cannot be withheld --- a
supernova of which nothing has been seen cannot be predicted --- so a validation object is
fit for its own six, sampled under the same priors with
$\turnzero, \turnone, q_{0}, q_{1}$ and the departure means held
at their training posterior means, and the score below averaged over the draws. Those six
are fit on the same epochs whose residuals are then
scored, which makes the exercise mildly optimistic.

The truncation orders are set by running the fit over
$K_{\psi} \in \{12, 24, 48\}$ and $K_{q} \in \{8, 16, 32\}$ and comparing the held-out
$-2\ln\mathcal{L}$ of \eqref{eq:like} over validation epochs, $\sigma_{b}$ held at its
training value, with $\sigma_{g}(K), \sigma_{r}(K)$ tabulated in magnitudes beside it.

The plain $\sum_{i}(f_{i} - \hat f_{i})^{2}/\sigma_{f,i}^{2}$ will not serve, and the
reason is a cost of restoring the second variance term rather than an oversight. A fitted
scatter absorbs in training whatever the truncation cannot fit; validation holds that
scatter and meets the same truncation, so the residual normalized by $\sqrt{V_{i}}$ has
unit spread at every order and truncation error is reported as model error instead of as
misfit. What makes the comparison proper again is the $\sum\ln V_{i}$ of
\eqref{eq:like}: an order that needs a larger $\sigma_{b}$ is charged for it there, so the
log score ranks orders where the normalized residual alone cannot. This is the arrangement
an earlier version of this section used, and it is the arrangement \eqref{eq:like} returns
to; the interval in which the plain statistic served was the interval in which the variance
was the archive's alone.

The diagnostic and the statistic are therefore two numbers again rather than one. The
diagnostic keeps $\sigma_{f,i}$ alone in the denominator, which is data and identical
across orders, so the spread of $(f_{i} - \hat f_{i})/\sigma_{f,i}$ stays free to exceed
unity by exactly the amount the truncation misses; $\sigma_{b}(K)$ is the same information
compressed to one number per band, and in magnitudes rather than in nats. Residual within the training
sample falls with either order by construction and so ranks nothing. \eqref{eq:coefprior}
is what keeps the comparison about truncation rather than prior: a larger basis is free to
leave its high modes near zero instead of obliged to fill them, and with $\alpha$ inferred
the fit sets how fast they decay, so an order past what the data support costs shrunken
modes rather than a worse fit. Orders the score cannot separate are orders
the data cannot separate, and the smallest of those is taken. Residuals normalized by the
measurement error $\sigma_{f,i}$ alone, binned in phase and by $x_{1}$ stratum as well as
pooled, are reported beside the score: truncation shows as structure at fixed phase, and
the dip of Section~\ref{sec:traversal} as structure inside $|\phase| < 3$~d in the
fast-decliner stratum, which is where the model is expected to fail first.

The brackets reach below what Section~\ref{sec:traversal} found a \salt\ template to ask
for, because a template carries no noise: \ztf\ epochs at signal-to-noise of order ten
cannot resolve structure at $0.02$~mag, so the orders the data support should fall under
the ones a template asks for. The asymmetry Section~\ref{sec:traversal} found between the
turning and the traversal across $x_{1}$ was measured with the traversal expanded in phase,
and the coordinate $v$ exists to remove it; whether it does is read from the residuals by
stratum.

\subsection{Bringing the fit up}
\label{sec:staging}

The cost of a fit rises by orders of magnitude across the stages below, so each is arranged
to test what the one before it could not, and to fail cheaply on it.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The forward map at fixed parameters, run in \texttt{Stan} itself, checked against
  the cases that have closed forms: constant $\psi'$ gives a circle, a unit rate gives
  $\arc = \phase$ on the unclamped map, and $\xstretch$ scales arclength by $e^{\xstretch}$.
  A time-dilated copy of a reference supernova walking at a constant rate, $q_{0} = 0$, must
  fit with $x_p = \shapeturn = 0$ to numerical precision and $\xstretch$ equal to the log
  dilation --- with $q_{0}$ a function of phase, dilating time rescales its argument and
  the copy leaves the family the model spans at $x_p = 0$, so the test is exact only for
  a constant rate --- and a constant vector added to both bands must move only
  $\peakmag$ and $\colr$. Each is a gradient of the log density that must vanish at the
  exact parameters, and a fit that must return there. The gradient is checked against
  finite differences of the log density. This is the instrument for the floors of
  Section~\ref{sec:samples}, the K-correction and the extinction drift, which are pushed
  through it before any fit.

\item One supernova, six parameters, the global functions supplied as data rather than
  sampled. This exercises the whole chain --- phase to arclength to magnitude to flux ---
  together with both clamps and \eqref{eq:like}, and it runs in seconds. A fault in the
  quadrature, in a clamp, or in the Jacobian of \eqref{eq:umap} surfaces here at no cost.

\item The full model at the smallest orders on a few tens of supernovae. First contact with
  the hierarchy: the funnel that \eqref{eq:coefprior} is written non-centered to avoid, the
  $\alpha$ against their prior, the two sign branches, and whether warmup arrives from the
  \salt-placed start. Read here too, per draw: the fraction of epochs with a taper factor
  below $0.99$ or an arclength clamp displacement above $\epsilon_{\arc}$, which must be
  near zero on accepted draws, and the distribution of $\Delta$ against its prior.

\item The full training sample at one pair of orders, which establishes the cost of a fit
  and whether convergence is reachable at all.

\item The grid over $K_{\psi}$ and $K_{q}$, once a single fit is healthy.
\end{enumerate}

The ordering is not fastidiousness. In a hierarchical fit that has failed to converge, an
error in a quadrature or a clamp is indistinguishable from a bad prior, a funnel, or a
model that the data cannot support; the early stages exist to remove the first kind of
explanation before the others are entertained. The reference implementation earns its keep
the same way: without it, a disagreement between prediction and data has two candidate
sources rather than one.


\section{Standardization without a reference color}
\label{sec:cmagic}

\citet{Wang2003} report that \sneia\ read at a fixed value of a color index have a small
magnitude dispersion, and that over much of the first month past maximum the relation
between magnitude and color is linear. Both statements are about the track of
\eqref{eq:curvedef}, not about either light curve, so the model carries them with no new
parameter. What follows writes that correspondence and then removes the reference color
from it, there being nothing in the geometry that chooses one.

The construction is \eqref{eq:stdmag}. Take $\arc^{\star}$, the first zero of $\curv$ at
positive arclength; read the slope \eqref{eq:cmslope} there,
$\lambda^{\star} = \cmslope(\arc^{\star})$; and standardize by
$\stdmag = \peakmag - \lambda^{\star}\colr$. The evaluation point is a feature of each
track rather than a color held fixed across tracks, and it is the point at which the
reference color of \citet{Wang2003} stops mattering and the relation stops being linear
only by truncation. Nothing is fit to residuals: $\lambda^{\star}$ is a functional of the
turning already in the posterior, so the correction carries no parameter of its own, and
with $\shapeturn \equiv 0$ it is one number for the sample. Neither $\xstretch$ nor
$\Delta$ enters, both reparameterizing \eqref{eq:curvedef} and so leaving $\curv(\arc)$
and $\cmslope(\arc)$ alone; the whole per-supernova correction is the color term, and the
classical shape correction becomes a prediction instead, \S\ref{sec:shapescalars}.
\eqref{eq:stdmag} is an apparent magnitude, and the rest of what it is not is
\S\ref{sec:cmagicgaps}.

\subsection{The slope is the tangent direction}
\label{sec:cmslope}

The empirical slope is a ratio of rates, which by \eqref{eq:tangangle} is the direction of
the tangent:
\begin{equation}
  \cmslope(\arc) \equiv \deriv{\magg}{(\magg - \magr)}
    = \frac{\cos\psi(\arc)}{\cos\psi(\arc) - \sin\psi(\arc)} ,
  \qquad
  \psi = \arctan\!\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{\cmslope}\Bigr) \;\;(\mathrm{mod}\ \pi) .
  \label{eq:cmslope}
\end{equation}
The convention $\psi(0) = -\pi/2$ reads $\cmslope = 0$ at the anchor, $\psi = 0$ reads
$\cmslope = 1$ at the $r$ maximum, and $\psi = \pi/4$ sends $\cmslope$ to infinity:
$\cmslope$ is a chart on the tangent direction rather than a function of it, singular where
the color is stationary. In that chart the slope is not a parameter of the model but
$\psi$ itself, and it inherits what $\psi$ has --- the translations
$\peakmag\mathbf{1} + \colr\dustunit$ of \eqref{eq:modelmag} and any reparameterization
$\arc(\phase)$ leave it alone.

Linearity is $\curv = 0$, and since $\curv = \psi'$,
\begin{equation}
  \deriv{\cmslope}{\arc} = \frac{\curv}{(\cos\psi - \sin\psi)^{2}} ,
  \label{eq:dslope}
\end{equation}
so $\cmslope$ is stationary exactly where the curvature vanishes. The linear interval
\citet{Wang2003} identify and the slope they fit on it are therefore one statement about
$\curv$: an interval over which it is small, and the stationary value of $\cmslope$ there.
Several zeros of $\curv$ give several such intervals, each with its own stationary slope,
so the zeros of $\curv$ enumerate the straight segments and \eqref{eq:cmslope} predicts
each one's slope.

\subsection{The reference color enters as a zero point}
\label{sec:refcol}

Write $\colfn$ for the color accumulated along the template,
\begin{equation}
  \colfn(\arc) = \Magg(\arc) - \Magr(\arc)
    = \int_{0}^{\arc}\bigl[\cos\psi(\zeta) - \sin\psi(\zeta)\bigr]\,\dd\zeta ,
  \label{eq:colfn}
\end{equation}
so that by \eqref{eq:modelmag} the observed color at arclength $\arc$ is
$\colfn(\arc) + \colr$ and the observed $g$ magnitude is $\Magg(\arc) + \peakmag$. Reading
the magnitude at a reference color $\refcol$ means solving $\colfn(\arc) + \colr = \refcol$
and evaluating there. With
\begin{equation}
  F(x) \equiv \Magg\bigl(\colfn^{-1}(x)\bigr) , \qquad
  F' = \cmslope , \qquad
  F'' = \frac{\curv}{(\cos\psi - \sin\psi)^{3}} ,
  \label{eq:Fdef}
\end{equation}
that magnitude is exactly
\begin{equation}
  M_{\refcol} = \peakmag + F(\refcol - \colr) .
  \label{eq:mxi}
\end{equation}
The same denominator that makes $\cmslope$ singular makes $\colfn$ non-invertible, both
vanishing at $\psi = \pi/4$, so \eqref{eq:mxi} is defined only on the branch before the
color turns around --- the branch \citet{Wang2003} restrict to, and not the whole window
the data reach.

Expanding about a fiducial color $\bar\colr$,
\begin{equation}
  M_{\refcol}
    = \underbrace{\bigl[\peakmag - \cmslope\,\colr\bigr]}_{\text{per supernova}}
    + \underbrace{\bigl[F(\refcol - \bar\colr) + \cmslope\,\bar\colr\bigr]}_{\text{set by }\refcol}
    + \tfrac{1}{2}F''\,(\colr - \bar\colr)^{2} + \dots ,
  \label{eq:mxiexp}
\end{equation}
with $\cmslope$ and $F''$ evaluated at $\colfn^{-1}(\refcol - \bar\colr)$. Where the curve
is shared --- $\shapeturn$ common, so $F$ is one function for the sample --- the second
bracket is a constant: the reference color shifts $M_{\refcol}$ bodily and contributes
nothing to its dispersion. What survives is the combination $\peakmag - \cmslope\,\colr$,
and the only trace left of $\refcol$ is which value of $\cmslope$ it selects, through the
arclength at which \eqref{eq:Fdef} is read.

Where the curve is not shared it does not cancel. By \eqref{eq:Fdef}
$\partial M_{\refcol}/\partial\refcol = \cmslope$, so moving the reference color by
$\delta\refcol$ injects a dispersion $\delta\refcol$ times the spread of $\cmslope$ over
the sample. \citet{Wang2003} fit a slope per supernova and so must place $\refcol$ near the
sample mean color to hold that term down; the slope dispersion they report in excess of
observational error is the same quantity. In this model that spread is carried by
$\shapeturn$ alone, \eqref{eq:curvlin}.

\subsection{The curvature supplies the evaluation point}
\label{sec:stdmag}

Both defects of \eqref{eq:mxiexp} are the one quantity. The nonlinearity in color is
$F''$, and so is the sensitivity of the surviving coefficient to the reference color,
\begin{equation}
  \pderiv{}{\refcol}\,\cmslope\bigl(\colfn^{-1}(\refcol - \bar\colr)\bigr)
    = \frac{\curv}{(\cos\psi - \sin\psi)^{3}} = F'' ,
  \label{eq:dlam}
\end{equation}
both proportional to $\curv$. Evaluating where the curvature vanishes removes both at
once. Let $\arc^{\star}$ be the first zero of $\curv$ at positive arclength, per
supernova, and define
\begin{equation}
  \lambda^{\star} = \cmslope(\arc^{\star}) , \qquad
  \stdmag = \peakmag - \lambda^{\star}\,\colr .
  \label{eq:stdmag}
\end{equation}
No color is chosen. Supernovae are compared at a common \emph{geometric} feature, the
vanishing of their own curvature, which sits at a different color and a different
arclength for each; a common color is what \eqref{eq:mxi} imposes and what
\eqref{eq:stdmag} does without. At $\arc^{\star}$ the coefficient is stationary against
$\refcol$ by \eqref{eq:dlam} and the quadratic term of \eqref{eq:mxiexp} vanishes, so
\eqref{eq:stdmag} is linear in the color through second order rather than by truncation.

$\lambda^{\star}$ is a color coefficient of the kind \citet{Tripp1998} fits to residuals,
here read off the fitted turning instead. Its per-supernova variation is $\shapeturn$'s, so
a Tripp fit returning one coefficient while \eqref{eq:stdmag} returns a spread is a
disagreement that can be measured.

Dust acts on \eqref{eq:stdmag} through \eqref{eq:mwext}: a constant $A_{\lambda}$
translates, $\peakmag \mapsto \peakmag + A_{g}$ and
$\colr \mapsto \colr + (A_{g} - A_{r})$, so
\begin{equation}
  \stdmag \mapsto \stdmag
    + \bigl[\,1 - \lambda^{\star}/\cmslope_{\mathrm{dust}}\,\bigr]\,A_{g} ,
  \qquad
  \cmslope_{\mathrm{dust}} = \frac{A_{g}}{A_{g} - A_{r}} ,
  \label{eq:dustresp}
\end{equation}
where $\cmslope_{\mathrm{dust}}$ is \eqref{eq:cmslope}'s chart applied to the reddening
direction rather than to the tangent. So \eqref{eq:stdmag} annihilates exactly the
translations whose direction has slope $\lambda^{\star}$, and it is dust-free to first
order if and only if the straight segment of the track is parallel to the reddening
direction: its dust response measures the failure of that parallelism and nothing else.
This is the geometric content of the reduction \citet{Wang2003} report, their $B,V$ slope
near $2$ against a reddening direction near $4$ leaving about half.

\subsection{Statistics with no color in them}
\label{sec:shapescalars}

Arclength is a length in magnitude space and is invariant under the translations of
\eqref{eq:modelmag}, so landmarks of the curve give magnitude-valued statistics that
involve no color, no distance, no time origin and no traversal: the arclength from the
anchor to the $r$ maximum, $\psi = 0$; the arclength between consecutive zeros of $\curv$,
the length of a straight segment; the total turning $\int|\curv|\,\dd\arc$ over the gauge
span, dimensionless. Each is a functional of $\psi(\cdot\,;\shapeturn)$ and therefore a
function of $\shapeturn$ alone, so with $\shapeturn \equiv 0$ each is one number shared by
the sample --- a prediction rather than a measurement. They are the geometric replacements
for $\Delta m_{15}$: shape scalars needing no clock, no reference color and no zero point.

They buy nothing against dust. By \eqref{eq:dustresp} a magnitude functional annihilates
only translations of its own slope, and a functional with no color in it has
$\lambda^{\star} = 0$, which annihilates the direction along which $\magg$ is constant.
Reddening is not that direction, so $\peakmag$ and every color-free statistic built on it
carry the full $A_{g}$. The leverage against dust is the color mixing, and dropping the
reference color is free where dropping the color is not.

\subsection{What this does not establish}
\label{sec:cmagicgaps}

\eqref{eq:stdmag} is an apparent magnitude. The dispersion claim of \citet{Wang2003} is
about absolute magnitudes and needs a distance modulus, which this model does not carry:
$\peakmag$ absorbs $\dm$ by construction, and a standardization is downstream of the fit
and out of sample.

$\lambda^{\star}$ and the zeros of $\curv$ are read off a fitted $\psi$, so whether
$K_{\psi}$ resolves a zero of $\curv$ at all, and whether its location and the slope there
agree across two resolutions, is a measurement and not a property of the model. The
turning basis is expanded in the coordinate $u$ of \eqref{eq:umap}, which concentrates
resolution at the anchor, and $\arc^{\star}$ is not at the anchor.

The dust statement holds for constant $A_{\lambda}$. By \eqref{eq:mwext} the band-averaged
coefficients drift as the source spectrum evolves, and that residual reaches $\curv$,
hence $\lambda^{\star}$.

Nothing above carries a value from $B,V$ to $g,r$. The slope, the arclength of the straight
segment and the colors at its ends are all to be computed in \ztf's own bands.

\section{The scale of the time offset}
\label{app:sdelta}

Section~\ref{sec:inference} writes the time origin as $\tmax = t_{0} + \Delta$ with
$\Delta_{n} = \mu_{\Delta} + s_{\Delta}u_{n}$ over $N$ supernovae, $u_{n}$ standard normal
and $s_{\Delta}$ flat on $s_{\Delta} > 0$. The likelihood reaches this pair only through
$\Delta_{n}$, which leaves a one-parameter redundancy: for any $\lambda > 0$ the map
\begin{equation}
  (s_{\Delta},\, \mathbf{u}) \longmapsto (\lambda s_{\Delta},\, \mathbf{u}/\lambda)
  \label{eq:deltaridge}
\end{equation}
fixes every $\Delta_{n}$, and so the likelihood exactly, while the log prior changes by
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1 - \lambda^{-2}\bigr)\sum_{n} u_{n}^{2} ,
  \label{eq:ridgegain}
\end{equation}
which increases monotonically to $\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n}u_{n}^{2}$. The joint density has no
stationary point along \eqref{eq:deltaridge}: its supremum is at $s_{\Delta} \to \infty$,
$\mathbf{u} \to \mathbf{0}$. At the thirty-supernova posterior $\sum_{n}u_{n}^{2} = 14.6$,
so \eqref{eq:ridgegain} predicts $7.31$ nats against $7.3$ measured by walking
\eqref{eq:deltaridge} out from that posterior, flat to machine precision by
$\lambda = 30$ --- which is where an optimizer stops, returning $s_{\Delta} = 5.48$ against
a posterior $0.186$.

This is a property of the coordinates and not of the posterior. Changing variables from
$\mathbf{u}$ to $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ at fixed $(\mu_{\Delta}, s_{\Delta})$ carries a
Jacobian $s_{\Delta}^{N}$, so the same posterior reads
\begin{equation}
  p(\mu_{\Delta}, s_{\Delta}, \boldsymbol{\Delta}) \propto
  s_{\Delta}^{-N}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2s_{\Delta}^{2}}
  \sum_{n}(\Delta_{n}-\mu_{\Delta})^{2}\Bigr)\,
  \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}) ,
  \label{eq:deltacentred}
\end{equation}
whose derivative in $s_{\Delta}$ vanishes at
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}_{\Delta}^{2} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n}(\Delta_{n}-\mu_{\Delta})^{2} ,
  \label{eq:sdeltahat}
\end{equation}
which is finite. The $s_{\Delta}^{-N}$ that bounds it is the Jacobian that the
$\mathbf{u}$ parameterization carries in its measure instead; the posterior is the same
either way and is proper, the volume of $\mathbf{u}$ holding $\mathcal{L}$ up shrinking as
$\lambda^{-N}$ along \eqref{eq:deltaridge}. A mode is not invariant under
reparameterization where a distribution is, so an optimizer run in $\mathbf{u}$ reports a
divergence the posterior does not have, and the sampler of
Section~\ref{sec:inference} is not affected by it.

It also gives the correction exactly. Moving along \eqref{eq:deltaridge} with
$\lambda = \lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert/\sqrt{N}$ takes $\mathbf{u}$ to unit root-mean-square and
$s_{\Delta}$ to
\begin{equation}
  s_{\Delta}\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n}u_{n}^{2}}
    = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n}(s_{\Delta}u_{n})^{2}} = \hat{s}_{\Delta} ,
  \label{eq:replace}
\end{equation}
so re-placing a mode this way is not a heuristic rescaling: it lands on
\eqref{eq:sdeltahat}, the conditional mode in $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$, and leaves every
$\Delta_{n}$ untouched. That is what makes an optimum usable as a starting point.

\section{A catalogued floor on the offset}
\label{app:t0floor}

$t_{0}$ enters the fit as data through \eqref{eq:modeltime} and \ztf\ DR2 reports an
uncertainty for it per object, which a single fitted $s_{\Delta}$ discards. Writing
$\sigma_{n}$ for that uncertainty,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{n} = \mu_{\Delta} + w_{n}u_{n} , \qquad
  w_{n} = \sqrt{\sigma_{n}^{2} + s_{*}^{2}} ,
  \label{eq:deltafloor}
\end{equation}
lets no object's offset scatter by less than the error already reported for it, and leaves
$s_{*}$ to measure what is left over. The present prior is $\sigma_{n} = 0$ and a
catalogue-only prior is $s_{*} = 0$, so \eqref{eq:deltafloor} contains both. Over the
thirty the catalogued uncertainties have root-mean-square $0.160$~d and range across a
factor of $8.2$, where the fit returns one $s_{\Delta}$ of $0.187$; over the training
sample the root-mean-square is $0.194$~d and the range a factor of a hundred. Equating
$s_{\Delta}^{2} = \overline{\sigma^{2}} + s_{*}^{2}$ on the thirty predicts
$s_{*} \simeq 0.10$~d, so the catalogue accounts for most of the fitted scatter and not
all of it.

What \eqref{eq:deltafloor} does not do is remove \eqref{eq:deltaridge}. Taking
$u_{n}' = u_{n}w_{n}(s_{*})/w_{n}(s_{*}')$ fixes every $\Delta_{n}$ as before, and the log
prior still gains, tending to $\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n}u_{n}^{2}$ as $s_{*}' \to \infty$
because $w_{n} \to s_{*}'$ for every $n$ whatever the $\sigma_{n}$ are. The redundancy
belongs to a free scale multiplying a standard normal and survives any floor; what bounds
it is a proper prior on $s_{*}$, or reading the mode in $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ as above.
The case for \eqref{eq:deltafloor} is therefore that it uses a per-object measurement the
present prior averages over, not that it repairs the geometry. It does bring a
\salt-derived uncertainty into the prior, where $t_{0}$ itself is already in the data.
