\section{Motivation}

The standard empirical treatment of \snia\ light curves, exemplified by \salt\
\citep{Guy2007}, models the spectral energy distribution as a linear expansion about a
mean sequence,
\begin{equation}
  F(p,\lambda) = x_{0}\left[M_{0}(p,\lambda) + x_{1}M_{1}(p,\lambda) + \dots\right]
                 \exp\!\left[\colr\,\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)\right],
\end{equation}
with per-supernova parameters $(x_{0}, x_{1}, \colr, t_{0})$: a description of a
\emph{surface} over phase and wavelength, from which the light curve in any band
follows by integration against a filter response. The parameter $x_{1}$ is read as a
``stretch'', but it does not act as a reparameterization of time --- it sets the
amplitude of a fixed additive component --- so shape and timing are entangled in one
number.

The geometric alternative starts elsewhere. A supernova observed in $n$ bands
supplies a point in $\Rn$ at each epoch, and over the event those points trace a
curve. The curve is independent of when it was sampled, and by the fundamental
theorem of curves it is determined, up to a rigid motion, by $n-1$ scalar invariants
of arclength. Shape then lives entirely in those invariants and timing entirely in
the arclength map, since a reparameterization of time changes how fast a supernova
moves along its curve but does not change the curve.

That separation is exact as a statement about curves, and is not thereby a guarantee about
fits: over a finite, noisy window a change in the traversal and a change in curvature can
mimic one another. Unlike the exact redundancies of Section~\ref{sec:pars}, which
are removed by convention, how far the two can be told apart is an empirical question.


We work in magnitude space, with the Euclidean metric
$\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$. Both are choices, argued at length in
\texttt{docs/model-note-backlog.md}: the space because distance and dust (to first order)
act there as translations, to which curvature is blind; the metric because none is
physically preferred, so one must be declared. The likelihood, by contrast, is evaluated
in native flux (Section~\ref{sec:samples}).

The primary configuration is \ztf\ $g$ and $r$ \citep{Bellm2019}, where $n=2$ and
$\curve = (\Magg, \Magr)$, so focus on $n=2$.

\section{The model}

\subsection{Light curves as traversal over curves in magnitude space}
\label{sec:curve}
The rest-frame $g$ and $r$-band light curves, as a function of phase $\phase$ measured
from $g$ maximum, are denoted as
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\phase)
    = \bigl(\Magg(\phase), \Magr(\phase)\bigr) \in \Rtwo.
  \label{eq:curvedef}
\end{equation}
$\curve$ is a parameterized curve in the $2$-dimensional magnitude space.
The arclength along the curve, $\arc(\phase)$, is defined by
$\dd \arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd \phase}\,\dd \phase$ with the constant of integration
fixed by $\arc = 0$ at $g$ maximum $s(0)=0$; wherever $\norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase} \neq 0$ this is
strictly increasing and invertible.  The curve $\gamma(s)$ is
unit-speed, $\norm{\curve'(\arc)} = 1$.
The curve is defined on a fixed arclength interval $\arc \in [\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$
with $\arc_{\min} < 0 < \arclen$, declared once for the model rather than fitted and wide
enough to cover the modelled phase range for every supernova. A unit-speed curve in $\Rtwo$ admits a Frenet frame,
 \begin{equation}
  \curve' = \tang, \qquad
  \tang' = \curv\,\norml.
  \label{eq:frenet2d}
\end{equation}
with $\tang$ the unit \emph{tangent} and $\norml$ the \emph{normal}, the counterclockwise
$90^{\circ}$ rotation of $\tang$; the \emph{curvature} $\curv$ is correspondingly signed.


We now map out the inverse: how a curve, specified by curvature and three initial conditions,
 and the traversal over that curve 
define light curves.


Given $\curv$, $\curve(\arc)$ is determined through the  differential equations in Eq.~\ref{eq:frenet2d} and
initial conditions for the direction of $\tang$ and the position of $\curve$. Writing the
unit tangent by its angle $\psi$, the equations are solved with
\begin{equation}
  \tang(\arc) = \bigl(\cos\psi(\arc),\ \sin\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \norml(\arc) = \bigl(-\sin\psi(\arc),\ \cos\psi(\arc)\bigr),
  \label{eq:tangangle}
\end{equation}
and $\psi' = \curv$. Curvature is thus the rate of turning of the tangent, and
\eqref{eq:frenet2d} integrates in two quadratures, giving $\psi$ and then the curve,
\begin{align}
  \psi(\arc)   &= \psi_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}\curv(\zeta)\,\dd\zeta ,
                  \label{eq:turning}\\
  \curve(\arc) &= \curve_{0} + \int_{{0}}^{\arc}
                  \bigl(\cos\psi(\zeta),\ \sin\psi(\zeta)\bigr)\,\dd\zeta .
                  \label{eq:quadrature}
\end{align}

When working with light-curve templates, we choose $\curve_{0} = (0,0)$, so that at
$\arc = 0$ both magnitudes, and hence also the color, are zero
($\Magg(0) = \Magr(0) = 0$). Both $\psi_{0} = \pm\pi/2$ make $\arc = 0$ a
critical point of $\Magg$, differing only in whether $r$ is brightening or fading there;
we take $\psi_{0} = -\pi/2$, for which $\Magr'(0) < 0$, since $r$ peaks after $g$.
The extremum is peak brightness, a local minimum of $\Magg$, if
\begin{equation}
  \curv(0) > 0 ,
  \label{eq:peakcond}
\end{equation}
since $\Magg'' = -\sin\psi\,\curv$. We impose no constraint based on it.

The connection to supernova phase remains specified by $s(p)$.

\subsection{Per-supernova light-curve model}
\label{sec:pars}
Having presented our mathematical representation of light curves, in this section we describe the
model used to describe data from the family of Type~Ia supernova light curves.
A supernova is described by six parameters,
$\allpars = (\peakmag,\, \colr,\, \xstretch,\, x_p,\, \shapekappa,\, \tmax)$, entering as
\begin{align}
  \phase(t \mid \tmax) &= \frac{t - \tmax}{1 + \zred} ,
    \label{eq:modeltime}\\
  \begin{pmatrix} \magg \\ \magr \end{pmatrix}\!(\phase \mid \allpars)
    &= \curve\bigl(\arc(\phase;\, \xstretch, x_p);\, \shapekappa\bigr)
       \;+\; \peakmag\,\mathbf{1} \;+\; \colr\,\dustunit .
    \label{eq:modelmag}
\end{align}
The model is read in the direction the data run. An epoch observed at $t$ is carried to
rest-frame phase by \eqref{eq:modeltime}, which involves no modelling functions, then to
arclength by $\arc(\phase)$, and \eqref{eq:modelmag} gives the magnitudes there. It is
$\arc(\phase)$ that is modelled rather than its inverse, so the chain is one of
evaluations and nothing is inverted.
The
basis $\mathbf{1} = (1,1)$, $\dustunit = (0,-1)$ is used to specify the light curve placement in magnitude space.
The parameter pair
$(\peakmag, \colr)$
gives the $g$ magnitude and the $g-r$ color  at $\arc = p = 0$.

The parameterized curves are given by $\curve(\arc;\shapekappa)$, which in turn are
specified by the curvature.  In our model,
the curvature is expanded as
\begin{equation}
  \curv(\arc; \shapekappa) = \curv_{0}(\arc) + \shapekappa\,\curv_{1}(\arc).
  \label{eq:curvlin}
\end{equation}
The traversal $\arc(\phase;\, \xstretch, x_p)$ is expanded through its rate, as a function
of phase,
\begin{equation}
  \deriv{\arc}{\phase}
    = \exp\bigl[\xstretch + q_{0}(\phase) + x_p\,q_{1}(\phase)\bigr] ,
  \qquad
  \arc(\phase) = \int_{0}^{\phase} \deriv{\arc}{\zeta}\,\dd\zeta ,
  \label{eq:travlog}
\end{equation}
so that $\arc(0) = 0$ and $\dd\arc/\dd\phase > 0$ hold identically, for every
$(\xstretch, x_p) \in \Rtwo$ and every basis. Here $q_{0}$ and $q_{1}$ are carried on the
rest-frame phase window $[\phase_{\min}, \phase_{\max}] = [-15, +40]$~d, which unlike the
arclength interval is the same for every supernova by construction; it is the window the
sample is selected on in Section~\ref{sec:samples}. Here $\xstretch$ is a \emph{log} speed: at
$x_p = 0$ the supernova covers $e^{\xstretch}$ times the reference arclength per rest-frame
day, which is the classical dilation of the time axis by $e^{-\xstretch}$.

Table~\ref{tab:pars} summarizes the parameters.
Only $\shapekappa$ affects the curve shape. The others translate it, retime it, or set the
clock.
The redshift $\zred$ is known rather than
fitted, and $\phase$ is rest-frame throughout.

\paragraph{Gauge.} Each expansion fixes only the span of its basis: a change of basis
undone by a compensating change of coefficients alters no prediction. The traversal has
four such freedoms, $q_{1} \mapsto \lambda q_{1} + \mu$ and
$q_{0} \mapsto q_{0} + \alpha + \nu q_{1}$, absorbed by $(\xstretch, x_p)$; the curvature has
two, $\curv_{1} \mapsto \lambda\curv_{1}$ and
$\curv_{0} \mapsto \curv_{0} + \nu\curv_{1}$, absorbed by $\shapekappa$. We remove them on
the functions rather than on the population. The two pairs live on different domains --- the
traversal on the rest-frame phase window, the curvature on the arclength interval --- so
each is normalized on its own. Writing $\langle f,g\rangle$ for $\int fg\,\dd\phase$ over
the phase window when the arguments are $q_{0}, q_{1}$ and for $\int fg\,\dd u$ in the
coordinate $u$ of \eqref{eq:umap} when they are $\curv_{0}, \curv_{1}$, and $\bar f$ for the
corresponding mean,
\begin{equation}
  \bar q_{1} = 0, \quad \langle q_{1},q_{1}\rangle = 1, \quad
  \bar q_{0} = 0, \quad \langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle = 0;
  \qquad
  \langle \curv_{1},\curv_{1}\rangle = 1, \quad
  \langle \curv_{0},\curv_{1}\rangle = 0 ,
  \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
four conditions for four freedoms and two for two. Each pair leaves a sign, fixed by
$\int_{0}^{\phase_{\max}} q_{1} > 0$ and $\int_{0}^{\arclen} \curv_{1} > 0$, so a positive
coefficient speeds the late traversal and turns the track further.


\subsection{Modelling functions}
\label{sec:traversal}

\paragraph{Unresolved.} This section is written as though all four functions share one
domain and one basis, which \eqref{eq:travlog} no longer makes true: $\curv_{0}, \curv_{1}$
are functions of $\arc$ on $[\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$, while $q_{0}, q_{1}$ are functions of
$\phase$ on $[\phase_{\min}, \phase_{\max}]$. The coordinate change below is what lets a
polynomial resolve the turn in $\arc$; expressed in $\phase$ the traversal rate is the speed
itself, bounded and smooth, and needs no such change. So the curvature keeps the map and the
traversal should not, and this section has to be restructured around two bases rather than
one --- including which of the two $K$ counts, and what the parameter count becomes.

Four global functions on $[\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$ carry the template. Together $\curv_{0}$
and $q_{0}$ are the reference \snia: the track through magnitude space, and the rate at
which a supernova walks along it. $\curv_{1}$ and $q_{1}$ are the first mode of departure
from it, split so the two halves cannot be confused --- $\curv_{1}$ moves where the track
goes, $q_{1}$ how fast the supernova covers it. All
four are expansions on a fixed basis, their coefficients fit jointly with the per-supernova
parameters.

The two maxima are close in time, about a day apart in $g$ and $r$, so the track doubles
back on itself and the turn is confined to a very short stretch of arclength. At the median
$x_{1}$ the speed at the turn falls to a ninth of its median, the curvature reaches
$\curv \sim 200$, and $|\phase| < 3$~d holds under two percent of the arclength; at
$x_{1} = -2$ the speed ratio is $260$ and $\curv$ exceeds $10^{3}$. A polynomial in $\arc$
spreads its resolution evenly and cannot meet this: Legendre polynomials in $\arc$ leave
errors of order a magnitude at every $K$ we tried. We therefore expand not in $\arc$ but in
\begin{equation}
  u = \asinh(\arc/\delta), \qquad \delta = 3\times10^{-4},
  \label{eq:umap}
\end{equation}
smooth and strictly increasing, linear for $|\arc| \ll \delta$ and logarithmic beyond, so
that resolution spread evenly in $u$ is resolution in $\arc$ concentrated where the track
turns. Too small a $\delta$ costs little and too large a $\delta$ fails, so it is set from
the sharpest track rather than the average one.

Let $\{\phi_{k}\}$ be the Legendre polynomials in $u$, shifted to
$[u(\arc_{\min}), u(\arclen)]$ and normalized. They meet two of the conditions the
construction needs natively --- orthonormal under the flat measure
$\langle f,g\rangle = \int fg\,\dd u$ that the gauge uses, and $\phi_{0}$ constant. The map
changes the measure the basis is orthonormal in, not that it is orthonormal, so the
coefficient identities below remain exact. Chebyshev polynomials share these properties but
are orthogonal under $(1-x^{2})^{-1/2}$, which concentrates them at the ends of the
interval, where \eqref{eq:umap} has already spent what resolution is needed. Writing
\begin{equation}
  q_{1} = \sum_{k \geq 1} a_{k}\phi_{k}, \quad
  q_{0} = \sum_{k \geq 1} b_{k}\phi_{k}, \quad
  \curv_{1} = \sum_{k \geq 0} c_{k}\phi_{k}, \quad
  \curv_{0} = \sum_{k \geq 0} d_{k}\phi_{k} ,
  \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
the traversal pair omitting $\phi_{0}$, which is what makes $\bar q_{0} = \bar q_{1} = 0$
in \eqref{eq:gauge} hold identically; the curvature pair carries no mean condition and
keeps it. The remaining conditions are then algebra:
$\langle q_{1}, q_{1}\rangle = \norm{a}^{2}$ and $\langle q_{0}, q_{1}\rangle = b \cdot a$
exactly, and likewise for $(c,d)$. Fitting unconstrained $\tilde a, \tilde b$ and setting
$a = \tilde a/\norm{\tilde a}$ and $b = \tilde b - (\tilde b \cdot a)\,a$ imposes
\eqref{eq:gauge} to machine precision; $q_{0}$ is orthogonalized but not normalized, its
norm being physical. The two sign conditions are linear inequalities on $a$ and $c$, fixed
by flipping the raw vector. \salt\ has the same freedom and fixes it the other way, by
normalizing $x_{1}$ over its training set; the difference is that a condition on functions
can be built into how the functions are written, while a condition on a population cannot.

A basis of $K$ functions gives $4K - 2$ coefficients, of which the four conditions of
\eqref{eq:gauge} leave $4K - 6$ free, and truncates the function
space: $K$ is a modelling choice and not only a parameterization, and raising it tests
whether the data want more freedom than it allows. We fit $K \in \{16, 32, 64\}$ and select
on out-of-sample residual, the three spaced so that each step is a real change in
flexibility. The construction degenerates only when $a$ and $b$ become
parallel, which is not a numerical nuisance to suppress but the statement that the two
functions carry the same information.

The map costs the closed-form integral that Legendre polynomials in $\arc$ would have given:
$\dd\arc = \delta\cosh u\,\dd u$, and $\int\phi_{k}\cosh u\,\dd u$ has no elementary
antiderivative, so $\psi$ in \eqref{eq:turning} is a quadrature like the others. Nothing
else changes, because
$\curve$ and $\phase$ never had one either, being $\cos$ and $\exp$ of a basis expansion.
So \eqref{eq:frenet2d} remains a set of quadratures of known integrands rather than an
initial-value problem, all evaluated on shared nodes rather than by stepping, and the gauge
\eqref{eq:gauge} does not touch them. Any such quadrature must carry the measure explicitly:
orthonormalizing sampled columns imposes $L^{2}$ of the sampling density, which on a grid
clustered by \eqref{eq:umap} is not $L^{2}(\dd u)$.

That the clock runs forward is not a condition here but a property of the form. Writing the
traversal through $\log\,\dd\phase/\dd\arc$ admits no coefficients for which phase runs
backwards, and an object whose phase ran backwards partway along its track would not be a
slow supernova but not a supernova.

A supernova will not lie exactly on any member of the six-parameter family, and the
departure is not photometric noise. It is carried by an intrinsic scatter, defined with the
likelihood in Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}.


\section{Data Sample}
\label{sec:samples}

Supernova light curves are taken from  the \ztf\ SN~Ia DR2 archive
\citep{Rigault2025}. Starting from the \Ncatalogued\ cataloged objects, we require
$\zred < 0.05$, both the \texttt{lccoverage} and \texttt{fitquality} flags equal to one,
and the archive's own cosmology classification \texttt{sn\_type == snia-cosmo}, leaving
\Nselected\ supernovae. That last cut is what restricts the sample to normal \sneia: it admits
\texttt{norm}, \texttt{91t} and \texttt{99aa} and no peculiar subtype, while
\texttt{snia-pec} holds the 91bg, Iax, 03fg, 02es, 00cx and 18byg objects whose light-curve
shapes a single $\shapekappa$ could not span alongside normals. Excluding
\texttt{snia-pec} instead would not do: one 91bg-like object is typed \texttt{snia}, its
peculiarity recorded only in \texttt{sub\_type}. Of the \Nselected, \Nnolc\ have no light-curve
file in the release; they are exactly those whose \salt\ $t_{0}$ is NaN, so the
absence is of photometry rather than of shape, and \Nphot\ objects remain with light curves. Epochs are counted per
band inside the rest-frame window $[-15,+40]$~d with signal-to-noise above five and with
none of the quality bits excluded by \texttt{flag \& 31 == 0}, the archive default. 
The analysis uses the \Nanalysis\ supernovae with at least five good epochs in \emph{each} of $g$
and $r$; the count is \Nthree\ at three or more and \Nten\ at ten or more.
Selection and counts are produced by \texttt{dgsn.data.sample}.

Observed-frame $g$ and $r$ are identified with their rest-frame counterparts. A two-band
model carries no spectral energy distribution and so cannot K-correct itself: only the time
axis is de-redshifted, in \eqref{eq:modeltime}. This is the reason for the $\zred < 0.05$
cut, which bounds the band shift rather than removing it. What remains is a systematic,
smooth in $\zred$ and hence partly absorbed by $\peakmag$ and $\colr$; it is quantified
after the fit against an external SED, never corrected for within it.

Counting epochs inside $[-15,+40]$~d requires a date of
maximum, and the numbers above use the archive's \salt\ $t_{0}$ to place the window.
For the fit,
$\tmax$ is compared with $t_{0}$ and the analysis repeated with one substituted for the
other. For the selection, the sample is
recounted against an SED-free anchor --- a window on the brightest good $g$ epoch --- and
the two samples compared by \emph{membership}: equal counts can hide different objects, so
the symmetric difference is what to report. The \Nnolc\ selected objects with NaN $t_{0}$
need neither, having no photometry to place a window on.

The arclength interval of Section~\ref{sec:curve} is declared from the sample rather than
assumed. Smoothing each well-observed light curve in flux and integrating
$\dd\arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase}\,\dd\phase$ outward from the $g$ maximum gives, over
the $[-15,+40]$~d window, a median span of $[-4.9, +3.9]$ mag with fifth and ninety-fifth
percentiles at $-8.9$ and $+7.4$. The measurement is biased high, since noise lengthens a
path, and moves by $0.7$ mag across a factor of ten in smoothing; the \citet{Hsiao2007}
template, having no noise, gives $[-3.2, +3.3]$. We declare
$[\arc_{\min}, \arclen] = [-8, +8]$, wide enough that every supernova's window maps inside
it, and verify after fitting that it does --- the fitted traversal gives
$\arc(\phase_{\min})$ and $\arc(\phase_{\max})$ per object, and both must fall inside
$[\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$. Width is not free, the basis spending resolution where no
supernova reaches, so that check is also how the interval is tightened.

Supernova light curves are not corrected for Galactic dust.
To first order, such corrections would appear in the form
\begin{equation}
  m_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{corr}} = m_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{obs}} - A_{\lambda},
  \qquad A_{\lambda} = R_{\lambda}\,E(B-V) ,
  \label{eq:mwext}
\end{equation}
with $R_{\lambda}$ from \citet{Schlafly2011} assuming a \citet{Fitzpatrick1999} law at
$R_{V} = 3.1$, and $E(B-V)$ the \citet{Schlegel1998} value at the supernova's coordinates,
tabulated per object as \texttt{mwebv}. A constant per band is a translation in magnitude
space, and $\mathbf{1}$ and $\dustunit$ span $\Rtwo$, so it is absorbed exactly by the
placement: $\peakmag \mapsto \peakmag + A_{g}$ and
$\colr \mapsto \colr + (A_{g} - A_{r})$, leaving the curve and hence $\curv$ untouched.
Not correcting therefore costs nothing in the fit, and means instead that $\peakmag$ and
$\colr$ carry the Galactic term, to be removed downstream where \texttt{mwebv} is known
per object. The absorption is exact only for constant $A_{\lambda}$: the band-averaged
extinction depends on a source spectrum that evolves, so $R_{\lambda}$ drifts across the
window by a few percent, and that residual does reach $\curv$ --- not negligibly, since
$E(B-V)$ exceeds $0.1$ for $23\%$ of the sample and reaches $1.06$.

\section{Likelihood}
\label{sec:likelihood}

The model is built in magnitude space and confronted with the data in flux. Magnitude space
is where distance and reddening act as translations, to which $\curv$ is blind; flux is
where the \ztf\ errors are Gaussian and where a non-detection is an ordinary measurement
near zero rather than a missing one.

For an epoch $i$ observed at $t_{i}$ in band $b_{i}$, \eqref{eq:modeltime} gives the
rest-frame phase $\phase_{i}$ and \eqref{eq:travlog} the arclength
$\arc_{i} = \arc(\phase_{i})$, both by evaluation; no inversion enters the forward model.
The predicted flux is
\begin{equation}
  \hat f_{i}(\allpars)
    = 10^{-0.4\,[\,m_{b_{i}}(\phase_{i} \mid \allpars)\, -\, \zp\,]} ,
  \label{eq:predflux}
\end{equation}
with $\zp = 30$, the archive's zero point. It is pinned, not fitted: $\zp$ and $\peakmag$
are exactly degenerate in \eqref{eq:modelmag} and \eqref{eq:predflux}, and this choice is
what makes $\peakmag$ the $g$ magnitude on the archive's own scale.
The epoch set is fixed before fitting. The window is placed by the archive's $t_{0}$ and
$\tmax$ is free within it; anchoring instead on the fitted $\tmax$ would change which
epochs enter as the sampler moves, and since each contributes $\ln\sigma_{i}^{2} \simeq 11$
to \eqref{eq:like} whatever its residual, admitting or dropping one shifts the objective by
more than a typical epoch fits it. Epochs are those inside the window passing
\texttt{flag \& 31 == 0}, the archive's quality mask; its first bit marks
$\sigma_{f,i} = 0$, without which \eqref{eq:like} is undefined for six epochs of this
sample. Within that set every epoch contributes, non-detections and negative fluxes
included:
\begin{equation}
  -2\ln\mathcal{L}
    = \sum_{i}\left[\frac{\bigl(f_{i} - \hat f_{i}\bigr)^{2}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}
      + \ln\sigma_{i}^{2}\right], \qquad
  \sigma_{i}^{2} = \sigma_{f,i}^{2}
    + \bigl(0.4\ln 10\ \sigma_{b_{i}}\,\hat f_{i}\bigr)^{2} ,
  \label{eq:like}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{f,i}$ the archive's flux error, which already carries \texttt{err\_scale};
\texttt{flux} already has \texttt{flux\_offset} subtracted. A low-signal epoch weights itself down
correctly, whereas discarding one would select on the noise realization of the very
quantity being fitted. The variance depends on $\allpars$, hence the log-determinant term.

Epochs are selected inside the phase window, but $\tmax$ is free, so an excursion can carry
one outside it during sampling. Dropping it would make the objective discontinuous, so the
map is softened instead. The clamp acts on phase, whose window is fixed and the same for
every supernova: with
$w_{i} = [\phase_{i} - \phase_{\min}]/[\phase_{\max} - \phase_{\min}]$,
$\mathrm{sp}(x) = \epsilon\log(1 + e^{x/\epsilon})$ and $\epsilon = 0.01$, one percent of
that range, the phase is soft-clamped and the flux tapered,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\phase_{i} = \phase_{\min} + (\phase_{\max} - \phase_{\min})
    \bigl[\mathrm{sp}(w_{i}) - \mathrm{sp}(w_{i}-1)\bigr] ,
  \qquad
  \hat f_{i} \;\longmapsto\; \hat f_{i}\,
    \exp\!\bigl[-\bigl(\mathrm{sp}(-w_{i}) + \mathrm{sp}(w_{i}-1)\bigr)^{2}/\epsilon^{2}\bigr] .
  \label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
Both are $C^{\infty}$ in $\allpars$, agree with the model to $O(\epsilon)$ for
$w_{i} \in [0,1]$, and send the predicted flux to zero outside. An epoch carried out of
range then contributes $f_{i}^{2}/\sigma_{i}^{2}$: free if it was consistent with zero,
expensive if it was a detection, which is the pushback wanted. The arclength needs the same
treatment for the same reason --- $\arclen$ bounds $\curv$, and $\xstretch$ can carry
$\arc(\phase_{\max})$ past it --- and takes the same softplus, in $\arc$ on
$[\arc_{\min}, \arclen]$.

The second variance term is the intrinsic scatter. Grey offsets, color shifts and time
shifts are already free in $\peakmag$, $\colr$ and $\tmax$, so what it carries is departure
in \emph{shape}; it is written as a fractional flux error because that departure is a
magnitude, and scaled by $\hat f_{i}$ rather than $f_{i}$ so a downward fluctuation does not
earn itself a smaller weight. Without it the global functions follow whichever supernovae
have the smallest photometric errors, the per-supernova uncertainties come out too small,
and no model comparison is trustworthy --- including the two this model exists to make. Two
scalars $\sigma_{g}, \sigma_{r}$ are deliberately less than the scatter deserves: one
flexible enough to follow its phase dependence is also flexible enough to absorb
$\shapekappa$, and that $\sigma$ does not inflate when $\shapekappa$ is removed is the check
that it has not.


\section{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

The posterior is sampled with the no-U-turn Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampler
\citep{Hoffman2011} in \texttt{cmdstan} \citep{Carpenter2017}. Global coefficients, per-supernova parameters and $\sigma_{g}, \sigma_{r}$
are sampled jointly: $4K$ coefficients, six parameters for each of the \Nanalysis\ supernovae, and
two scalars. Nothing is profiled or held at a point estimate. The per-supernova parameters
are incidental and grow in number with the sample, so a fit that maximized over them would
return $\sigma_{g}, \sigma_{r}$ biased low \citep{Neyman1948}; marginalizing over them does
not.

The gauge is imposed by construction rather than sampled. The sampler moves on
unconstrained raw coefficients $(\tilde a, \tilde b, \tilde c, \tilde d)$, and
$a = \tilde a/\norm{\tilde a}$, $b = \tilde b - (\tilde b \cdot a)a$ and their curvature
counterparts are deterministic transforms of them, so \eqref{eq:gauge} holds at every
iteration and no constraint enters the likelihood. Priors are standard normal on the raw
coefficients and on $(\xstretch, x_p, \shapekappa)$ --- the gauge fixes the scale of the
latent parameters, which is what makes a unit normal a statement rather than an arbitrary
width --- half-normal on $\sigma_{g}$ and $\sigma_{r}$, and normal on $\tmax$ centered on
the archive's $t_{0}$ with a width of one day. $\peakmag$ and $\colr$ are given wide normal
priors, being placement rather than shape.

Chains are started from the archive's \salt\ fit: $\tmax$ at $t_{0}$, $\peakmag$ and
$\colr$ at the magnitude and color it implies, shape parameters at zero, and the global
coefficients at a least-squares fit to the mean template. Initialization is not use of
\salt\ as a prior --- it sets where the chains begin and not what they converge to, and the
run is only accepted if chains from dispersed starts agree. Convergence requires
$\hat R < 1.01$ and effective sample size above $400$ on every parameter, with no divergent
transitions.

$K$ is selected as in Section~\ref{sec:traversal}, by residual on supernovae held out of
the fit; in-sample residual falls with $K$ by construction and ranks nothing.
