\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

The standard empirical treatment of \snia\ light curves, exemplified by \salt,
models the spectral energy distribution as a linear expansion about a mean
sequence,
\begin{equation}
  F(p,\lambda) = x_{0}\left[M_{0}(p,\lambda) + x_{1}M_{1}(p,\lambda) + \dots\right]
                 \exp\!\left[\colr\,\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)\right],
  \label{eq:salt2}
\end{equation}
with per-supernova parameters $(x_{0}, x_{1}, \colr, t_{0})$: a description of a
\emph{surface} over phase and wavelength, from which the light curve in any band
follows by integration against a filter response. The parameter $x_{1}$ is read as a
``stretch'', but it does not act as a reparameterisation of time --- it sets the
amplitude of a fixed additive component --- so shape and timing are entangled in one
number.

The geometric alternative starts elsewhere. A supernova observed in $n$ bands
supplies a point in $\Rn$ at each epoch, and over the event those points trace a
curve. The curve is independent of when it was sampled, and by the fundamental
theorem of curves it is determined, up to a rigid motion, by $n-1$ scalar invariants
of arclength. Shape then lives entirely in those invariants and timing entirely in
the arclength map, since a reparameterisation of time changes how fast a supernova
moves along its curve but does not change the curve.

That separation is exact as a statement about curves; it is not a guarantee about
fits. Over a finite window with noisy sampling, a warp in the arclength map and a
change in curvature can mimic one another, so the separation may fail in practice
even though it holds identically in principle. Section~\ref{sec:gauge} gives the
degeneracy and its diagnostic; a large measured correlation is a negative result to
report, not to absorb.

\section{The geometric object}

\subsection{The curve in band space}

Let a supernova be observed in $n$ photometric bands. Write its magnitude vector
as a function of rest-frame phase $\phase$,
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\phase) = \bigl(m_{1}(\phase), \dots, m_{n}(\phase)\bigr) \in \Rn .
\end{equation}
The $m_{X}$ are magnitudes in the \emph{observed} bands, corrected for Galactic
extinction but not K-corrected; only the phase argument is rest-frame, through
$\phase = (t - \tmax)/(1+\zred)$. The distinction matters and is not pedantry:
recasting the $m_{X}$ as rest-frame quantities would require an SED, which this
model does not have, and the $\zred < 0.05$ cut exists precisely to keep the
resulting mismatch small rather than to remove it
(Sections~\ref{sec:samples} and~\ref{sec:limitations}).

The primary configuration of this project is \ztf\ $g$ and $r$
\citep{Bellm2019}, so $n=2$ and $\curve = (\magg, \magr)$. Direct inspection of
the \ztf\ SN~Ia DR2 archive \citep{Rigault2025} shows that a subset of the
low-redshift sample also carries usable $i$-band photometry, giving a second
configuration with $n=3$ on fewer objects. Section~\ref{sec:samples} quantifies
both. The implementation is written for general $n$ for this reason, not as
speculative generality.

\subsection{Arclength and the generalized Frenet frame}

Reparameterise by arclength $\arc$, defined by
$\dd \arc = \norm{\dd\curve/\dd \phase}\,\dd \phase$, so that
$\norm{\curve'(\arc)} = 1$. A unit-speed curve in $\Rn$ that is sufficiently
non-degenerate admits a Frenet frame $\frenet{1},\dots,\frenet{n}$ obeying
\begin{align}
  \frenet{1}' &= \curv_{1}\frenet{2}, \nonumber\\
  \frenet{i}' &= -\curv_{i-1}\frenet{i-1} + \curv_{i}\frenet{i+1},
                 \qquad 1 < i < n, \label{eq:frenet}\\
  \frenet{n}' &= -\curv_{n-1}\frenet{n-1}, \nonumber
\end{align}
together with $\curve' = \frenet{1}$. The $n-1$ functions
$\curv_{1}(\arc),\dots,\curv_{n-1}(\arc)$ determine the curve up to a rigid
motion of $\Rn$.

For $n=2$ this collapses to a single invariant, the signed planar curvature,
\begin{equation}
  \curve' = \tang, \qquad
  \tang' = \curv\,\norml, \qquad
  \norml' = -\curv\,\tang .
  \label{eq:frenet2d}
\end{equation}
For $n=3$ a second invariant appears, the torsion $\tors = \curv_{2}$, which
measures departure from the instantaneous osculating plane. With two bands
there is no torsion; this is a real limitation of the \ztf\ public
configuration and is recorded in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Why the curve lives in magnitude space}
\label{sec:magspace}

The case for magnitude space is a physical one. Several of the quantities that
stand between an observed \snia\ and its intrinsic behaviour are, in magnitudes,
simply \emph{added} to the photometry --- and curvature does not respond to an
added constant. Adding a constant slides the curve bodily through the plane
without bending it, and $\curv$ measures only bending.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Physical effect & Acts on the curve as & Removed? \\
\midrule
Luminosity distance        & rigid displacement, all bands alike & exactly \\
Absolute luminosity        & rigid displacement, all bands alike & exactly \\
Peculiar velocity          & rigid displacement, all bands alike & exactly \\
Per-band calibration error & rigid displacement, per band        & exactly \\
Time dilation, stretch     & reparameterisation, no displacement & exactly \\
\midrule
Host and Galactic dust     & deformation: rigid part $+$ drift   & rigid part only \\
Intrinsic diversity \emph{(signal)} & deformation                & no, and must not be \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The horizontal rule in that table is the content of this section. Above it sit
effects the geometry disposes of for nothing; below it sit effects it cannot touch,
one of which is the signal.

\paragraph{The first variation of $\curv$ makes the rule precise.} Let the curve be
displaced by a vector field, decomposed in the Frenet frame,
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\arc) \mapsto \curve(\arc) + \delta\curve(\arc),
  \qquad
  \delta\curve = \varphi\,\tang + \psi\,\norml .
  \label{eq:pertdecomp}
\end{equation}
For a plane curve the induced change in the signed curvature is, to first order,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\curv = \psi'' + \curv^{2}\psi + \curv'\varphi ,
  \label{eq:firstvar}
\end{equation}
where $'$ denotes $\dd/\dd\arc$. Now let the displacement be a \emph{constant} vector
$\mathbf{v}$, so that $\varphi = \tang\cdot\mathbf{v}$ and
$\psi = \norml\cdot\mathbf{v}$. Then $\psi' = \norml'\cdot\mathbf{v} = -\curv\varphi$
and $\varphi' = \tang'\cdot\mathbf{v} = \curv\psi$ by \eqref{eq:frenet2d}, so
$\psi'' = -\curv'\varphi - \curv^{2}\psi$ and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\curv = \bigl(-\curv'\varphi - \curv^{2}\psi\bigr) + \curv^{2}\psi
                + \curv'\varphi = 0
  \label{eq:transinv}
\end{equation}
identically, for every curve and every constant $\mathbf{v}$. This is the row group
above the rule, and \eqref{eq:transinv} is the whole of its content: what matters is
that $\mathbf{v}$ be independent of $\arc$, not that it be achromatic. A zeropoint
error differs in every band, is strongly chromatic, and is still annihilated by
\eqref{eq:firstvar}.

Conversely, nothing in \eqref{eq:firstvar} vanishes when $\delta\curve$ depends on
$\arc$. Dust is such a displacement, because the absorption a broad filter delivers
depends on the spectrum inside it and that spectrum evolves
(Section~\ref{sec:phasedust}); so is intrinsic diversity, which is the signal
$\curv(\arc;\shape)$ exists to describe. Dust and signal therefore enter $\curv$
through the same channel, and no choice of ambient space or of invariant separates
them --- separation can come only from population-level assumptions, stated as such
in Sections~\ref{sec:phasedust} and~\ref{sec:gauge}. What magnitude space buys is
narrower and still decisive: by \eqref{eq:transinv} the quantity being inferred is
\emph{exactly} orthogonal to shape, which is why $\dm$ may be free per supernova and
absorb peculiar velocity exactly (Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}). Being the same type
as the signal is not being the same size; Section~\ref{sec:phasedust} bounds the size
using \eqref{eq:firstvar} itself.

Equation~\eqref{eq:transinv} also bounds what the geometry can deliver: $\curv$
is insensitive to every constant offset, so it carries no information about
absolute magnitude or absolute colour, only about their evolution. These must be
supplied separately --- by $\dm$, by the globally fixed initial point $\curve(0)$,
and by $\colr$ in rung L2c.

\subsubsection*{Distance and dust in flux space}

In flux the same effects are multiplicative, and multiplication does not preserve
shape. Distance is an isotropic dilation $f \mapsto af$, giving $\arc \mapsto a\arc$
and $\curv \mapsto \curv/a$: a photographic enlargement, so shape survives but
$\curv$ does not, and the damage is repairable in principle by a scale-invariant
combination such as $\arclen\,\curv(\arc/\arclen)$. Dust is an \emph{anisotropic}
dilation,
\begin{equation}
  (\fluxg, \fluxr) \mapsto
  \bigl(\fluxg\,10^{-0.4 A_{g}},\ \fluxr\,10^{-0.4 A_{r}}\bigr),
  \qquad A_{g} \neq A_{r},
\end{equation}
which takes a circle to an ellipse and constant curvature to non-constant. No
rescaling or reparameterisation recovers it. Dust, not distance, settles the choice
of space.

The curve is therefore defined in magnitude space --- independently of the space in
which the likelihood is evaluated, which is native flux
(Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}), for reasons of measurement error that have nothing
to do with geometry.

\subsubsection*{Remark: the invariance in precise terms}
\label{sec:magspacemath}

The two constant displacements singled out by the astrophysics are
\begin{align}
  \text{distance, luminosity, peculiar velocity:} \quad
    &\curve \mapsto \curve + \dm\,(1,1), \label{eq:transdist}\\
  \text{reddening (idealised as constant):} \quad
    &\curve \mapsto \curve + \colr\,(1,-1), \label{eq:transcol}
\end{align}
the first achromatic and the second pure colour, and both annihilated by
\eqref{eq:transinv}. The normalisation is such that $\dm$ is the offset added to
\emph{each} band, as in the forward model \eqref{eq:forward}; the unit directions are
$(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ and $(1,-1)/\sqrt{2}$ and the displacement lengths $\dm\sqrt{2}$,
$\colr\sqrt{2}$. Nothing depends on the convention, but a stray $\sqrt{2}$ between
length and per-band offset would propagate into the absolute-magnitude offset of
\eqref{eq:tripp}.

Two facts sharpen \eqref{eq:transinv} beyond first order. Translations are
isometries, $\curv$ is invariant under isometries of the ambient space, and --- among
maps of that space --- under nothing else, a rigidity theorem: a diffeomorphism
preserving the curvature of every curve is necessarily an isometry. This converse is
what makes the flux-space comparison a derivation rather than a preference. Two
qualifications: the signed curvature of \eqref{eq:frenet2d} picks up $\det Q$ under
an isometry of linear part $Q$, so the invariance group is $\SE(n)$ and only
$\abs{\curv}$ is reflection-invariant; and the converse concerns maps of the ambient
space only, so reparameterisation --- which leaves $\curv(\arc)$ unchanged without
moving the image at all --- is no counterexample.

Finally, curvature has dimensions of inverse length and so presupposes a metric on
magnitude space, of which none is physically preferred: nothing dictates how one
magnitude of $g$ compares in length to one magnitude of $r$. Normalising the
directions of \eqref{eq:transdist} and \eqref{eq:transcol} by $\sqrt{2}$, and
calling them orthogonal, presumes the Euclidean form $\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$. The argument of this
section is untouched by the choice, because translations are isometries of every
translation-invariant metric; the numerical values of $\curv$ are not, since
rescaling $\magr \mapsto 2\magr$ alters every curvature while altering nothing
physical. The metric is therefore a gauge convention, recorded with the others in
Section~\ref{sec:gauge}.

\subsection{Dust extinction is phase-dependent, so \eqref{eq:transcol} is only
approximate}
\label{sec:phasedust}

Equation~\eqref{eq:transcol} treats reddening as a fixed vector. It is not.
Broadband extinction is a flux-weighted average of the monochromatic law over the
filter,
\begin{equation}
  A_{X}(\phase) = -2.5\log_{10}
    \frac{\int S_{X}(\lambda)\,F(\lambda,\phase)\,10^{-0.4A(\lambda)}\,\dd\lambda}
         {\int S_{X}(\lambda)\,F(\lambda,\phase)\,\dd\lambda},
  \label{eq:bandext}
\end{equation}
and the weights are the supernova's own spectral energy distribution
$F(\lambda,\phase)$, which evolves strongly over the event
\citep{Nugent2002}. As the \snia\ reddens intrinsically, the flux-weighted
effective wavelength within each filter shifts, so the extinction suffered per
unit reddening drifts with phase. Both the magnitude and the direction of the
displacement change. Dust therefore acts not as a translation but as a
\emph{phase-dependent displacement},
\begin{equation}
  \curve(\arc) \mapsto \curve(\arc) + \colr\,\dustdir(\arc),
  \qquad \dustdir(\arc) \ \text{a fixed vector-valued function, not a constant},
  \label{eq:dustcurve}
\end{equation}
which is not an isometry and does change both $\curv$ and arclength.

\paragraph{Order counting: the shape contamination is second order.} Split the
displacement into its phase-average and the variation about it,
\begin{equation}
  \colr\,\dustdir(\arc)
    = \underbrace{\colr\,\bar{\dustdir}}_{\text{translation}}
    + \underbrace{\colr\,\delta\dustdir(\arc)}_{\text{deformation}},
  \qquad
  \bar{\dustdir} = \langle \dustdir \rangle,
  \quad \langle \delta\dustdir \rangle = 0 .
  \label{eq:dustsplit}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:dustsplit} into \eqref{eq:firstvar} and using
\eqref{eq:transinv} on the constant part, the induced curvature change is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\curv(\arc) = \colr\Bigl[
      \bigl(\norml\cdot\delta\dustdir\bigr)''
    + \curv^{2}\bigl(\norml\cdot\delta\dustdir\bigr)
    + \curv'\bigl(\tang\cdot\delta\dustdir\bigr)\Bigr] ,
  \label{eq:dkappa}
\end{equation}
in which $\bar{\dustdir}$ does not appear at all. Writing
$\varepsilon = \norm{\delta\dustdir}/\norm{\bar{\dustdir}}$ for the fractional phase
variation of the reddening displacement and $\ell$ for the arclength scale over which
it varies, \eqref{eq:dkappa} gives
\begin{equation}
  \abs{\Delta\curv} = \mathcal{O}\!\left(
    \colr\,\varepsilon\,\norm{\bar{\dustdir}}\,
    \max\!\bigl(\ell^{-2},\,\curv^{2}\bigr)\right),
  \label{eq:orders}
\end{equation}
so the contamination of shape is small twice over: once in the reddening amplitude
$\colr$, and once in $\varepsilon$, which is small because $A_{X}$ in
\eqref{eq:bandext} responds only weakly to SED evolution inside a broad filter: the
flux-weighted effective wavelength shifts by a small fraction of the filter width.
Intrinsic diversity, by contrast, enters $\curv$ at first order, being what
$\curv(\arc;\shape)$ parameterises. The expectation is thus a \textbf{hierarchy} ---
the phase-dependent variance the geometry sees should be dominated by intrinsic
supernova dispersion --- and it is an expectation to be measured, not assumed.

Two limits on the argument. It is amplitude-dependent through the $\colr$ in
\eqref{eq:dkappa}, so it degrades for the reddest objects and every diagnostic below
is reported \emph{stratified by} fitted $\colr$ rather than sample-averaged. And it
covers the \emph{shape} channel only: in the colour channel $\colr\,\bar{\dustdir}$
and a per-supernova intrinsic colour offset are the same displacement, so they mix at
first order, exactly as \salt's $\colr$ does. That degeneracy is not resolved here.

Rung L2c accordingly fits $\colr$ as the amplitude of the fixed function
$\dustdir(\arc)$ of \eqref{eq:dustcurve} --- precomputed once from an extinction law
and a template SED --- rather than of a constant vector, which by
\eqref{eq:dustsplit} would remove $\colr\,\bar{\dustdir}$ and leave
$\colr\,\delta\dustdir$ free to be absorbed by $\shape$. The parameter count is
unchanged.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} Three numbers, in increasing directness.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\mathrm{Corr}(\colr, \shape)$ over the population, computed both with and
        without the identifying condition of Section~\ref{sec:gauge} imposed. A large
        unconstrained value falsifies \eqref{eq:orders}.
  \item The error-weighted overlap of the dust direction with the shape directions at
        the epochs actually observed. Let $\mathbf{d}$ stack
        $\partial f_{X}/\partial\colr$ over observed epochs, $J$ have columns
        $\partial f_{X}/\partial\shapeone$, $\partial f_{X}/\partial\shapetwo$,
        $W = \mathrm{diag}(1/\sigma_{i}^{2})$, and
        $P = J(J^{\!\top} W J)^{-1}J^{\!\top}W$. Then
        \begin{equation}
          \rho^{2} = \frac{\mathbf{d}^{\!\top} W P\, \mathbf{d}}
                          {\mathbf{d}^{\!\top} W \mathbf{d}} \in [0,1]
          \label{eq:overlap}
        \end{equation}
        is the fraction of the dust signal this cadence and these error bars cannot
        distinguish from shape. Two details decide whether it is meaningful.
        It is built in \emph{flux}, where the errors are Gaussian, so
        $\partial f_{X}/\partial\shape = -0.4\ln\!10\,f_{X}\,
        \partial m_{X}/\partial\shape$; and $\mathbf{d}$ is formed from
        $\delta\dustdir$, not $\dustdir$, since by \eqref{eq:transinv} the mean part
        cannot be confused with shape and including it would inflate $\rho^{2}$.
  \item $\Delta\curv$ from \eqref{eq:dkappa}, with $\dustdir$ built by evaluating
        \eqref{eq:bandext} on a grid of $E(B-V)$ and $R_{V}$, against the range of
        $\curv$ the network spans over fitted $\shape$. This tests
        \eqref{eq:orders} directly. Small at every $\colr$ licenses the
        interpretation of $\shape$; order unity at any $\colr$ means the shape
        latents are substantially measuring dust there --- a negative result for
        interpretation, to be reported as one, and not an obstacle to the
        Hubble-diagram result, for which the causes need not be separated.
\end{enumerate}

An external SED template is used here only to quantify a systematic, never to fit,
initialise, or K-correct, so the independence from \salt\ of
Section~\ref{sec:ladder} is preserved. The discussion behind several points
compressed above --- the tolerability of the mixing for the distance indicator, the
status of the identifying assumption, the cost of holding $\dustdir$ fixed, and why
$\zred < 0.05$ does not help with dust --- is held in
\texttt{docs/model-note-backlog.md} until this note stabilises.

\section{The model}

\subsection{Curvature network}

The curvature invariants are represented by a neural network conditioned on
per-supernova latent parameters,
\begin{equation}
  \curv_{i}(\arc; \shape) = \NN_{i}\bigl(\arc, \shapeone, \shapetwo\bigr),
  \qquad i = 1,\dots,n-1 .
  \label{eq:kappanet}
\end{equation}
The network replaces the B-spline basis of \salt\ with a smooth, unconstrained
function approximator. Conditioning is full rather than linear: $\shape$ enters
as an input, not as coefficients multiplying fixed basis functions, so
nonlinear interactions between shape and phase are learned rather than imposed.
The cost of this choice is that $\shapeone$ and $\shapetwo$ do not carry an
a~priori interpretation as ``amount of component one''; their meaning must be
established after training by correlating them against observables.

A supernova's curve is obtained by integrating \eqref{eq:frenet} from a
\emph{globally fixed} initial point and initial frame. The only per-supernova
\emph{motion of the ambient space} is the translation $\dm$. The optional colour
parameter $\colr$ of Section~\ref{sec:ladder} is not a motion of the space at
all: it scales the phase-dependent displacement $\dustdir(\arc)$ of
\eqref{eq:dustcurve}, which moves different points of the curve by different
amounts and therefore acts on the curve as a deformation rather than a
translation (Section~\ref{sec:phasedust}). Fixing the frame is not an assumption about
supernovae; it is a gauge choice, justified in
Section~\ref{sec:rotation}, where it is also shown that no per-supernova
rotation should be fitted because no physical effect acts as one.

\subsection{Time-to-arclength map}
\label{sec:reparam}

Photometry is recorded in observer-frame MJD, whereas the model curve is a
rest-frame object. Observer-frame time intervals must therefore be divided by
$1+\zred$ before anything else happens. Rest-frame phase is
\begin{equation}
  \phase = \frac{t - \tmax}{1 + \zred},
  \label{eq:phase}
\end{equation}
with $t$ and $\tmax$ both observer-frame MJD. Since $\zred$ is measured for
every object, the $1+\zred$ factor is a \emph{known} kinematic rescaling and is
divided out deterministically rather than fitted. This keeps $\tscale$ below a
genuine rest-frame stretch instead of a repackaging of time dilation, and it is
the same logic applied to \texttt{mwebv} in Section~\ref{sec:samples}: known
effects are removed, not absorbed into free parameters.

Timing is then a monotone map from rest-frame phase to arclength. Writing
$u = \phase/\tscale$ with $\tscale$ in rest-frame days,
\begin{equation}
  \arc(t) = u\left[1 + \warpone u + \warptwo u^{2}\right],
  \qquad
  u = \frac{t - \tmax}{\tscale\,(1+\zred)},
  \qquad \deriv{\arc}{t} > 0 \ \text{on the fit window}.
  \label{eq:reparam}
\end{equation}
Setting $\warpone = \warptwo = 0$ recovers the affine map $\arc = \phase/\tscale$,
which is the direct analogue of classical stretch \citep{Phillips1993}. The
quadratic and cubic terms permit asymmetric warping between rise and decline.
The positivity constraint is definitional, not regulatory: $\arc$ is arclength,
so $\dd \arc/\dd t = \norm{\dot{\curve}}$ is strictly positive wherever the
supernova is moving.

\paragraph{$\tmax$ is free, and the gauge makes it the $g$-maximum.} The date of
maximum is a fitted parameter for every supernova; it is never taken from the
archive's \salt\ $t_{0}$, which would violate the requirement that \salt\ stay
strictly downstream. Nothing needs to be added to enforce its interpretation.
By construction $\arc(\tmax) = 0$, and the initial condition of
Section~\ref{sec:rotation} places the template's $g$-band maximum at $\arc = 0$
for every value of $\shape$. Because $\dd\arc/\dd t > 0$,
\begin{equation}
  \deriv{\magg}{t} = \deriv{\magg}{\arc}\,\deriv{\arc}{t}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \deriv{\magg}{t} = 0 \iff \deriv{\magg}{\arc} = 0 ,
\end{equation}
so the fitted $\tmax$ \emph{is} the observer-frame epoch of $g$-band maximum.
The identification is enforced by the gauge choice rather than imposed as a
penalty or recovered after the fit.

\paragraph{The anchor band is $g$, not $B$.} Phase zero is conventionally set at
rest-frame $B$-band maximum, but locating $B$ maximum requires K-correcting into
a filter that was not observed, hence an SED, which a two-band model does not
have (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The anchor here is the maximum of
observed \ztf\ $g$, a band actually in hand. Since $g$ and $B$ are different
filters, $\tmax$ and a $B$-maximum epoch need not coincide, and the offset
between them is not predictable from effective wavelength: peak epoch is not a
monotonic function of wavelength across UV to NIR. When \salt\ is used as an
external benchmark, the distribution of $\tmax - t_{0}$ must therefore be
\emph{measured} and reported --- neither its centre nor its sign assumed --- and
its scatter, not its mean, is the check on timing recovery.

\subsection{Per-supernova parameters}

The baseline model assigns seven parameters to each supernova:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Symbol & Role & Geometric character \\
\midrule
$\dm$        & normalization & translation along $(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ \\
$\shapeone$  & shape         & conditions $\curv$ \\
$\shapetwo$  & shape         & conditions $\curv$ \\
$\tscale$    & timing scale (rest-frame d) & reparameterisation \\
$\tmax$      & time origin (observer MJD)  & reparameterisation \\
$\warpone$   & timing warp   & reparameterisation \\
$\warptwo$   & timing warp   & reparameterisation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For comparison, \salt\ uses four: $(x_{0}, x_{1}, \colr, t_{0})$. The excess is
deliberate and is the subject of the model ladder in
Section~\ref{sec:ladder}; it is not a claim that seven parameters are needed.

\subsection{Forward model and likelihood}
\label{sec:likelihood}

Given latents, the predicted magnitude in band $X$ at observer-frame epoch $t$
is, with $\arc(t)$ from \eqref{eq:reparam},
\begin{equation}
  m_{X}^{\mathrm{pred}}(t)
    = \curve_{X}\bigl(\arc(t)\bigr) + \dm
      + \colr\,\dustdir_{X}\bigl(\arc(t)\bigr) ,
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
with $\curve$ obtained by numerical integration of \eqref{eq:frenet}. The final term
appears only in rung L2c; below it $\colr = 0$ and reddening is left to the shape
latents, which is the failure that rung tests for. Being phase-dependent, it cannot
be folded into $\dm$ after the fact. Equation~\eqref{eq:forward} is converted to flux
on the instrumental system,
\begin{equation}
  f_{X}^{\mathrm{pred}}(t) = 10^{-0.4\left(m_{X}^{\mathrm{pred}}(t) - \zp_{X}(t)\right)},
\end{equation}
and compared to the observed forced-photometry flux,
\begin{equation}
  -2\ln\mathcal{L} = \sum_{X}\sum_{k}
    \frac{\left[f_{X,k}^{\mathrm{obs}} - f_{X}^{\mathrm{pred}}(t_{k})\right]^{2}}
         {\sigma_{X,k}^{2}} ,
  \label{eq:chi2}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{X,k}$ is the reported flux error of epoch $k$; $\sigma$ denotes
measurement error throughout and never the timing scale, which is $\tscale$.

Evaluating in flux is essential: \ztf\ difference-imaging photometry has
approximately Gaussian flux errors and retains negative fluxes at faint phases, both
destroyed by conversion to magnitudes. \textbf{No data are ever converted to
magnitudes; only the model is expressed there.} The sum runs over observer-frame
epochs as recorded in MJD, the rest-frame transformation happening once inside
\eqref{eq:reparam}.

The curve is defined on a fixed rest-frame window, nominally
$\phase \in [-15,+40]\,\mathrm{d}$, held in configuration so sensitivity to it can be
tested. Since $\tmax$ is free the window is a function of the fitted parameters: it
spans $57.75$~d of observer time at $\zred = 0.05$ against $55$~d at $\zred = 0$, and
slides as $\tmax$ moves. Peculiar velocities need no treatment here --- they shift
the normalization only, and $\dm$ absorbs them exactly.

\section{Samples}
\label{sec:samples}

Coverage was measured directly from the \ztf\ SN~Ia DR2 archive. Starting from
the 3628 catalogued objects, we require $\zred < 0.05$ and both the
\texttt{lccoverage} and \texttt{fitquality} flags, leaving 669 supernovae, of
which 654 have a light-curve file present in the release. Epochs are counted
inside the rest-frame window $[-15,+40]$~d with signal-to-noise above five and
with none of the quality bits $\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ set.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Minimum epochs per band & $g$ & $r$ & $i$ & $g\,\&\,r$ & $g\,\&\,r\,\&\,i$ \\
\midrule
$\geq 3$  & 636 & 639 & 216 & 636 & 215 \\
$\geq 5$  & 606 & 631 & 179 & 599 & 177 \\
$\geq 10$ & 490 & 537 &  82 & 465 &  80 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two samples follow. The \textbf{primary sample} is the 599 supernovae with at
least five good $g$ and $r$ epochs; here $n=2$ and there is a single invariant.
The \textbf{torsion subsample} is the 177 that additionally have at least five
good $i$-band epochs; here $n=3$ and $\tors(\arc)$ exists as a genuine second
invariant. The latter is small, but it is the only configuration in which the
original motivation for a second shape parameter --- that torsion describes the
evolution of inter-band magnitude differences --- can actually be tested. It
should be treated as a distinct analysis, not as an afterthought.

\paragraph{A caveat on the window used for counting.} Counting epochs inside
$[-15,+40]$~d requires a date of maximum, but $\tmax$ is precisely what the model
fits (Section~\ref{sec:reparam}); the numbers above were obtained using the
archive's \salt\ $t_{0}$ to place the window. This is \salt\ entering upstream of
\emph{selection}, which is far weaker than \salt\ entering the fit --- no
photometry is transformed, initialised, or K-corrected by it, and the fitted
$\tmax$ remains free and independent. It is nonetheless an exception to the rule
that \salt\ stays strictly downstream, and is recorded here rather than left
implicit. The check that settles it is to recount using an SED-free anchor, for
instance a window placed on the brightest good $g$ epoch, and confirm the sample
sizes move negligibly. The 36 objects with NaN $t_{0}$ must in any case be
selected by such an estimator or excluded explicitly.

\paragraph{Milky Way extinction.} Galactic reddening is tabulated per object as
\texttt{mwebv}, so its \emph{amplitude} is known and is removed deterministically
before fitting rather than being fitted. Only host-galaxy reddening remains a free
quantity, and only in rung L2c. Note that what is known is $E(B-V)$, not
$A_{g}(\phase)$ and $A_{r}(\phase)$; by Section~\ref{sec:phasedust} the conversion
is phase-dependent and needs an SED, so removing a constant offset is an
approximation here as well. It is a milder one, since Galactic columns at the
high latitudes of this sample are low, but it is the same approximation and is
not exact.

\section{Relation to \salt}

The two models can be placed side by side once the geometric quantities are
mapped onto their photometric counterparts.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Concept & \salt & This work \\
\midrule
Underlying object & SED surface $F(p,\lambda)$ & curve $\curve(\arc) \subset \Rn$ \\
Basis             & B-splines in $(p,\lambda)$ & neural network in $\arc$ \\
Shape freedom     & $x_{1}$, linear coefficient & $\shapeone,\shapetwo$ conditioning $\curv$ \\
Timing freedom    & $t_{0}$ only                & $\tmax, \tscale, \warpone, \warptwo$ \\
Normalization     & $x_{0}$                     & $\dm$ \\
Colour            & $\colr$ with law $\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)$ & $\colr\,\dustdir(\arc)$, or absorbed into $\curv$ \\
K-corrections     & built in via the SED        & suppressed by $\zred < 0.05$, not removed \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The decisive structural difference is the treatment of timing. \salt\ has a
single time parameter and forces all remaining temporal variation into the
shape components; the geometric model gives timing its own map and can
therefore ask whether shape variation is real or merely mis-assigned timing.

On colour the comparison runs the other way, and the point is worth conceding
plainly. \salt's colour law $\mathrm{CL}(\lambda)$ is phase-independent, but \salt\
carries an SED surface from which \eqref{eq:bandext} is evaluated at each phase, so
the phase dependence emerges automatically; having no SED, this model must import
$\dustdir(\arc)$ from an external template. \salt\ is structurally better placed
here. What the two share is the mixing itself: \salt's $\colr$ conflates dust with
intrinsic colour, which is why $\beta$ is fitted rather than predicted.

Distance is not delivered directly by the geometry. Since $\dm$ is free per
supernova, the geometry supplies standardization \emph{variables}, and distance
follows from a Tripp-style relation \citep{Tripp1998},
\begin{equation}
  \dm_{\mathrm{corr}} = \dm - \alpha\,\shapeone - \beta\,\shapetwo + \mathcal{M},
  \label{eq:tripp}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\mathcal{M}$ fitted globally. This is the same
logical structure as \salt\ cosmology and makes the comparison direct.

\section{Orientation, boundary conditions, and a curvature sum rule}
\label{sec:rotation}

Integrating \eqref{eq:frenet} requires an initial frame. Two questions are easily
conflated and are answered separately below: whether rotation is a physical degree
of freedom, and whether the template frame is determined.

\subsection{No physical effect acts as a rotation}

Enumerating the per-supernova nuisances in magnitude space:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Effect & Action on $\curve$ \\
\midrule
Distance and absolute luminosity & translation $\parallel (1,1)$; exact \\
Host dust and intrinsic colour   & displacement along the reddening direction,
                                   phase-dependent \\
Per-band zeropoint error         & translation $(\delta_{g}, \delta_{r})$; exact \\
Time dilation and stretch        & reparameterisation; does not move $\curve$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every entry is a translation, a phase-dependent displacement, or a
reparameterisation; none is a rotation. A per-supernova rotation would therefore be
a degree of freedom with no physical referent and appears in no rung of the ladder.
A supernova-dependent $R_{V}$ is not a counterexample: it changes the
\emph{direction} of the reddening displacement, selecting a different
$\dustdir(\arc)$ in \eqref{eq:dustcurve}, but does not rotate the curve.

\subsection{The frame is fixed by the $g$-maximum}

That rotation is unphysical does not determine the template frame. It is fixed
instead by the same anchor that fixes the arclength origin. At a $g$-band maximum,
\begin{equation}
  \deriv{\magg}{\arc}\bigg|_{\arc=0} = 0
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \tang(0) = (0, \pm 1).
  \label{eq:frameanchor}
\end{equation}
Imposing this \emph{is} the statement that $\arc = 0$ lies at the $g$-maximum, so
one condition fixes both gauges. It costs no generality and, unlike the asymptotic
constraint of Section~\ref{sec:sumrule}, it is observable inside the phase window.

\subsubsection*{The residual sign is empirical, not a gauge choice}
\label{sec:framesign}

Arclength increases with time and a brightening band moves toward \emph{smaller}
magnitude, so $\dd m_{X}/\dd \arc < 0$ while band $X$ rises. The two branches of
\eqref{eq:frameanchor} are therefore distinct physical statements about the epoch of
$g$-maximum:
\begin{equation}
  \tang(0) = (0,-1) \iff \dd\magr/\dd\arc < 0 \iff \text{$r$ still brightening},
  \qquad
  \tang(0) = (0,+1) \iff \text{$r$ already fading}.
  \label{eq:framesign}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the sign records \emph{which band reaches maximum first} --- a
measurable property of the photometry, not a convention. It cannot be inferred from
effective wavelength, since peak epoch is not a monotonic function of wavelength
across UV to NIR. Nor do the asymptotics of Section~\ref{sec:sumrule} supply an
independent handle, because the \emph{sense} of turning is itself equivalent to the
ordering: writing $\tang = (\cos\phi, \sin\phi)$, the tangent runs from
$-(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ to $+(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ and with monotone turning may do so either
way,
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Turning & $\phi$ traverses & Implied ordering \\
\midrule
$\curv > 0$ & $225^{\circ} \to 405^{\circ}$, crossing $270^{\circ}$ then $360^{\circ}$ & $g$-maximum, then $r$-maximum \\
$\curv < 0$ & $225^{\circ} \to 45^{\circ}$, crossing $180^{\circ}$ then $90^{\circ}$   & $r$-maximum, then $g$-maximum \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The sum rule fixes the net turning but not its sense, so it restates the question
rather than answering it. The table does supply, given monotone turning, that each
band has exactly one maximum in the window; a second --- as in the secondary maximum
of redder bands --- forces $\curv$ to change sign.

\paragraph{Determination and its risks.} The measurement needs no model and no
\salt: fit a low-order polynomial to each band near its own peak and record the
order of the two maxima. The convention is the sample-majority sign, carried in
configuration rather than hard-coded, and reported with the minority fraction. Since
the frame is global, a sample heterogeneous in the ordering could not be represented
by one template at all, which would be evidence against the no-rotation design
rather than a detail to tune; the minority fraction and the distribution of
maximum-to-maximum separation bound that risk.

\paragraph{Regularity: the maxima must not coincide.} If all bands are stationary at
the same epoch then $\norm{\dd\curve/\dd\phase} = 0$, hence $\dd\arc/\dd t = 0$, the
positivity requirement of \eqref{eq:reparam} is violated, and the unit-speed
parameterisation fails --- the curve has a stationary rather than a regular point.
Non-coincident band maxima is thus a regularity condition on the data, distinct from
the vanishing-$\curv$ degeneracy of Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, with the same
separation distribution as its diagnostic.

\paragraph{One condition suffices only for $n=2$.} The frame is an element of
$\SO(n)$, of dimension $n(n-1)/2$, while $\dd\magg/\dd\arc = 0$ supplies one
condition. For $n=2$ that is exactly enough, up to the sign above. For the $n=3$
subsample it leaves $\tang(0) = (0, T_{r}, T_{i})$ with $T_{r}^{2}+T_{i}^{2} = 1$
--- a continuous family, not a discrete sign --- and $\norml(0)$ free in the plane
orthogonal to $\tang(0)$: two further conditions are needed. They must come from
observables inside the phase window, on the principle of \eqref{eq:frameanchor}, and
are left open here rather than assumed away.

\subsection{Asymptotic structure and a sum rule on $\curv$}
\label{sec:sumrule}

Supernova flux vanishes before explosion and tends to zero long after, so in
every band $f \to 0$ and hence $m \to +\infty$ at both extremes. In magnitude
space \emph{both ends of the curve escape to infinity along the diagonal}
$(1,1)$. The curve is a hairpin, not an arc, and its two asymptotes are
parallel rays whose separation is the terminal colour.

This pins the tangent at both ends. While brightening the supernova moves
toward smaller magnitudes, and while fading it moves toward larger ones:
\begin{equation}
  \tang \longrightarrow -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1) \quad (\text{early}),
  \qquad
  \tang \longrightarrow +\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1) \quad (\text{late}).
\end{equation}
The tangent therefore turns through $\pi$ over the life of the supernova, and
since $\curv$ is precisely the rate of turning of the tangent, the total
curvature obeys the exact sum rule
\begin{equation}
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \curv(\arc)\,\dd \arc \;=\; \pi \pmod{2\pi}.
  \label{eq:sumrule}
\end{equation}
The modulus allows for complete loops; local reversals of turning, such as
those the secondary maximum in redder bands may produce
\citep{Deckers2025}, redistribute curvature without altering the net total. Two
corollaries follow: $\curv \to 0$ at both extremes as the curve straightens
onto its asymptote, and the asymptotic offset between the rays is the terminal
colour.

Equation~\eqref{eq:sumrule} is a global constraint on the shape function that
has no counterpart in a linear component decomposition. \salt\ has no way to
express it, because ``the total turning of the light curve'' is not a
statement about the amplitude of an additive component.

\paragraph{Use, and a cost.} The asymptotes lie far outside the fitted window
$[-15,+40]$~d, so \eqref{eq:sumrule} constrains the \emph{extrapolation} of a
trained $\curv$, not the fit. It is used as a validation diagnostic --- does a
trained $\curv$ integrate to something near $\pi$ beyond the window --- and at most
as a weak prior; it must never be imposed as a hard constraint on a fit that cannot
see the asymptotes. The divergence of $m$ at both ends is also why the phase window
exists at all: flux space would keep the curve bounded, beginning and ending at the
origin, but would forfeit \eqref{eq:transinv}.

\section{Degeneracies and gauge fixing}
\label{sec:gauge}

The model as stated has exact degeneracies that must be removed before the
latents can be interpreted or compared across rungs of the ladder.

Two kinds of condition appear below and should not be confused. A \emph{gauge}
condition removes a mathematical redundancy: the model makes identical predictions
before and after, so the condition costs nothing, cannot be wrong, and is not
testable. An \emph{identifying assumption} is a statement about the physical world
that pins down a split the data alone leave open; it is substantive, it is
falsifiable, and it may be false. The arclength origin, the metric, the latent
normalisation and the frame orientation are gauge. The two headed ``against
shape'' are not: they are the substantive content of this section, and each can
fail.

\paragraph{Arclength origin.} The substitution $\arc \mapsto \arc + \arc_{0}$
is absorbed by $\tmax$. Gauge is fixed by anchoring $\arc = 0$ to a
geometrically defined point on the template curve, taken here as the $g$-band
maximum, $\dd\magg/\dd \arc = 0$. For $n=2$, \eqref{eq:frameanchor} shows this
simultaneously fixes the frame orientation, so the two gauges are one condition;
for $n=3$ it does not suffice, as noted below.

\paragraph{Metric on magnitude space.} Curvature presupposes a metric, and no
metric on magnitude space is physically preferred
(Section~\ref{sec:magspace}). The Euclidean choice $\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$ is adopted
as a convention. It does not affect the invariance arguments, since translations
are isometries of any translation-invariant metric, but it does set the numerical
scale of $\curv$ and hence what the model means by shape. It must be declared
alongside the other conventions here rather than left implicit.

\paragraph{Latent scale, sign and rotation.} Nothing in \eqref{eq:kappanet}
fixes the scale, sign, or mutual orientation of $(\shapeone, \shapetwo)$. Gauge
is fixed by requiring the population of fitted latents to have zero mean and
unit diagonal covariance, and by fixing the sign so that $\shapeone$ correlates
positively with light-curve width.

\paragraph{Timing warp against shape.} This is the substantive one. Although a
reparameterisation cannot change the curve in principle, over a finite phase
window with noisy sampling a warp in $\arc(t)$ and a change in $\curv$ can
mimic one another. The mitigation is to regularise $\warpone, \warptwo$ toward
zero and to report the fitted correlation between them and $\shape$ as a
diagnostic. If that correlation is large, the shape--timing separation that
motivates the model is not being realised in practice, and this must be
reported as a negative result rather than absorbed.

\paragraph{Dust against shape.} The second substantive one, measured by
$\rho^{2}$ of \eqref{eq:overlap}: $\colr$ is degenerate against $\shape$ to the
extent that $\delta\dustdir$ lies in $\mathrm{span}\{\partial\curve/\partial\shape\}$
at the observed epochs. This is not a redundancy that can be gauged away but a
genuine ambiguity of physical cause. The identifying assumption is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Corr}(\colr, \shape) = 0
  \quad\text{over the population,}
  \label{eq:idcond}
\end{equation}
justified by the physical independence of line-of-sight dust from explosion physics,
and known to be only approximate --- the host mass step couples \snia\ properties to
host environment, and dust column is environmental. By \eqref{eq:orders} it is
expected to function as a \emph{check} rather than to carry weight, so it is imposed
\emph{and} its unimposed value reported. A large $\rho^{2}$ or a large unconstrained
correlation is a negative result for the interpretation of $\shape$, not for the
distance indicator (Section~\ref{sec:phasedust}); the two are reported separately.

\paragraph{Frame orientation.} Fixed for $n=2$ by the $g$-maximum condition
\eqref{eq:frameanchor}, which is the same anchor that fixes the arclength origin.
The residual sign in \eqref{eq:framesign} is not gauge: it encodes which band
peaks first and must be measured from the photometry, as
Section~\ref{sec:rotation} sets out. For $n=3$ two further conditions are needed
and are not yet fixed. No per-supernova rotation is fitted, because
Section~\ref{sec:rotation} shows that no physical effect acts as one.

\section{Model ladder and validation}
\label{sec:ladder}

With seven per-supernova parameters and a conditioned network, good fits are
guaranteed and therefore carry no evidential weight. The scientific deliverable
is not a fit but a comparison, conducted on held-out data.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Rung & Free per supernova & Question \\
\midrule
L0  & $\dm, \tscale, \tmax$        & is a rigid template sufficient? \\
L1  & $+\ \shapeone$                & does one shape parameter earn its place? \\
L2  & $+\ \shapetwo$                & does a second? \\
L2c & L2 $+\ \colr$ amplitude of $\dustdir(\arc)$ & does dust need its own direction? \\
L3  & $+\ \warpone, \warptwo$       & does nonlinear timing earn its place? \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Rungs are compared by scatter in held-out Hubble residuals under
cross-validation, never by in-sample scatter. \salt\ is fitted independently on
the same supernovae and used as an external benchmark. It is never used
upstream of the data.

Rung L2c deserves emphasis. Below it the only ambient motion is $\dm$, so the
template must predict each supernova's absolute colour from its shape alone and
host-galaxy dust, having nothing else to occupy, is forced into $\shapeone,
\shapetwo$. Whether shape can absorb reddening or requires its own direction is a
result either way. The identifying condition \eqref{eq:idcond} is part of the rung's
definition, not an afterthought: without it the fitted split between $\colr$ and
$\shape$ is arbitrary even where the fit improves. It constrains only the
interpretation of that split, so L2c's held-out Hubble score is meaningful either
way.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Two bands admit no torsion.} In the primary sample $n=2$ and there
is a single curvature invariant, so $\shapetwo$ is a second direction of
variation in $\curv$ rather than the torsion originally envisaged, and should
not be described as torsion. Torsion is available only on the 177-object
subsample of Section~\ref{sec:samples}, where the reduced sample size is traded
against the presence of a second genuine invariant.

\paragraph{No SED, hence no K-corrections.} A two-band model cannot
self-consistently K-correct, which requires the SED \citep{Nugent2002}; hence the
restriction to $\zred < 0.05$, where K-corrections are small. This buys independence
from \salt\ at the cost of sample size and of any redshift lever for cosmology.

The missing SED has a second branch the redshift cut does \emph{not} touch.
Band-integrated extinction depends on the SED inside the filter, hence on phase
(Section~\ref{sec:phasedust}), and that is a rest-frame effect, identical at
$\zred = 0$ and $\zred = 0.05$. It also forces $\dustdir(\arc)$ to be imported from an
external template rather than generated internally as \salt\ can. Rung L2c and the
diagnostics of Section~\ref{sec:phasedust} are a mitigation, not a solution.

\paragraph{Dust is not separable from shape by the geometry.} No geometric argument
divides them, so the shape latents carry some reddening (Sections~\ref{sec:phasedust}
and~\ref{sec:gauge}). The consequences are asymmetric and must be quoted as such: for
the distance indicator the mixture is absorbed by the fitted Tripp coefficients and
costs nothing, whereas for reading $\shape$ as explosion physics it is a limitation
whose size --- second order by \eqref{eq:orders}, but measured, and stratified by
$\colr$ --- has to be reported. The \emph{colour} degeneracy is the more serious one
and is untouched by the order counting: $\colr$ conflates dust with intrinsic colour
at first order, as in \salt.

\paragraph{Curvature degenerates at inflection points.} Where $\curv \to 0$ the
Frenet frame is ill-defined in the classical construction. Light curves in the
$(\magg, \magr)$ plane may pass near such points, particularly around the
secondary maximum seen in redder bands \citep{Deckers2025}. The implementation
must use a frame construction that remains well-defined through inflections,
and must report where along $\arc$ the curvature approaches zero.

\paragraph{Interpretability is traded for expressiveness.} Full conditioning
means the latents are not linear coefficients and ablation is less clean than
in \salt. The gauge fixing above is a partial remedy, not a complete one.
